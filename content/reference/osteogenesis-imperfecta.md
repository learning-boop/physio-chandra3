# Osteogenesis imperfecta (OI)

Almost everyone with OI who reaches the site already knows they have it, so this is a diagnosed-patient overlay, not a diagnostic screen.

## On the site

- **The tick (open item 1, adopted):** "Do you have osteogenesis imperfecta (brittle bone disease)?" on "A little about you", as a single OPTIONAL tick ("Yes, I have osteogenesis imperfecta"). It is optional, unlike the diabetes and steroid questions, because OI is about 1 in 10,000 to 15,000: every visitor should not have to answer it. Taking the tick off clears the details.
- **Red flags first, in every area** (document §6), after the obstetric ones and before diabetes and steroids, minus any the page already asks:
  - Emergency department: after a fall or knock, a limb that is bent, cannot take weight or be used, or is numb, pale or cold (911 if cold, pale, numb, or no safe way there).
  - Emergency department today: a knock to the head or neck in the last few days, even small, not yet checked.
  - Emergency department today: a headache at the back of the head worse with coughing, sneezing or straining, with dizziness, trouble swallowing or speaking, or arm or leg tingling or weakness (basilar invagination).
  - Emergency department: spreading numbness or weakness, or a bladder or bowel change (left out where the area asks its own cauda equina or cord question).
  - **Fracture first (Q2, open item 2):** new pain in the last couple of weeks, even mild, after a small knock, lift, twist, sneeze or fall, or for no remembered reason: one tender or swollen spot, or sudden severe back pain (height loss, pain on sitting up). Doctor, walk-in, urgent care or emergency department "today or tomorrow" for an X-ray; avoid loading the area; booking held until after the X-ray (the site's same-day doctor-first route). The document's sudden-back-pain flag is merged into this one question.
  - Doctor today: chest pain, breathlessness with everyday activity or lying flat, palpitations or swollen ankles, new or worse; the explanation says call 911 now for chest pain.
- **Details on "Before your results"** (optional, shown only with the tick): OI type (Q1), the main problem (Q3: months of pain in several areas / weaker, less steady or less fit / loose joints / curved or rounded spine / after a fracture or surgery), fractures and falls in the last year (Q4), and specialist care (Q6: under a specialist with a scan in 2 to 3 years / not for years / stopped bone medicine without a review / not sure).
- **Results panel "Osteogenesis imperfecta and physiotherapy"** (also in the PDF): the Q3 route paragraph leads (the after-fracture route says "once your fracture team or surgeon has cleared you"), then the OI explanation. Notes always: fracture-first, OI-safe exercise (low impact, controlled strength; no jumping, contact, high fall risk, heavy loaded flexion or twisting, forceful stretching of loose joints), the handling statement (open item 3: "gentle, controlled hands-on techniques only: no forceful manipulation and no hard stretching"), home falls safety, denosumab (open item 5: in the panel, not a safety question; a stronger line when "stopped" is answered), the specialist line (referral back when not under a specialist, stopped, or not sure; open item 7, "an adult metabolic bone clinic" named generically), and a hearing test. Two or more fractures or falls adds the falls block and "ask your doctor to review your bone medicine".
- **Chandra's summary:** an OSTEOGENESIS IMPERFECTA block with the handling precautions and every detail answer. Not in the anonymous copy or the AI overview; the privacy page says so.

## Decisions on the open items (the document's recommendations; awaiting sign-off)

1. Overlay with no diagnostic screen: adopted, as an optional tick.
2. Fracture first: the site's "today" doctor-first screen, with "today or tomorrow" in the explanation; walk-in, urgent care or emergency department all named.
3. Handling statement: in the results panel once, and in Chandra's summary, rather than on every condition card.
4. Exercise dosage language: "progressive, low-impact, controlled range"; no numbers.
5. Denosumab: a panel note, not a safety question.
6. Recognition rule for undiagnosed mild adults: **not built**. Its triggers (two or more fragility fractures before 50 with blue-grey sclerae, early hearing loss, hypermobility or a family history) are not asked anywhere: the osteoporosis question (upper back T9) is asked only at 50 and over. Building it would need a new question for every visitor. To revisit if Chandra wants it.
7. Referral wording: "an adult metabolic bone clinic", no named service.
8. Hypermobility spectrum disorder: no record of its own yet; the loose-joints route is a paragraph in the OI panel.

## Not built

- The document's "no regional cause cards" after the fracture flag: the site keeps the results page as for any same-day doctor-first flag, with the doctor advice repeated at the top.
- Route cards as ranked results (the Q3 route leads the OI panel instead).
- The hot joint with fever (the general "fever, chills…" question asks it), and rapidly worsening hearing as a safety question (panel note).
- A Sillence-type-specific rule (basilar invagination is commonest in types III and IV; its question is asked for every type).
- Language audit of the rest of the site ("fragile" elsewhere is not about OI).

## Clinic notes (from the document)

- History: type and gene if known; lifetime and recent fractures and sites; rods, spinal fusion, joint replacements; bisphosphonate, denosumab (stopping → rebound vertebral fractures), teriparatide; DXA (short stature and deformity limit it: Z-scores, TBS); vitamin D and calcium; menopause; hearing; dentinogenesis imperfecta; valve disease, reduced FVC with scoliosis; basilar invagination symptoms (occipital headache with Valsalva, lower cranial nerve signs, myelopathy → imaging); bruising; fatigue; fear of fracture and mood.
- Examination: spinal alignment, height against recalled height, limb deformity, Beighton, joint stability and OA (hips, knees, ankles, first MTP), pes planus, strength (MMT or dynamometry; weakness is intrinsic as well as disuse), 30-second chair stand, TUG, Berg or mini-BESTest, 6MWT or 2-minute step with a dyspnoea scale, gait aids. Pain is often mixed nociceptive and nociplastic: BPI, PSEQ, sleep.
- Imaging: new focal pain = X-ray first; low threshold to repeat if pain persists (occult fractures).
- Handling: no HVLA, no forceful mobilisation, no end-range passive stretch, no heavy resisted testing over deformed segments; care with transfers and positioning; taping and compression fine on intact skin.
- Exercise (Mueller 2018, extrapolated to adults; adult trials scarce): regular, progressive, low impact; resistance with controlled range and gradual load; pool, cycling, walking, balance and falls training; avoid contact, jumping, high fall risk, heavy loaded flexion or rotation; whole-body vibration evidence limited. After a fracture: early mobilisation within the fracture team's plan.
- Refer: OI or adult metabolic bone clinic (BC Children's transition for young adults), audiology, cardiology or respiratory if symptomatic, pain clinic, OT, genetics for family planning. Never raise non-accidental injury in the guide (clinician consideration only).

## Look-alikes (document §5; none built as cards)

Post-menopausal or age-related osteoporosis (upper back osteoporosis record); hypermobility spectrum disorder or hypermobile EDS; hypophosphatasia, osteopetrosis, idiopathic juvenile osteoporosis, fibrous dysplasia; secondary osteoporosis (steroids, Cushing's, hyperparathyroidism, coeliac, anorexia, hypogonadism: the hormonal records); bone stress injury; inflammatory arthritis; fibromyalgia; cervical spondylosis or migraine against basilar invagination.
