# Upper cervical spine (C0-C3): clinical reasoning supplement

Pain from C1-C3 converges with the trigeminal nerve in the trigeminocervical nucleus, so a neck source can be felt in the forehead, temple or eye. Head location alone cannot separate cervicogenic headache (CGH) from migraine; side-locking, mechanical provocation and the physical triad do. Upper cervical pain without a main headache is somatic: it refers to the back of the head and behind the ear, has no dermatomal arm signs, and changes with neck movement. The engine separates it from CGH (headache dominant), radiculopathy (arm signs) and the red-flag conditions at this level (instability, arterial dysfunction).

## A1. Upper cervical segments: where each one refers pain

| Segment / structure | Nerve supply | Where its pain is felt (somatic referral) | Reasoning note |
|---|---|---|---|
| C0-C1 (atlanto-occipital joint) | C1 ventral ramus | Base of the skull and back of the head | Main nodding joint. Pain and stiffness looking up or tucking the chin. |
| C1-C2 (lateral atlanto-axial joint) | C2 ventral ramus | Suboccipital and back-side of the head, can reach behind the ear | About half of neck rotation. Tested by the flexion-rotation test. |
| C2-C3 facet joint | Third occipital nerve (C3 dorsal ramus) | Upper neck and back of the head; can spread forward to forehead, temple and around the eye | Commonest source of headache after whiplash in block studies. Forward spread is via the trigeminocervical nucleus, not an eye or sinus problem. |
| C3-C4 facet joint | C3 / C4 dorsal rami | Mid-upper neck, side of neck, upper trapezius edge | Overlaps mechanical neck pain; headache is less typical. |
| Upper cervical muscles (suboccipitals, semispinalis, splenius, upper trapezius, SCM) | C1-C3 dorsal rami; accessory nerve (trapezius, SCM) | Back of head, temple, around the eye (SCM), top of head | Muscle tenderness is common in all headache types; on its own it does not confirm a neck source. |
| Upper cervical dura and ligaments | C1-C3 sinuvertebral nerves | Deep head pain, back of head | Ligament laxity (transverse / alar) is a red-flag topic, not a self-guide condition. |

## A2. Upper cervical nerves and dermatomes

| Nerve (roots) | Skin area | Irritation / entrapment clue | Engine note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Suboccipital nerve (C1 dorsal ramus) | None (motor); C1 usually has no dermatome | Deep suboccipital ache only | No skin sign expected |
| Greater occipital nerve (C2 dorsal ramus) | Back of the scalp from the base of the skull up to the crown | Shooting, electric, stabbing scalp pain; tender about a third of the way from the midline bump to the mastoid; scalp may feel sore to brush | Paroxysmal shooting pain → occipital neuralgia, not CGH (the head's D2, "occipital" card) |
| Lesser occipital nerve (C2-C3) | Side of the back of the head and behind the ear | Along the back edge of SCM; similar shooting pain behind the ear | Occipital neuralgia variant |
| Third occipital nerve (C3 dorsal ramus) | Upper neck near the midline and lower occiput | Innervates the C2-C3 joint | Upper neck + occipital headache after whiplash |
| Great auricular nerve (C2-C3) | Ear, skin over the angle of the jaw and parotid | Pain at the jaw angle or ear | Check jaw (TMD), dental, ear causes |
| Transverse cervical nerve (C2-C3) | Front and side of the neck | Rarely symptomatic | Anterior neck pain → consider throat, thyroid, arterial causes |
| Trigeminal nerve, ophthalmic branch (V1) | Forehead, around the eye, temple | Receives converging input from C1-C3 | Frontal or eye pain can come from the neck; do not use as a migraine-only sign |

| Root | Dermatome | Key test point | Engine note |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | Usually none | — | No sensory sign expected |
| C2 | Back of the head up to the crown; behind the ear | At least 1 cm lateral to the occipital protuberance | Back of the head (head@back) |
| C3 | Upper and side of neck, under the jaw line | Supraclavicular fossa | Neck |
| C4 | Lower neck and top of the shoulder | Over the acromioclavicular joint | Below this → content/reference/cervical-radiculopathy.md |

## A3. Telling the headache types apart

| Feature | Cervicogenic headache | Migraine | Tension-type headache |
|---|---|---|---|
| Side | One side, the same side every time | Often one side but can switch; can be both | Both sides |
| Where it starts | Neck / base of skull, spreads forward | Temple, around the eye; neck ache may come first as a warning sign | Band around the head, forehead or back of head |
| Quality and intensity | Steady, non-throbbing ache; moderate | Throbbing; moderate to severe | Pressing or tightening; mild to moderate |
| Triggers | Neck movement, sustained postures, pressure at the base of the skull | Hormonal changes, sleep change, missed meals, stress, light, smells | Stress, fatigue, poor sleep |
| Other symptoms | Neck stiffness; vague ache in the same shoulder | Nausea or vomiting, light and sound sensitivity, aura in some | Few; not both light and sound sensitivity |
| Worse with routine activity | Not usually | Yes | No |
| Physical triad (A4) | Present | Usually absent | Usually absent |

## A4. Clinical tests and their value

| Test / measure | How it is done / positive finding | Diagnostic value | Practical note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cervical flexion-rotation test | Neck in full flexion, head rotated each way. Positive: 32° or less, or about 10° less than the other side | C1-2 CGH: sensitivity about 91%, specificity about 90% (Ogince 2007) | Also a re-test after treatment |
| Jull physical triad | Reduced active extension; painful C0-C3 joint dysfunction on manual examination; impaired craniocervical flexion test | CGH vs migraine and tension-type: sensitivity 100%, specificity 94% (Jull 2007) | Absence makes a neck source unlikely |
| Craniocervical flexion test | Chin nod against a pressure sensor (20 → 30 mmHg in 2 mmHg stages). Impaired: cannot reach or hold target, substitutes with superficial flexors | Standard measure of deep neck flexor control (Jull 2008) | Also the basis of the exercise programme |
| Manual examination C0-C3 | Sustained pressure reproduces the familiar headache, and it eases with sustained pressure | Clinician-dependent; part of the triad | Record the segment and side |
| Upper cervical ligament tests (Sharp-Purser, alar, transverse ligament) | Only with risk factors: trauma, rheumatoid arthritis, Down syndrome, long-term steroids, recent throat infection, connective tissue disorder | Accuracy limited and variable (Hutting 2013); a negative test does not clear the patient | Positive or strong suspicion → stop, medical referral or imaging |
| Cervical arterial dysfunction screen | History-led per the IFOMPT framework: new or unusual headache and neck pain, neuro symptoms, blood pressure, cranial nerves if indicated | A framework, not a single test (Rushton 2023) | Suspicion → emergency pathway |
| Outcome measures | Headache diary (days a month, intensity, duration), HIT-6, NDI, PSFS | — | Baseline before treatment |

## A5. Dizziness with neck pain: sorting the causes (upper neck document)

| Pattern | Likely source | Route |
|---|---|---|
| Unsteady or light-headed when the neck is stiff or painful, eases as the neck improves; no spinning | Cervicogenic dizziness | neck-cgd (N9, N11); assessment |
| Brief spinning (under a minute) rolling in bed, lying down or looking up | Inner ear (BPPV) | BPPV card; vestibular assessment |
| Dizziness with double vision, speech or swallowing change, facial numbness, drop attacks, or a new unusual headache | Arterial (vertebrobasilar) or central | Safety pages → 911 |
| Constant dizziness unrelated to neck position, with hearing change | Vestibular / ENT | Inner-ear card; GP |

## A6. Weighting rules for the pattern engine (both documents)

| Pattern the patient reports | Reasoning | Weight / route | Where it is built |
|---|---|---|---|
| One-sided, side-locked headache starting in the neck, brought on by posture or neck movement | Classic CGH | Raise CGH | N4, D1, D3 |
| Headache switches sides, throbbing, nausea, light and sound sensitivity | Migraine features; neck pain is common in migraine too | Lower CGH; route to GP / assessment | D1, D2 negatives; migraine card |
| Bilateral pressing band, no neck provocation | Tension-type | Lower CGH | D1 band -1, D3 negatives; head-tth |
| Neck pain AND migraine features | Mixed headache is common | Possible match; suggest assessment | Both can show |
| Brief electric or shooting scalp pain, scalp sore to touch | Occipital nerve (A2) | Occipital neuralgia → assessment | D2 "shooting", card |
| Base-of-skull pain and stiffness, little or no headache | Upper cervical pain without CGH | neck-upper | N13, N4 "occasional" |
| Same picture but headache is the main complaint | CGH fits better | Route CGH | N4 CGH answers -2 on neck-upper |
| Pain lower in the neck, no suboccipital tenderness | Mid / lower segments (C4-C7) | neck-mech | N13 "middle or lower" 1 only |
| Arm tingling or pain past the elbow | Nerve root | Add radiculopathy | N2, neck-radic |
| Constant pain unchanged by movement or position | Non-mechanical | Lower score; GP / assessment | Not scored; general safety check |
| Older adult, both shoulders and neck stiff in the morning, unwell | Inflammatory / systemic (PMR) | GP | Final check pc-over50stiff |
| Painkillers on 10+ days a month | Medication-overuse headache possible | Flag; GP review | N4 "meds", D6; card |
| Any SNNOOP10 flag, instability, arterial or trauma feature | Secondary headache or not a self-guide condition | Stop scoring → red-flag screen | Safety pages; injury screen; pc-headache, pc-upperinstab |

## B. Scope boundaries

- **Cervicogenic headache, in scope:** recurrent, side-locked headache from the upper cervical spine (C0-C3) with neck movement or posture provocation, non-traumatic or after a resolved minor neck strain.
- **Upper cervical pain, in scope:** non-traumatic mechanical pain and stiffness of C0-C3 and the suboccipital muscles, with or without a minor occipital ache or mild cervicogenic dizziness.
- **Routed elsewhere:** headache-dominant → CGH; upper neck pain without a main headache → upper cervical pain; arm symptoms → radiculopathy; mid or lower neck → neck-mech; head injury → concussion / injury path; trauma → Canadian C-spine injury screen; jaw pain → jaw region; migraine and tension-type → assessment or their own records; instability, arterial, systemic and SNNOOP10 flags → hardcoded red-flag screen.
