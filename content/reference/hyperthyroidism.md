# Hyperthyroidism (an overactive thyroid)

Common (about 1-2% of women, far fewer men); about two-thirds have measurable muscle weakness. The guide never diagnoses it.

## On the site

- **Route A, undiagnosed:** `pc-thyroid`, the last pattern question (after the calcium one), asked for both shoulders, upper arms, hips or thighs drawn, or a weakness answer. It follows the document's rule: both-sided weakness built up over weeks or months together with weight loss while eating the same or more; or two or more of the racing heart, heat, tremor, anxiety or poor sleep, bowel and period changes, together with a neck swelling or eye changes. A yes: family doctor in the next week or two for a thyroid blood test, keep activity light and avoid intense exercise until then, no booking. Names the thyroid (open item 1).
- **Emergency, thyrotoxic periodic paralysis (open item 2):** `pc-paralysis`, 911: "Sudden weakness or being unable to move both legs (sometimes the arms too), without pain or numbness, especially on waking, after a large meal, alcohol or hard exercise". Asked for both hips, thighs, knees, lower legs, ankles or feet drawn, or a weakness answer with a leg, hip or low-back drawing. Ancestry-neutral, as the document recommends; the explanation names low potassium and the heart, and "do not drive". Placed near the top of the pattern list so no count limit can cut it.
- **Route B, diagnosed:** "An overactive thyroid (for example Graves' disease), diagnosed by a doctor (being treated, or treated in the past)" on the cautions list (`ca-thyroid`). The exercise-safety overlay (open item 4): light to moderate activity only, no vigorous or heavy exercise until the doctor confirms levels are controlled (often the first 4-8 weeks); stop signs; effort scales instead of heart rate on a beta-blocker. Results and PDF panel "An overactive thyroid and rebuilding strength" with seven lines: take tablets as prescribed; sit-to-stands and step-ups once controlled; bone density after the menopause or long-standing disease; the swing to underactive after radioactive iodine or surgery; sore throat, mouth ulcers or fever on anti-thyroid tablets (and jaundice or dark urine) → doctor the same day for a blood test (open item 7: kept); eye pain, double vision or reduced vision → same day; thyroid storm and arrhythmia → 911.
- **Frozen-shoulder cross-link (open item 5):** content/conditions/shoulder-frozen.md adds "It came on with no injury: please also ask your doctor whether your thyroid and blood sugar have been checked".

## Safety-page changes made while building

- **Emergency pattern questions are always asked on the first safety pages**, on top of the two non-emergency pattern questions there (an emergency could otherwise be deferred to the final check, after the questionnaire). The drawing's list now holds up to 12 matches instead of 7, so none is cut by count.
- **The gloves-and-socks question is no longer asked twice:** an area that asks its own "both feet numb, burning or tingling" question (lower leg, ankle, foot) replaces the drawing's general one, like the heart, clot and organ questions.
- (Before hypothyroidism.md raised the final check to six.) With this document, a both-thighs drawing at 50 or over asks: the 911 paralysis question and two others (myositis, the Cushing's question) on the doctor pages, then PMR, low hormones, the nerve and muscle screen, calcium and thyroid on the final check (five). A sixth would be cut; the calcium and thyroid questions sit last so they are the ones that drop first.

## Decisions on the open items (accepted with the sign-off, 3 Oct 2026)

1. Names the thyroid on the undiagnosed screen.
2. Periodic paralysis: in the leg, hip and low-back drawings (and a weakness answer), wording ancestry-neutral; the East and South-East Asian risk stays in these notes only.
3. Scores collapsed into one question with the document's doctor-first rules; the POSSIBLE band (5-7) is not built.
4. "No vigorous exercise until controlled" and the 4-8 week window are in the diagnosed entry and panel. New or unknown atrial fibrillation does not get its own booking block (no question about it); a racing or irregular heartbeat with chest pain, fainting or breathlessness is 911 in the panel, and the existing heart questions remain.
5. Frozen-shoulder cross-link: added (thyroid and blood sugar).
6. Kept separate from the coming hypothyroidism record.
7. The agranulocytosis warning is kept on the site, in the diagnosed panel.

## Not built

- Thyroid storm, arrhythmia and stroke as tick-box safety questions for the undiagnosed (the stroke and heart questions already exist; storm is in the diagnosed panel).
- A growing hard neck lump with hoarseness (rare, not specific).
- The "keep exercise moderate until checked" line for the POSSIBLE band.

## Clinic notes (from the document)

- Red flags first: resting HR over 100-110 or an irregular pulse (new AF needs medical review before exertion); fever and agitation (storm); acute flaccid paraparesis history (TPP: ED, potassium); orbitopathy with diplopia or visual change; agranulocytosis symptoms on thionamides.
- History: cause (Graves': TRAb; toxic multinodular goitre; thyroiditis, self-limiting; iodine, amiodarone, lithium; thyroxine over-replacement), stage (thionamide 4-8 weeks to euthyroid; RAI → hypothyroid within months; thyroidectomy → calcium and voice), beta-blocker (HR unreliable → RPE/Borg), last TSH and FT4.
- Exam: tremor, hyperreflexia, lid lag or proptosis, goitre, warm moist skin, pretibial myxoedema, acropachy; proximal strength (weakness more than wasting; CK normal or low; raised CK → inflammatory myopathy); fatigability if myasthenia suspected (coexists with Graves' in about 1%).
- Function: 30-second chair stand, 5 times sit-to-stand, TUG, grip; 6MWT only once euthyroid or with HR and BP monitoring and medical clearance.
- Bone: DXA via the GP after the menopause and with long-standing or subclinical hyperthyroidism; height, kyphosis; bone-safe loading after fracture.
- Shoulder: capsular pattern (frozen shoulder is linked with thyroid disease, as with diabetes); check the other shoulder.
- Exercise (ATA): avoid vigorous exercise until euthyroid; then moderate aerobic and progressive resistance; heat and hydration; stop for palpitations, dizziness, chest pain or breathlessness. After RAI or surgery, watch for hypothyroidism. Coexisting autoimmunity: type 1 diabetes, coeliac, myasthenia, vitiligo, Addison's (fatigue with low BP is urgent).
