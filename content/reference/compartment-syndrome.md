# Acute compartment syndrome: emergency route and rehabilitation

## On the site

- **Emergency (go to an emergency department now; the screen also says to call 911 if they cannot get there safely):** one shared question per limb, asked once across neighbouring areas. Arm (forearm, wrist, hand; group `compartment-arm`): frf-, wrf-, hnd-compartment. Leg (lower leg, ankle, foot; group `compartment-leg`): lgf-, af-, ft-compartment. Thigh: tgf-compartment. The question asks about a trigger in the last day or two (fracture, crush, tight cast or bandage, very hard exercise), pain far worse than expected and climbing, no longer helped by pain relief, a tight, hard, swollen muscle, or pain much worse when the fingers or toes are gently moved.
- **Same day:** a cast, splint or bandage getting tighter and more painful, "do not cut it off yourself" (groups `casttight-arm`, `casttight-leg`).
- **Also:** the injury screens' compartment questions (upper arm, forearm, wrist, thigh, lower leg, foot); rhabdomyolysis (dark urine with muscle pain or weakness, asked generally since 2 Oct 2026: shoulder, upper arm, hip, thigh and calf, group rhabdo); acute limb ischaemia (lower leg, ankle, foot).
- **Tier:** emergency department now, not 911, as agreed in the 911 split (compartment syndrome needs hospital within hours, and the person can usually be driven). The document's wording "call 911 or go to the nearest emergency department" matches that screen.
- **Not built:** the document's scored block (section 4). A condition result on this site always lands on the results page, where the document says no tips or booking may appear; the safety questions above end the visit before any result. Its "possible" band (4-6, "go today") is covered by the same emergency question.

## Key facts

- Pain out of proportion and pain on passive stretch are the earliest, most reliable signs; numbness, weakness, pallor and pulselessness are late. A palpable pulse does not exclude it.
- Irreversible muscle and nerve damage from about 6 hours; near-complete recovery when decompressed within 6 hours, about 68% within 12, 8% after 12 (Donaldson 2014).
- Most often after a tibial or forearm fracture, a crush, a tight cast or dressing, a burn, reperfusion, prolonged lying on a limb; young men with high-energy injuries; anticoagulants; children after elbow and forearm fractures (rising analgesic need, anxiety, agitation). Regional anaesthesia can mask it.
- Look-alikes: chronic exertional compartment syndrome (predictable onset with exercise, settles within an hour of stopping), DVT, cellulitis, expected post-fracture pain, muscle strain, acute limb ischaemia (can coexist), exertional rhabdomyolysis (cola urine).

## If they walk into the clinic

Stop the assessment. Do not elevate above heart level. Release any circumferential dressing you are able to. Arrange immediate transfer to the emergency department (911 if they cannot travel safely). Document times. Diagnosis is clinical; compartment pressure (delta P under 30 mmHg against diastolic) is an in-hospital adjunct, and fasciotomy follows immediately on diagnosis (BOAST).

## After fasciotomy (with the surgical team's clearance)

- Assess: wound and graft status and precautions; oedema; range of ankle and toes or wrist and fingers; strength grading; sensory map (common and deep peroneal, tibial; median, ulnar); gait and aids; established ischaemic contracture (Volkmann's in the forearm; equinus or claw toes in the leg) and foot drop; LEFS or QuickDASH, pain, range.
- Early: swelling control and positioning within the surgeon's instructions, gentle movement of permitted joints, general activity, and clear information so healing pain is not frightening.
- As wounds heal: scar massage and desensitising once closed, progressive range, splinting or positioning where muscles risk shortening.
- Strength and function: graded strengthening, balance and gait or hand-function retraining, staged return to work, driving and sport; work with what a nerve is recovering and compensate safely for what is not; keep the surgical team informed and refer back promptly if anything worsens.
