# Work participation after injury or illness

## On the site

- Work comes up only in the psychosocial statements (src/data/psychosocial.js): "The pain is causing problems at work" (yellow), the work statement after 6 weeks or with severe pain (blue), and expectation and confidence items (pink); a claim (ICBC, WorkSafeBC) through the `ca-claim` caution (black). Nothing asks about time off work: ask it at intake.

## Screen (throughout the episode)

- Risk of delayed return (A): injury type, previous episodes, a long absence before referral, other health conditions, high perceived disability, pain severity, pain behaviours, fear-avoidance, low recovery expectations, low self-efficacy. Do not use age, sex or education alone (D).
- Psychosocial tools (A), at the start and repeated: FABQ work subscale (low back: under 30 LR- 0.08, over 34 LR+ 3.33), Orebro (cut-off 147: sens 38%, spec 89%), Work and Health Questionnaire, Readiness for Return to Work scale. Waddell signs do not predict return.
- Work environment (B): job demands, workplace psychosocial factors, availability of modified or transitional work. Document essential job functions and exertion level (C).
- Self-report (B): Work Ability Index, DASH work module. Performance (B): functional capacity evaluation (full or short form), job-specific tests.
- Document the work-limiting areas (F): lifting and carrying, changing posture, walking, hand and arm use, self-care, transport, dealing with people.

## Timing and alliance

- Seen within 7 days of injury where possible (C). Off work 6 to 8 weeks: multidisciplinary assessment (B).
- Include the worker in planning; record goals, preferences and concerns; encourage work-focused behaviour (B).

## Interventions

- Low risk of delayed return: condition-specific exercise plus clinic-based work-focused care such as work-task practice (A).
- High risk: clinic-based plus workplace interventions (A) and a behavioural approach (B); communicate and coordinate with the employer, worker, case manager and other providers (B). Match the components to the risk (F).
- Psychologically informed care for psychosocial barriers (B): goal setting, motivational interviewing, pacing, problem solving, relaxation, coping.
- Graded, modified or transitional duties (B); ergonomic advice when demands exceed ability (B).
- Not at work at 6 weeks: intense graded exercise with work-oriented functional activity (C); light exercise alone is not enough (B).
- Education on findings, the benefits of activity and return-to-activity strategies (F); not written or group education alone (B).

## BC context (local, verify yearly)

- WorkSafeBC claims: reports on time; modified duties agreed with the employer and case manager, with the worker's consent. ICBC Enhanced Care for crash injuries (see the neck-whiplash clinic notes). Check current WorkSafeBC physiotherapy report forms and rules before relying on them.
