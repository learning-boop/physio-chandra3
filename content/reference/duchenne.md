# Duchenne muscular dystrophy: early signs in children and community physiotherapy

## On the site

- **Route A, early signs (Duchenne not named, the document's open item 4):** a final-check question, `pc-child-muscle` in src/data/patternChecks.js, asked when the age answer is "Under 18" and the legs, hips or low back are drawn (a parent answering for a child). It asks about Gowers' manoeuvre, falling behind peers at running, jumping and stairs, late walking (after 18 months) or a lost skill, toe-walking, waddling or a swayed back, and large firm calves. A yes means the family doctor in the next week or two (this week after a lost skill) and a CK blood test, with no booking until then (`noBooking`). It never reassures.
- **Route B, diagnosed:** "Duchenne or Becker muscular dystrophy, diagnosed by a neuromuscular team" (`ca-dmd`) on the "Anything else we should know?" list, with the stretching, activity, avoid-eccentric and emergency (rhabdomyolysis, fracture on steroids, anaesthesia) wording.
- **Already on the site:** breathing or swallowing trouble with weakness (pc-muscle-crisis, 911); the hip, knee and thigh children's flags (SUFE, Perthes, bone tumour, septic hip); the hip and thigh injury screens.
- **Not built:** the "this is for my child" tick (open item 2, a global feature of the body-select screen); the scored block (one question asks its strongest items and routes the same way); route B's booking wording, until the clinic decides whether it treats children and from what age (open item 1).

## Recognising it

- Boys, signs from 2-5: late walking, frequent falls, cannot run, jump or climb like peers, Gowers' manoeuvre, toe-walking, calf pseudohypertrophy, waddling gait, lumbar lordosis; speech or learning delay sometimes first. About a third have no family history. Carrier girls can have milder symptoms. Becker: milder and later.
- Undiagnosed: Gowers', time to rise from the floor, proximal weakness; GP letter requesting CK (typically 10-100 times normal), then genetics and the neuromuscular clinic (BC Children's). Do not treat as idiopathic toe-walking.
- Look-alikes: idiopathic toe-walking (can heel-walk and rise normally), Becker, SMA, cerebral palsy, hip disorders (DDH, Perthes, SUFE), juvenile arthritis, other myopathies, developmental delay.

## Diagnosed: physiotherapy (Birnkrant 2018; SMN 2024)

- Measures: NSAA; timed tests (10 m run or walk, rise from floor, 4-stair climb); 6MWT; PUL when non-ambulant; goniometry (ankle DF, knee and hip extension, ITB); Brooke and Vignos; spinal screen each visit; respiratory (FVC via the team); steroid effects; pain and fatigue.
- Daily stretching (ankles, knees, hips, later upper limbs), night AFOs, serial casting where appropriate; submaximal aerobic activity (swimming, cycling); avoid eccentric and high-resistance exercise and exhaustion; standing programme (1 h a day or more) once walking is lost; 24-hour postural management and seating; falls and fracture prevention; timely powered mobility.
- Emergencies: rhabdomyolysis (severe muscle pain, cola urine); a fall with leg pain or refusal to stand (fracture on steroids), confusion and breathlessness after a fracture (fat embolism); anaesthesia and sedation precautions.
