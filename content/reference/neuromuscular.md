# Nerve and muscle conditions: myasthenia gravis, myotonic dystrophy and myositis

## On the site

- **Route A, undiagnosed (not named, as both documents recommend one "nerve and muscle" gate):** two final-check questions in src/data/patternChecks.js.
  - `pc-muscle-crisis` (911), for anyone who chose a weakness answer (weak arm or grip, shoulder weakness, the thigh giving way, foot drop): difficulty breathing or breathless lying flat, a weak cough, trouble swallowing or clearing saliva, or fainting, near-fainting or a racing or irregular heartbeat.
  - `pc-myositis` (see the family doctor this week and ask about a CK test, no booking until then), before `pc-muscle`, for a weakness answer with the neck, shoulders, upper arms, hips, thighs or knees drawn, or the same areas drawn on both sides: weakness in both thighs or hips or both shoulders built up over weeks to months without injury, with or without a heliotrope or Gottron's rash, a new dry cough or breathlessness, or dysphagia. Not named ("inflammatory myopathy" on the diagnosed side only).
  - `pc-muscle` (see the family doctor in the next few days, no booking until then: `noBooking`), for a weakness answer or weakness-type drawings on both sides (shoulders, upper arms, hips, thighs): fatigable weakness worse by evening, ptosis or diplopia that comes and goes, chewing or speech fatigue, grip myotonia worse in the cold, slowly increasing weakness in both hands or feet, early cataracts or muscle weakness in the family. It is last in the list, so it only fills a free place in the three-question final check.
- **Route B, diagnosed:** "Myasthenia gravis, diagnosed by a neurologist" (`ca-mg`) and "Myotonic dystrophy or another muscle disease, diagnosed by a neurologist" (`ca-dm`) and "Myositis (an inflammatory muscle disease…), diagnosed by a specialist" (`ca-myositis`) on the "Anything else we should know?" list. Neither stops physiotherapy; the results page explains safe exercise and when to call the team or 911.
- **Already on the site:** stroke signs (911), spinal cord and cauda equina (emergency), spreading weakness in hands and feet with breathing or swallowing trouble (the neck's nrf-cord-legs, 911), the MS screen (pc-neuro), glove-and-stocking neuropathy, foot drop.
- **Choices to confirm:** the documents send fainting or palpitations with weakness to the emergency department; here they are 911, as the site's other fainting and irregular-heartbeat checks are. The documents' rapidly spreading weakness (emergency department) is covered by the neck's cord questions only, not in every region.
- **Myositis, decided by Chandra (2 Oct 2026):**
  - Dark urine with muscle pain or weakness is asked generally, not only after exercise: one shared question (group `rhabdo`) on the shoulder, upper arm, hip, thigh and lower leg, asked once; emergency department now.
  - Polymyalgia rheumatica has its own doctor-first question, `pc-pmr`: over 50 with both shoulders or both hips (upper arms, thighs) drawn, not the neck (pc-over50stiff asks there); morning stiffness over 45 minutes, feeling unwell, or the giant cell arteritis signs; no booking until a doctor has seen them, the same day with those signs.
  - The cancer link is mentioned calmly in the diagnosed entry (`ca-myositis`): some types are linked with lung problems and, less often, cancer, so the team may arrange screening tests.
  - The phase-based exercise intensities and the local referral wording (Mary Pack Arthritis Program and rheumatology, VGH neuromuscular clinic) are confirmed as written.

## Myasthenia gravis

- Recognise: weakness that fades with use within minutes and recovers with rest; worse by evening, with heat, infection, stress, some medicines; ptosis (often asymmetric) and diplopia first in most; bulbar (chewing, nasal speech, swallowing); neck flexor and proximal limbs; no sensory loss. Peaks: women 20-40, men over 60.
- Undiagnosed: document sustained up-gaze, arm-abduction time, repeated sit-to-stand decrement; GP letter for neurology (AChR/MuSK antibodies, RNS/SFEMG, CT chest); do not treat as mechanical.
- Diagnosed: confirm MGFA class and stability (no recent crisis or immunotherapy change); MG-ADL, QMG items (arm-abduction, leg-raise, neck-flexion times, grip), Fatigue Severity Scale, 6MWT, TUG, Berg; respiratory screen (orthopnoea, weak cough, single-breath count), FVC via the team.
- Exercise: moderate aerobic and progressive resistance with rests, 1-2 h after pyridostigmine, cool room, morning; stop at the onset of fatigable decline, never at exhaustion. Inspiratory muscle training with the team's agreement. Balance and falls; secondary neck, scapular and lumbar pain; steroid effects (bone, myopathy); pre- and post-thymectomy reconditioning.
- Crisis (breathing or swallowing failure) is 911. Every new medicine is checked against the MGFA cautionary list (some antibiotics, magnesium, beta-blockers, botulinum toxin, checkpoint inhibitors).

## Myotonic dystrophy

- Recognise: DM1, distal weakness (grip, foot drop), grip and percussion myotonia worse in cold and easing with repetition, ptosis, facial and temporal wasting, frontal balding, nasal speech, early cataracts, hypersomnia, family history with anticipation; DM2, proximal weakness and myalgia, milder and later. Average diagnostic delay about 7 years.
- The dangers are invisible on a body map: cardiac conduction disease (sudden death), respiratory failure, anaesthetic and sedation risk.
- Undiagnosed: GP letter for neurology (genetic test for the CTG expansion; CK, ECG); do not treat as carpal tunnel or ankle instability.
- Diagnosed: MIRS; grip and pinch dynamometry; myotonia relaxation time; MMT (ankle DF, neck flexion, finger flexors; hip and shoulder girdle in DM2); 6MWT, 10 m walk, TUG, Berg or Mini-BESTest; falls; Fatigue Severity Scale, Epworth; swallowing and respiratory screen; AFOs, footwear, grip aids; cognitive and apathy features, so keep programmes simple and written and involve family.
- Exercise: moderate aerobic and submaximal strength training are safe (Cochrane 2019); CBT with optional graded exercise increased activity and reduced fatigue (OPTIMISTIC 2018); avoid exhaustion and cold. Annual cardiology review (ECG, Holter, devices) and anaesthesia alert card.

## Inflammatory myopathy (myositis)

- Recognise: symmetrical proximal weakness (hips, thighs, shoulders, neck flexors) over weeks to months, constant rather than fatigable, hands, feet and face spared; aching, fatigue; dermatomyositis rash (heliotrope, Gottron's, V-sign, shawl), mechanic's hands; dysphagia, dry cough or dyspnoea (antisynthetase, ILD); statin onset (anti-HMGCR IMNM); cancer association (anti-TIF1γ, NXP2). Most often 40-60, women twice as often. Inclusion body myositis: over 50, slow over years, asymmetric, finger flexors and quadriceps, falls, dysphagia.
- Undiagnosed: sit-to-stand, neck flexion and shoulder abduction MMT; skin signs; weight loss; GP letter requesting CK, ESR/CRP, TSH, vitamin D and prompt rheumatology or neurology referral; flag statin history; do not treat as mechanical. Look-alikes: PMR (stiffness more than weakness, over 50; check for GCA), OA and rotator cuff (pain-limited), statin myalgia, thyroid, vitamin D, MG, dystrophies, MND, GBS (emergency).
- Diagnosed (BSR 2022: exercise led and monitored by specialist physiotherapy as integral treatment): subtype and antibody profile, disease-activity phase from the team; MMT-8, Functional Index-2 or -3, 30-s chair stand, TUG, 6MWT, grip, Myositis Activities Profile, HAQ; CK trend (a small transient rise after exercise is expected: monitor, do not stop); respiratory screen and FVC via the team; dysphagia to SLP.
- Exercise by phase: early-active, low-intensity resistance (about 40% 1RM, isometrics), ROM, breathing, short walks; improving, moderate resistance and aerobic 2-3 times a week; stable, progressive resistance to 70-80% 1RM and aerobic at 60-75% HRmax, supervised at first; a home programme in every phase. Steroid myopathy (no CK rise), bone health and falls. IBM: strengthening and aerobic still help; falls, quadriceps and finger flexors, orthoses, swallowing referral.
- Same-day team contact: a sudden drop in strength, new dysphagia or breathlessness, dark urine; emergency department if breathing is affected; on steroids, a fall with back, hip or wrist pain.
