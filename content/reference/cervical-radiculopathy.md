# Cervical radiculopathy: clinical reasoning supplement

Three different maps are in play, and mixing them up is the commonest reasoning error: (1) where the **skin** of a root is (dermatome), (2) where **pain** from an irritated root travels (radicular or dynatomal pattern, broader and more proximal than the dermatome), and (3) where pain from a joint or disc at that **level** is felt (somatic referred pain, no neurological signs). A peripheral nerve map (4) then tells a root problem from a nerve trapped further down. C7 is the most commonly affected root, then C6.

## A1. Three kinds of arm pain from the neck

| Type | What the patient describes | What it means for reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Somatic referred pain (joint, disc, muscle) | Deep, dull, aching, hard to pin down; a broad zone that spreads further when pain is stronger; neck, shoulder blade, top of shoulder, upper arm; rarely below the elbow; no pins and needles | Not a nerve root problem. Dermatome maps do not apply; use the segmental referral map (A3). Favour mechanical neck pain / CTJ. |
| Radicular pain (irritated nerve root) | Sharp, shooting, burning or electric; a narrow band or line; often past the elbow; usually with pins and needles; often worse in the arm than the neck | Nerve root irritation. Use the radicular pattern (A3) and paraesthesia location (A2, A5) to estimate the level. |
| Radiculopathy (loss of conduction) | Numbness, weakness, dropping things, a reflex change found in clinic; may occur with little or no pain | Objective root deficit. Level confirmed by dermatome + myotome + reflex (A2, A4). Worsening weakness: prompt medical review. |

## A2. Dermatomes (sensory map of each root)

Dermatomes overlap by up to half a segment and vary between people, so a single-root lesion rarely numbs the whole area shown. The ISNCSCI key point is the most reliable spot to test. Tingling is more level-specific than pain.

| Root | Dermatome (skin area) | ISNCSCI key sensory point |
|---|---|---|
| C2 | Back of the head, behind the ear, upper back of neck | At least 1 cm lateral to the occipital protuberance |
| C3 | Upper and side of neck, under the jaw line to the collarbone | Supraclavicular fossa, mid-clavicular line |
| C4 | Lower neck, top of the shoulder, upper chest below the collarbone | Over the AC joint |
| C5 | Outer upper arm over the deltoid, to the outer elbow | Lateral antecubital fossa, just above the crease |
| C6 | Outer (radial) forearm, thumb and index finger, radial hand | Dorsal proximal phalanx of the thumb |
| C7 | Back of the forearm, middle of the hand, middle finger | Dorsal proximal phalanx of the middle finger |
| C8 | Inner (ulnar) lower forearm, ulnar hand, ring and little fingers | Dorsal proximal phalanx of the little finger |
| T1 | Inner forearm and inner elbow | Medial antecubital fossa, above the medial epicondyle |
| T2 | Inner upper arm and armpit | Apex of the axilla |

## A3. Radiating distribution of each segment

Radicular pain is broader than the dermatome and very often includes the neck and shoulder blade. Somatic pain from the joint or disc stays proximal with no neurological signs. Scapular pain on its own does not identify a level.

| Root / level | Radicular pain (root irritated) | Somatic referred pain (joint / disc) | Engine note |
|---|---|---|---|
| C3 / C2-3 | Upper neck, back of head, around the ear; no arm pain | Upper neck and back of the head; can be headache | Neck-related headache |
| C4 / C3-4 | Side and back of neck, upper trapezius, top of shoulder; rarely the arm | Back and side of neck, upper trapezius | Neck-dominant |
| C5 / C4-5 | Neck, top and outside of shoulder, outer upper arm to the elbow | Lower neck, neck-shoulder angle, top of shoulder | Shoulder look-alike |
| C6 / C5-6 | Neck, shoulder blade, outer arm and forearm into thumb and index | Base of neck, upper shoulder blade, top of shoulder | Check carpal tunnel |
| C7 / C6-7 | Neck, inner border of the shoulder blade, back of arm and forearm into the middle finger; can cause front-of-chest pain | Middle and lower shoulder blade, medial border | Chest pain: heart first |
| C8 / C7-T1 | Lower shoulder blade, inner arm and forearm into ring and little fingers | Between the shoulder blades, lower medial scapula | Check ulnar nerve, TOS |
| T1 / T1-2 | Inner arm and elbow, armpit; hand intrinsic weakness | Upper thoracic, between the shoulder blades | Red flag: Horner, weight loss |

## A4. Motor, reflex and what the patient notices

| Root | Key muscles / movements | Reflex | What the patient may report |
|---|---|---|---|
| C4 | Shoulder shrug; diaphragm | None | Neck and shoulder heaviness |
| C5 | Elbow flexors; shoulder abduction; external rotation | Biceps | Hard to lift the arm to a shelf or brush hair |
| C6 | Wrist extensors; brachioradialis; supination | Brachioradialis, biceps | Weak wrist lifting a cup or pan |
| C7 | Triceps; wrist flexion; finger extension | Triceps | Hard to push up from a chair or push a door |
| C8 | Finger flexors (FDP middle); thumb flexion and extension | None reliable | Weak grip, jars, dropping things |
| T1 | Little-finger abductor; hand intrinsics | None | Clumsy with buttons, coins, keys |

## A5. Peripheral nerves (upper limb)

A peripheral nerve carries several roots, so its skin area crosses dermatome borders. Symptoms that fit a nerve map better than a dermatome point away from the neck.

| Nerve (roots) | Skin area | Main muscles | Where trapped / clue |
|---|---|---|---|
| Supraclavicular (C3-C4) | Collarbone and top of the shoulder | Sensory | Rarely trapped; overlaps C4 |
| Dorsal scapular (C5) | None | Rhomboids, levator scapulae | Medial scapular ache; mimics C5 / neck |
| Suprascapular (C5-C6) | None (deep shoulder ache) | Supra- and infraspinatus | Notches; overhead athletes; mimics C5 or cuff |
| Long thoracic (C5-C7) | None | Serratus anterior | Winging after heavy carrying or strain |
| Axillary (C5-C6) | Lower deltoid patch | Deltoid, teres minor | After dislocation or fracture; biceps spared (unlike C5) |
| Musculocutaneous (C5-C7) | Outer forearm | Biceps, brachialis | Rare; mimics C6 in the forearm, thumb spared |
| Radial (C5-T1) | Back of arm and forearm; back of thumb side; first web space | Triceps, brachioradialis, wrist and finger extensors | Spiral groove (wrist drop, flexion normal); PIN (finger drop, no numbness); superficial radial (numbness only) |
| Median (C6-T1) | Palm side of thumb, index, middle, half ring | Pronators, flexors, thenar | Carpal tunnel (night tingling, thenar skin spared); pronator; AIN (no "OK" sign) |
| Ulnar (C8-T1) | Little and half ring finger, front and back | FCU, FDP ring/little, hypothenar, interossei | Cubital tunnel (elbow bent); Guyon canal (back of hand spared) |
| Medial cutaneous of forearm (C8-T1) | Inner forearm | Sensory | Inner-forearm numbness = C8 root, lower trunk or TOS, not ulnar |
| Medial cutaneous of arm (T1), intercostobrachial (T2) | Inner upper arm and armpit | Sensory | Post-axillary surgery |

## A7. Root or peripheral nerve?

| Pair | Points to the root (neck) | Points to the peripheral nerve |
|---|---|---|
| C6 vs median (carpal tunnel) | Neck movement changes it; outer forearm and back of thumb; wrist extension or elbow flexion weak; BR / biceps reflex down | Night tingling eased by shaking; thenar palm spared; forearm normal; thumb abduction weak; reflexes normal |
| C8 vs ulnar (cubital tunnel) | Inner forearm numb; median C8/T1 thumb muscles weak too; neck provocation; scapular pain | Inner forearm normal; ring finger split; thumb median muscles normal; worse with the elbow bent; Froment; elbow tender |
| C7 vs radial | Triceps weak, reflex down; wrist flexion weak; middle finger; neck provocation | Wrist drop with normal wrist flexion; first web space numb; triceps spared; PIN = weakness without numbness |
| C5 vs shoulder / axillary / suprascapular | Biceps reflex down; elbow flexion weak; tingling down the outer arm; neck provocation | Painful arc, resisted cuff pain, normal sensation and reflexes; axillary = deltoid plus badge area |
| C8-T1 vs thoracic outlet / apical lung | Neck-provoked, dermatomal, one side | Whole-arm heaviness overhead or carrying (TOS); droopy eyelid, small pupil, smoking, weight loss: red flag |
| Both at once (double crush) | A root problem makes the same nerve more vulnerable further down | If both fit, screen and treat both; do not force one answer |

| Level | Pain / tingling (drawing) | Weakness (clinic) | Reflex |
|---|---|---|---|

| C5 | Top of shoulder, outer upper arm to the elbow | Abduction, elbow flexion | Biceps |
| C6 | Outer forearm into thumb and index | Elbow flexion, wrist extension | Brachioradialis (biceps) |
| C7 | Back of arm and forearm into middle finger; scapular ache | Elbow extension, wrist flexion, finger extension | Triceps |
| C8 | Inner forearm into ring and little fingers | Finger flexion, thumb extension, grip | None reliable |
| T1 | Inner upper arm and elbow | Finger abduction | None |

## A9. Weighting rules for the pattern engine

| Pattern the patient reports | Reasoning | Weight / route | On the site |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arm worse than neck + tingling in one part + worse looking up / turning to that side | Classic nerve root picture | Raise radiculopathy | N2 + N3 points |
| Neck worse than arm, vague ache above the elbow, no tingling | Somatic referral, not root | Favour mechanical neck pain | Upper arm only 2 vs past the elbow 3; mechanical neck pain +1 |
| Hand on head eases arm pain | Shoulder abduction relief | Supports radiculopathy | N2, 2 points |
| Numbness or weakness with little pain | Conduction loss without radicular pain | Keep radiculopathy; prompt booking | Weakness 1 point (for Chandra to confirm) |
| Both sides, clumsy hands, balance change | Possible myelopathy | Stop scoring, red-flag screen | N9 cord signs: radiculopathy -16, myelopathy card with doctor note |
| Night tingling thumb to middle, shaking eases, little neck pain | Peripheral entrapment more likely; both can coexist | Favour carpal tunnel; mention both | Wrist guide (not asked on the neck path) |
| Tingling thumb/index AND outer forearm | Crosses the wrist: C6, not median | Tag C6 | N12, clinic notes |
| Tingling ring/little AND inner forearm | Inner forearm is not ulnar territory | Tag C8 | N12, clinic notes |
| Ring/little, inner forearm normal, worse with the elbow bent | Ulnar nerve map | Favour ulnar entrapment, assessment | Elbow guide |
| Neck and shoulder blade only, no arm tingling | Scapular pain shared by several levels | No level | — |
| Arm pain with front-of-chest pain | C7 can refer to the chest; heart first | Red-flag screen first | Heart question (any arm, chest pain or pressure) |
| Severe, constant, no comfortable position, poor sleep | High irritability, sensitisation likely | Gentle wording; book sooner | Pain behaviour screen |

## B. Scope

- **In scope:** one-sided neck pain with arm-dominant radiating pain, tingling, numbness or weakness from the C5-T1 roots (degenerative or disc-related, non-traumatic onset).
- **Routed elsewhere:** upper neck (C0-C2) headache: neck-related headache. Upper neck instability signs: red-flag screen. Traumatic onset: Canadian C-Spine injury screen. Base of the neck without arm symptoms: base of neck (CTJ). After neck surgery: assessment.
