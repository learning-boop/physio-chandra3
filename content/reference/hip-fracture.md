# Hip fracture in older adults: emergency route and rehabilitation

## On the site

- **Emergency (911):** the hip injury screen, after a fall: cannot stand or walk, or the leg looks shorter or turned out; a high-energy injury; the SI region's fracture flag.
- **Same day:** after a minor fall, walking but with groin pain on weight bearing, at 65 or over or with osteoporosis (hidden fractures are often missed on the first X-ray).
- **Not built (see the CPG review notes):** sudden groin pain with no remembered fall in osteoporosis; dislocation after a hip operation; a "recovering from a hip operation" condition.

## Key facts

- Over 90% follow a fall, usually sideways; fall risk predicts fracture independently of bone density.
- 4-8% have another fracture within a year. Mortality rises with frailty, age over 85, dementia, other illness and low mobility before the fracture.
- After surgery, weight bearing as tolerated is the norm. Dislocation precautions (limit adduction, flexion and IR) may apply after a posterior-approach hemiarthroplasty or replacement; none after nails, screws or a sliding hip screw.

## Measure

- All settings: knee extension strength (A), verbal pain scale (A), gait speed (A), TUG (A), FES-I (B), New Mobility Score for status before the fracture (B).
- In hospital: Cumulated Ambulation Score (A).
- After discharge: hip extensor and abductor strength (B), 5-times or 30-second sit-to-stand (B), 6-minute walk (B); SPPB, DEMMI, AM-PAC, EQ-5D optional (C).

## Treat

- Orthogeriatric team care with early mobilisation (A): out of bed and walking as soon as possible, then daily (A); daily physiotherapy in hospital (B).
- Structured exercise: progressive high-intensity strength, balance, weight-bearing and functional training (A); the same approach in mild to moderate dementia (B).
- Extended therapy when deficits remain after 8-16 weeks (A): outpatient, a progressive home programme or community programmes; advice on safe physical activity (A); aerobic training optional (C).
- Quadriceps NMES, or electrical stimulation for pain if other methods fail (C).
- With the team: delirium prevention (A); falls assessment (A); pain at rest and on walking (F); pressure injury screen (F); osteoporosis care (F); individual goals (F); physiotherapy review within 72 hours of discharge (F).

## Refer

- Osteoporosis assessment and treatment with the family doctor (Osteoporosis Canada 2023; see upperback-osteoporosis).
- Sudden new pain or a clunk with a shortened or turned leg after surgery: emergency.
