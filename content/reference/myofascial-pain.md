# Myofascial pain (muscle-referred pain)

## On the site

- **Chandra's choices (2 Oct 2026):** one shared tender-spot question, and dry needling stays in (CHCPBC authorisation confirmed).
- **The question:** "Can you find a tender spot or tight band in a muscle that, when you press it, brings on your usual ache, including the part that spreads?" It is on the opening screen of the neck, shoulder, upper back, low back and hip, asked once however many of them are drawn (shared id `tender`, src/data/assessmentFlow.js), so it never takes one of the five scored-question places.
- **The conditions:** neck-myofascial (upper trapezius, levator scapulae, suboccipitals), shoulder-myofascial (infraspinatus, supraspinatus), upperback-myofascial (rhomboids, trapezius), lowback-myofascial (quadratus lumborum, paraspinals), hip-myofascial (gluteus medius and minimus, piriformis). Scored only from the opening screen: the tender spot (+4 when it reproduces the spread, +1 for a spot alone, -2 for none), a posture or desk start, pain under 6 weeks, and a fall or a dislocation against. They therefore never change which scored questions are asked, and the nerve, joint and tendon conditions still compete on their own answers.
- **Patient wording:** "a sensitive, tight muscle" and "a patch of muscle held tense to protect you", never "you have knots" or "damaged muscle"; the spreading ache is safe to move through.
- **Not built:** the document's six-question score and its hard rules (sharp, electric or tingling quality, or pain into the hand or foot, hand over to the nerve card; many regions over 3 months hand over to the widespread route). The nerve conditions, the MS screen and the widespread-pain section (content/reference/fibromyalgia.md) cover those routes on their own answers. A referred-pattern overlay on the 3D body (open item 5) is not built.

## Recognising it

- 2018 Delphi: 2 of 3 of a taut band, a hypersensitive spot, referred pain on stimulation; local twitch supportive. Palpation reliability is moderate: a working hypothesis, judged by response.
- Deep, dull, poorly bordered ache spreading in a cloud or band, not into the hand or foot, no tingling, numbness or weakness; posture and load related; the person can point to the source.
- Referral maps: upper trapezius to the temple and occiput; levator scapulae to the angle of the neck; suboccipitals to the eye and forehead; infraspinatus to the front of the shoulder and outer arm; masseter and temporalis to the teeth, jaw and temple; quadratus lumborum to the iliac crest and buttock; gluteus minimus to the outer and back of the thigh and calf; piriformis to the buttock and back of the thigh; gastrocnemius and soleus to the heel.
- Rule out first: radiculopathy, facet or disc referral, tendinopathy or bursitis, visceral referral, PMR, myopathy, red flags.

## Management

- Pressure release and soft tissue techniques; dry needling within CHCPBC authorisation and with consent (GRADE weak, short-term benefit, very low certainty long-term); heat, contract-relax stretch; then the active core: graded loading, movement variability, workstation, sleep and load changes, self-release teaching.
- Over 3 months, several regions and poor sleep: the persistent-pain framing and the widespread plan; do not keep chasing spots.
