# Osteomalacia ("soft bones") and rickets

The curable look-alike inside widespread aching ("fibromyalgia"), both-sided proximal weakness ("deconditioning") and "stress fractures" in people who do not run. The guide's job is a doctor-first bone blood test for the undiagnosed, and a recovery route for the diagnosed.

## On the site

- **Route A, undiagnosed adults: folded into the calcium screen (`pc-calcium`).** The hyperparathyroidism screen already asked about a deep ache "in the bones" on both sides for the same drawings (both shins, thighs or hips, or a widespread drawing), and the final check is already full for those drawings (up to six questions). A second, near-identical question would have pushed the thyroid and acromegaly questions out. The one question now adds osteomalacia's clues to its "together with any of these" list:
  - bones that hurt when pressed firmly on the shin, breastbone or front of the pelvis (Q2);
  - weak hips and thighs, or a side-to-side waddle (Q3);
  - a "stress fracture" or crack without running or heavy sport (Q5);
  - two or more of the risk cluster (Q4): darker skin; little sun on the skin (indoor or shift work, covering clothing); little dairy or a vegan diet; a gut condition or weight-loss surgery; kidney or liver disease; long-term anti-seizure medicine.
  The explanation names both causes ("osteomalacia, 'soft bones'", the parathyroid gland), says soft bones are often mistaken for fibromyalgia or arthritis, and lists one blood panel: vitamin D, calcium, phosphate, alkaline phosphatase, parathyroid hormone and kidney function. Family doctor in the next week or two; booking after that visit (the calcium screen's signed rule); "if a bone may have cracked, avoid loading it until it has been checked".
- **Children: `pc-rickets`** (final check, after the early-signs question for a child). Under 5 with a leg or wrist drawn, or 5 to 15 with both legs drawn: bowing or knock-knees beyond other children (after about 3, worsening, or one-sided), thick wrists or ankles, a row of bumps along the ribs, aching legs most days, late walking or teeth. Family doctor or paediatrician, no booking; 911 for a seizure, breathing trouble or floppiness.
- **Route C, diagnosed and being treated:** `ca-osteomalacia` on the cautions list. Results panel "Soft bones and getting strong again" (also in the PDF): recovery timeline (strength 4 to 12 weeks, aches and cracks 3 to 6 months), keep taking prescribed vitamin D and calcium (symptoms return when supplements stop; no self-dosing), graded walking, sit-to-stands and step-ups, the doctor's weight-bearing plan for a crack, balance and trip hazards, calcium-rich foods, safe sun and winter supplements (open item 7, kept, no doses), review if not better after about 3 months (rarer forms), same day for a sudden groin, hip or thigh pain with trouble weight-bearing, same day for tingling around the mouth or hand and foot cramps, 911 for a seizure.
- **Cross-links (open item 5, adopted):**
  - The hip, thigh, knee, shin and foot stress-fracture safety questions (`hpf-stress`, `tgf-stress`, `kf-stress`, `lgf-stress`, `ft-stress`) add: "If this is not your first stress fracture, or it came on with only a small increase in training, also ask your doctor for a bone blood test" (those questions are asked of runners only, so this catches the soft-bone and under-fuelling cases among them; a crack without sport is a clue in the calcium screen). The shin and foot stress-fracture result cards say the same.
  - The widespread-pain doctor line names vitamin D and calcium alongside the thyroid test.
  - The osteoporosis record asks whether vitamin D was checked with calcium and PTH; its clinic note warns that a low DXA can be osteomalacia (no antiresorptives before repletion).

## Decisions on the open items (the document's recommendations; awaiting sign-off)

1. The self-press test (Q2) is kept, as one of the clues in the screen.
2. The risk cluster is in the screen, worded neutrally ("darker skin", "little sun on your skin", "clothing that covers most of your skin outdoors"). "Moved to Canada from a sunnier country" is left out (covered by the sun and skin items, and it would single out newcomers). No community is named anywhere. The answer is a single yes to the whole question; no risk factor is recorded on its own.
3. Named on the screen as "osteomalacia, 'soft bones'".
4. Booking: the merged question keeps the calcium screen's signed "doctor first, book after that visit". The document's "booking may follow when no fracture is suspected" is not built, because the same question also catches a possibly high calcium.
5. Cross-links: adopted (above).
6. Child content: a short doctor-first question only; no child physiotherapy route (under 5s are not booked in any case).
7. Diet and sun tips: kept in the diagnosed panel, general, no doses.
8. Local resources (Fraser Health, South Asian Health Institute): not added; to add if Chandra wants a named service.

## Not built

- The scored set and its POSSIBLE band (4-7 "mention it to your doctor"): the screen is one yes/no question, as for the other hormonal entries.
- The Q3 + Q4 route without bone pain (weak hips plus risk factors): the screen's gate stays a deep bone ache on both sides; both-sided weakness alone goes to the myositis, steroid, pituitary, nerve-and-muscle and thyroid questions.
- §6 red flags already asked elsewhere: spreading numbness or bladder change (cauda equina questions), steadily worse or night bone pain with weight loss (the general "fever, weight loss…" question), rapidly worsening weakness (the general weakness question), a hip crack without a fall (the hip's fracture questions). Low-calcium tingling and cramps, and the child emergencies, are in the panel and the rickets explanation rather than separate safety questions.

## Clinic notes (from the document)

- Uday & Högler criteria: bone pain + bone tenderness (tibia, sternum, pelvis) + proximal weakness or waddle + risk factors, with or without raised ALP and low 25-OH-D.
- Exam: pressure tenderness (tibial shaft, sternum, iliac crests and pubic rami, ribs), proximal MMT, Trendelenburg, 30-second chair stand or 5 times sit-to-stand (expect hands), TUG, gait, metatarsal and rib tenderness, kyphosis and height loss, Chvostek and Trousseau if hypocalcaemia suspected.
- Femoral-neck pseudofracture: groin pain with an antalgic gait → non-weight-bearing and imaging (MRI if the X-ray is negative).
- Labs are the GP's: 25-OH-D (deficiency under 25-30 nmol/L), calcium and phosphate low-normal, ALP raised, PTH raised, eGFR, coeliac serology; Looser's zones on X-ray. DXA often looks osteoporotic: exclude osteomalacia before antiresorptives.
- Not responding to vitamin D: hypophosphatasia, XLH, tumour-induced osteomalacia → bone specialist.
- Physio timing: myopathy improves over 4-12 weeks; start low-load strength and gait retraining; protect pseudofractures (partial weight-bearing per plan, 3-6 months); progress as pain settles and ALP normalises; pool early; balance and falls work.
- Prevention: maintenance vitamin D per Health Canada and Osteoporosis Canada (dosing to the GP); recurrence is common when supplements stop. Interpreter and culturally sensitive discussion of clothing, sun and diet; no stereotyping.
