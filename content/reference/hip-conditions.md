## Safety changes

- hpf-septic (emergency): adds a child or teenager suddenly unable to stand or walk on the leg at all, with or without a fever (an unstable slip; Webb, AFP 2025). Placed here rather than in a child-only question because every emergency question must be asked at every age.
- hpf-sufe (same day): a limp even without pain (Perthes), or not wanting to take weight; the pain-site clause dropped.
- hpf-stress (doctor first) keeps the running ache; its reason names missed periods, low bone density, low energy and a past stress fracture. New hpf-stressday (same day): the ache now comes with every step or at night.
- Injury screen I7 (emergency): after a fall, crash, twist or tackle, a numb foot or a foot or toes that will not lift (sciatic nerve injury with a dislocation).
- Card lines: hip-fai and hip-snapping, a locked hip, see the doctor soon; hip-loosebody, a locked hip, same day; hip-hipoa, stiffness over an hour or other swollen joints, doctor; hip-postop, new groin pain or a limp months after a dislocation or fracture, doctor (AVN).

## New answers (no new questions)

- G2: "Taking long strides or walking fast (easier with shorter steps)".
- G3: "Stiff in the morning for more than an hour"; "A snap or clunk I can feel or see, and can make happen"; "Sudden sharp twinges that stop me mid-step, eased by shaking the leg"; "I am very flexible, or a doctor told me I have a shallow hip socket". G3 now has 9 answers.
- G5: "Squeezing my knees together hurts in the groin".
- Start: "After hip surgery (a replacement or fracture repair), or a dislocation put back in hospital".

## Choices made against the forms (the site's rules win)

- No "unstable" in patient wording (forms 6, 13); no injection or medication advice.
- The forms' single-area scores and thresholds map to pointers so a form's "likely" lands near the site's 40% rule; the forms' own 6-question sets are not added one by one (5-question budget).
- Tendon tears (hamstring, gluteal, adductor pops) stay same day or doctor first, as the injury screen already routes them.
- SIJ P5 (pregnancy pelvic girdle questions) now has priority whenever it is asked: the new hip answers had pushed it out on a pelvis-and-hip line (test SIJ 2b).

## Checked against the manuals

- Match the Lower Quadrant Clinical Manual Ch. 5: Sutlive 2008 (4 of 5, +LR 24.3), Metcalfe 2019 (medial thigh +LR 7.8), Wright 2011, Grimaldi 30-s single-leg stance (spec 100, +LR 12.2), hip lag sign (89/97), Hoppe 2017 (AB-HEER 80.6/89.4, prone instability 33.9/97.9), Table 5-8 labral tests, patellar-pubic percussion (95/86).
- Match the AIM Theory Manual 2023: ischiofemoral impingement tests (IFI 0.82/0.85, long stride 0.94/0.85).
- Checked online 26 Oct 2026 (not in either manual): the labral form's Thomas test (sens 89 / spec 92) is in Reiman 2013 (BJSM 47:893-902), from one small arthroscopy study with no confidence intervals; Narvani 2003 found sens 75 / spec 43 against MR arthrogram, and Dhillon 2025 (ASMR) calls hip test accuracy highly variable. Added to the hip-fai notes with that caveat.
- Microinstability Delphi (Khanduja 2023, KSSTA 31(1):40-49): 37 criteria, 14 major; at least 4 in each of history, examination and imaging, with 6 or more major. The hip-hipinstab note said 34 criteria: corrected.
- Bone stress injury Delphi (Hoenig 2025, BJSM 59(2):78-90): confirmed; 41 panel members, consensus on 41 of 58 statements across pathophysiology and diagnosis, risk and prevention, and management and return to sport. Its return-to-sport statements were not read in full; return planning depends on the site and grade (MRI grade 1 to 4: about 42, 70, 84 and 99 days to return, Hoenig 2022 meta-analysis), and the low-risk / high-risk site split is not well validated (Hoenig 2023). The femoral neck stays doctor first on the site.
- Osteonecrosis of the femoral head: no 2021 Chinese guideline was found; the 2019 version (Zhao, J Orthop Translat 2020;21:100-110) with ARCO 2019 staging remains the current reference.
