# Acromegaly (growth-hormone excess)

Rare (about 6 per 100,000) but a physiotherapy diagnosis in disguise: joint pain is the commonest complaint and the median delay to diagnosis is about 10 years. The guide never diagnoses it.

## On the site

- **Route A, undiagnosed:** `pc-acromegaly`, the last pattern question. The growth change is a hard gate (open item 1): hands or feet grown in adulthood (rings, gloves, shoe size) or a heavier jaw, brow or nose, together with any of: several large joints or a broad aching, stooping back early for the age; both hands numb at night or thick and clumsy; or two or more of snoring or daytime sleepiness, headaches, thick oily or sweaty skin, new high blood pressure or sugar, skin tags, irregular periods or low libido. That is the document's rule "Q1 yes with Q3, Q4 or Q5 at least 2". A yes: family doctor in the next few weeks for an IGF-1 blood test, with an old photo; booking still offered (open item 4, as for hypothyroidism); names the pituitary and growth hormone (open item 5).
- **When it is asked (open item 2):** for both knees, hips, shoulders, hands or wrists drawn, the jaw, or a widespread drawing. This drawing-based question stands in for the document's optional "have your hands, feet or jaw grown?" chip on the carpal tunnel, large-joint and back records; the records themselves are unchanged. It is not asked for a single joint or the low back alone, so ordinary back pain never sees it.
- **Route B, diagnosed:** "Acromegaly or another growth-hormone problem, diagnosed by a doctor (including before or after pituitary surgery)" on the cautions list (`ca-acromegaly`). Results and PDF panel "Acromegaly and your joints, spine and hands": joint and spine changes tend to stay after control, so a long-term, joint-protective plan; low-impact daily movement; bone-safe lifting, with spinal fractures possible "even when a bone-density scan looks normal" (open item 6); sleep study for snoring; after surgery, the surgeon's own advice on straining and nose-blowing (open item 8) and the signs that need the team (clear fluid from the nose, fever with stiff neck, extreme thirst, severe tiredness and dizziness); 911 for sudden headache with vision loss or for chest pain; breathlessness lying flat, swollen ankles or palpitations → doctor urgently; the bowel line (open item 7: kept, worded as "worth seeing your doctor about").
- **Space on the final check:** both shoulders at 50 or over now fill all six places, with acromegaly last. Both hips at 50 or over have seven candidates, so the acromegaly question is the one that drops there; every other both-sided drawing reaches it.

## Decisions on the open items (accepted with the sign-off, 3 Oct 2026)

1. Growth change is a hard gate: never asked on joint and hand symptoms alone.
2. The "have your hands, feet or jaw grown?" question is asked from the drawing (both large joints, both hands, the jaw, widespread) rather than as chips inside three condition records.
3. Scores collapsed into one question with the document's rule; the POSSIBLE band is not built.
4. Booking stays available after the screen.
5. Names the pituitary and growth hormone.
6. "Even when a bone-density scan looks normal", once, as a loading rule.
7. The bowel-polyp line is in the diagnosed panel.
8. After surgery: the surgeon's stated period, no fixed number on the site (4-6 weeks stays in these notes).

## Already on the site, not repeated

Sudden severe headache and stroke-type signs (head and neck questions); the 911 heart questions; spine fragile-fracture questions (they name osteoporosis and steroids, not acromegaly; the diagnosed panel covers it); the cervical myelopathy and spinal stenosis questions.

## Not built

- Gradual side-vision loss, worsening headaches and the cardiac and sleep-apnoea items as tick-box questions for the undiagnosed (the screen's cluster asks about snoring and headaches).
- The POSSIBLE band line.

## Clinic notes (from the document)

- Red flags: visual fields (bitemporal), new or worsening headache, cardiac symptoms (cardiomyopathy, arrhythmia, hypertension), untreated OSA (driving), vertebral fracture after minor load, post-operative complications.
- Cues (Endocrine Society 2014, Pituitary Society 2021, Consensus 2024): acral enlargement, frontal bossing, prognathism and malocclusion, macroglossia, spade-like hands, heel-pad thickening, skin tags, hyperhidrosis. Comorbidities: hypertension, dysglycaemia, OSA, colonic polyps, thyroid nodules, hypopituitarism from mass effect (hypopituitarism.md), carpal tunnel. Diagnosis is the GP's and endocrinologist's: age-adjusted IGF-1, OGTT GH suppression, pituitary MRI.
- MSK exam: early hypermobility and widened joint space, then OA-like narrowing, osteophytes and crepitus in knees, hips, shoulders and spine (thoracic kyphosis, lumbar hyperlordosis, widened discs, stenosis); TMJ; hand and foot enlargement; carpal tunnel (Phalen, Tinel, CTS-6, thenar wasting); proximal strength and endurance (reduced despite bulk); 30-second chair stand, TUG, 6MWT with BP and HR; balance.
- Bone: vertebral fractures 3-8 times more prevalent despite normal or high BMD, so DXA is not reassuring; morphometric VFA or X-ray via the GP; height, wall-occiput, rib-pelvis; bone-safe loading regardless of DXA. Arthropathy persists or progresses after control (HR about 1.7): set long-term expectations.
- Exercise: BP control and OSA treatment before vigorous work; joint-protective progressive resistance, aquatic options for load-sensitive hips and knees, thoracic mobility and posture, footwear and orthoses for enlarged feet and heel pads, TMJ advice. After surgery: no nose-blowing, Valsalva or heavy straining for the surgeon's period (typically 4-6 weeks); watch for hypopituitarism and adrenal insufficiency; medicine side effects (somatostatin analogues: GI; pegvisomant; dopamine agonists).
