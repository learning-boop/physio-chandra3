## red flags
<!-- Because a low-back mark also asks the TL junction, flags that ask the
     same thing there (aneurysm, bladder/bowel, fracture, cancer,
     osteoporosis, infection, kidney) are shown once, in this wording. -->
- Do you have new numbness or tingling between your legs, around your bottom, or in your genitals (the area you would sit on a saddle)? | emergency | Possible cauda equina syndrome
- Have you had new trouble starting to pass urine, not being able to feel when your bladder is full, leaking urine, or losing control of your bowels? | emergency | Possible cauda equina syndrome
- In the last few days, has your leg pain spread to both legs, or has weakness in your leg or foot been getting quickly worse? | emergency | Possible cauda equina syndrome or severe nerve compression
- Do you have a sudden, severe pain in your back or tummy, with a pulsing feeling in your tummy, or feeling faint or sweaty? | emergency | Possible leaking abdominal aortic aneurysm (higher risk over 60 and in smokers)
- Did this start in the last few days after a car crash, a fall from a height, or landing hard on your feet or bottom? | emergency | Possible spinal fracture
- Have you ever had cancer, and is this a new back pain? | urgent | Cancer can spread to the spine
- Did the pain start suddenly after a minor strain, cough, or lift, and you have osteoporosis, take long-term steroid tablets, or are over 70? | urgent | Possible osteoporotic fracture of the spine
- Do you have a fever or chills with the back pain, or a weakened immune system, or have you injected drugs? | urgent | Possible spinal infection
- Is your foot slapping down or your toes catching when you walk, even if it is not getting worse? | urgent | Nerve weakness (foot drop) needs medical review
- Does the pain come in waves from your side to your groin, or come with a fever, burning when you pass urine, or blood in your urine? | urgent | Possible kidney stone or kidney infection
- Are you under 20, and does it hurt to arch your back, especially with sport such as gymnastics, dance, cricket bowling, or tennis? | urgent | Possible stress fracture of the spine (spondylolysis); needs imaging
- Is the pain linked to your periods, or do you have unusual vaginal bleeding, or (for men) new trouble passing urine? | urgent | Pelvic organ and prostate problems can be felt in the low back

## questions
Q: How far does the pain go?
- Low back only
- Into the buttock
- Down the thigh, stopping above the knee
- Below the knee, into the leg or foot
- Front of the thigh or groin

Q: Which is worse?
- The back pain
- The leg pain
- About the same
Ask only if: How far = "Down the thigh, stopping above the knee", "Below the knee, into the leg or foot", or "Front of the thigh or groin"

Q: Which of these do you notice in the leg? Tick all that apply.
- Pins and needles or numbness in the foot or toes
- Shooting pain down the leg when I cough or sneeze
- The leg pain is worse when I bend forward or sit
- None of these
Ask only if: How far = "Below the knee, into the leg or foot"

Q: Which of these bring it on? Tick all that apply.
- Bending forward, or sitting
- Arching back, or standing for a long time
- Twisting
- Getting up after sitting or lying
- Rolling over in bed, or standing on one leg

Q: What happens when you walk?
- Walking eases it
- Walking brings on leg pain, heaviness, or tingling that eases when I sit or bend forward
- Walking makes my back worse, but not my legs
- Walking does not change it
Ask only if: Age = "50 to 64" or "65 or over", or How far = "Below the knee, into the leg or foot"

Q: If you point to the worst spot with one finger, where is it?
- In the middle of the low back, on the spine
- On one side, beside the spine above the belt line
- Over the dimple at the back of my pelvis
- Spread over a wide area; I cannot point to one spot

Q: Which of these apply? Tick all that apply.
- My back catches or gives way with small movements
- I push on my thighs to stand up straight after bending
- I get frequent flare-ups from small movements
- It feels stiff rather than weak
- None of these

Q: Which of these do you do regularly? Tick all that apply.
- Sport with a lot of arching back (gymnastics, dance, fast bowling, tennis)
- Heavy lifting at work or the gym
- Sitting most of the day
- Driving long distances
- None of these

## referral patterns
- Low back → buttock and back of the thigh, stopping above the knee | Referred pain from the lumbar joints or discs (not a nerve root). Joints and discs can also refer below the knee, so distance alone does not mean a nerve root | Sacroiliac joint, hip joint, deep gluteal pain
- Low back → below the knee (L4 inner shin, L5 top of the foot and big toe, S1 outer foot and sole) | Nerve root (radicular) when there are nerve-type symptoms. Without them, consider joint, disc, or gluteus minimus referral instead | Deep gluteal (piriformis), peroneal nerve at the knee, tarsal tunnel
- Dimple at the back of the pelvis → buttock, back of the thigh, sometimes the groin | Sacroiliac joint pain | Hip joint, lumbar spine
- Upper lumbar (L2–L3) → front of the thigh and groin | Femoral nerve root or referred pain | Hip joint, meralgia paraesthetica, thoracolumbar junction (see TL file)
- Aorta, kidney, or pelvic organs → low back | Not musculoskeletal: red flag | Emergency or doctor first
- Gluteus minimus → outer and back of the thigh and calf, down to the ankle | Muscle referral that looks like sciatica ("pseudo-sciatica"), without nerve-type symptoms | L5 or S1 nerve root
- Quadratus lumborum → top of the hip bone, sacroiliac joint, buttock, outer hip | Muscle trigger point referral | Lumbar joints (L1–L3), kidney, trochanteric pain
- Iliopsoas → vertical band beside the lumbar spine; front of the thigh | Muscle referral | L2–L3 nerve root, hip joint
- Hip joint → buttock (71%), thigh (57%), groin (55%); below the knee in about 1 in 5; knee via the obturator nerve | Hip joint referral (Lesher 2008) | Lumbar spine, knee joint

Flags: none

Drawing: Low back and both buttocks
Answers: Age = 30 to 49; After lifting or bending; Less than 2 weeks
Flags: New numbness or tingling between your legs, around your bottom...
Expect: top condition = none; must not show = any condition, any booking; route = 911

CASE: 5. Spondylolysis look-alike
Drawing: Low back in the middle, on the spine
Answers: Age = Under 18; Gradually, no clear reason; 2 to 6 weeks; Q1 = low back only; Q4 = arching back, or standing for a long time; Q6 = in the middle of the low back, on the spine; Q8 = sport with a lot of arching back
Flags: Are you under 20, and does it hurt to arch your back...
Expect: top condition = none; must not show = LBP with mobility deficits, any booking before review; route = physician first
