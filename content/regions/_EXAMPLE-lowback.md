## red flags
- New numbness or tingling around the groin, genitals, or inner thighs (the 'saddle' area) | emergency | Possible cauda equina compression, which needs same-day surgical assessment.
- New difficulty starting urination, or loss of bladder or bowel control | emergency | Possible cauda equina compression, which needs same-day surgical assessment.
- Weakness in both legs, or leg weakness that is getting worse | emergency | Suggests a nerve or spinal cord problem rather than simple irritation.
- First-ever episode of severe back pain and you're over 55 | urgent | A first severe episode later in life is screened for fracture and other medical causes before physiotherapy.

## referral patterns
- low back → below the knee | nerve root irritation (radicular) when there is burning, shooting, pins and needles or numbness | deep gluteal / piriformis, hip joint referral, tarsal tunnel
- low back → buttock and thigh only | referred ache from the back's joints, disc and muscles — treated like local back pain, NOT nerve pain | hip joint referral, sacroiliac joint
