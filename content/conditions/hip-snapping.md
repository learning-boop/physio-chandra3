## blurb
A snap or clunk you can feel, see or hear as the hip moves. On the outside of the hip, a band of tissue flicks over the bony point; at the front, the hip flexor tendon flicks over the bone underneath. It is common in dancers, gymnasts and runners, is often painless and harmless, and when it is sore most people settle with changes to training and a targeted exercise plan.

## noticed
- A snap or clunk with certain movements, that you can usually make happen on purpose
- Over the outer hip when walking, running or on stairs, or at the front of the hip when lifting the leg out and lowering it
- Often painless, or an ache afterwards

## homeCare
- Avoid making the hip snap on purpose
- Reduce the drills or sessions that bring it on for a short while, then build back gradually
- Warm up gradually before training
- Keep active with movements that feel comfortable

## seePhysioIf
- The snapping is painful, or is affecting your training or daily activities
- It comes with catching or giving way: see us, as the joint itself may be involved
- The hip locks so you cannot move it: see your doctor soon
- It started after a fall or an injury
- Painful snapping has not settled after 2 to 3 months of a guided plan: we can talk with you about a medical opinion

- A physiotherapist can watch the movement that makes the snap, feel where it comes from, and explain it in plain words.
- Exercises for hip and trunk strength and control, with flexibility work where it helps, can help settle a painful snap.
- You get a plan to adjust training for a while and build back up.

## clinicNotes
- External (ITB or gluteus maximus over the greater trochanter; AIM Theory Manual 2023 p. 375): reproduce with flexion-extension with or without IR in standing or side-lying; palpable or visible; Ober or modified Ober for TFL-ITB length. Often coexists with gluteal tendinopathy (hip-gtps).
- Internal (iliopsoas over the iliopectineal eminence or femoral head): reproduce moving from FABER into extension-adduction-IR; usually audible; Thomas test; psoas bursa provocation (manual pp. 305-306). Painful internal snapping with flexor pain: hip-iliopsoas.
- Intra-articular "snapping" (labrum, loose body): a painful click, often after trauma, with catching or giving way: work up as intra-articular (hip-fai, hip-loosebody).
- Dynamic ultrasound confirms tendon movement; an injection can separate internal from external. Walker 2021's test table appears to swap the internal and external columns: verify before use. Most settle within 6 to 12 months; surgery is rarely needed.
- Stretching: the gluteal protocols advise against ITB stretching while the outer hip tendons are sore; Chandra's snapping hip protocol recommends it. Use flexibility work only where the tissue is short and the tendon is not irritable.
- No CPG exists (searched 26 Oct 2026); the JOSPT nonarthritic hip CPG 2023 does not grade it. Sources: AIM Theory Manual 2023 Ch. 2.6 pp. 375-376; Lower Quadrant Clinical Manual (Jan 2023) Ch. 5 pp. 292-294, 305-306; Walker P et al., Orthop Rev (Pavia) 2021;13(2):25088.
