## blurb
A sprain is an over-stretch of the tissues that hold the shoulder joint together, from a fall, a tackle or the arm being wrenched. The joint stays in place, but it is sore and stiff while it heals. Many sprains settle within a few weeks, and strengthening can help you get back to full activity.

## noticed
- Pain that started with a fall, a tackle or a sudden wrench
- A deep ache with stiffness, sometimes swelling or bruising over the next day or two
- Sore and protective rather than loose

## homeCare
- Support the arm for comfort for the first 2 to 3 days, with ice wrapped in a cloth for 10 to 15 minutes at a time
- Start gentle arm swings and keep the elbow, wrist and hand moving
- Avoid heavy lifting and contact sport until it has been checked

## seePhysioIf
- The arm looks a different shape, is very swollen, or you cannot lift it at all: see a doctor the same day
- A numb patch on the outer upper arm, or the arm is clearly weak: see a doctor within a few days
- Pain that comes on with effort, chest tightness or breathlessness: call 911
- It is painful and stiff: book in the first week, so recovery can start
- It is not improving after 4 to 6 weeks: see your doctor

## physioHelps

- A physiotherapist can check the injury and whether anything needs an X-ray.
- Gentle movement, then shoulder and shoulder-blade strengthening, can help you get back to full use.
- You get a step-by-step return to sport or work.

## clinicNotes
- Mainly a diagnosis by exclusion: rule out fracture or dislocation (X-ray if indicated), a rotator cuff tear (strength testing, lag signs), AC and SC joint injury (palpation), labral injury. Directional passive stability tests with end-feel; grade I-III; PSFS.
- Sources: Lizzio VA et al. 2017; Hegedus EJ et al., BJSM 2012; Lewis J, Man Ther 2016; "PhysioChandra Shoulder Conditions.docx" form 25 (v1.0, 19 Oct 2026).
