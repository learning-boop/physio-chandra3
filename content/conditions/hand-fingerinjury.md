## blurb
Jammed fingers, thumb sprains and small finger fractures are common, and the finger often stays swollen and stiff for weeks. Once a doctor has checked it, a splint for the right length of time and then gentle, regular movement and strengthening can bring most fingers back to good use.

## noticed
- A swollen, stiff finger or thumb joint after an injury
- It will not fully bend or straighten, or a weak pinch or grip
- Tender at the joint, sometimes for several months; it can stay a little swollen and stiff in the morning for longer

## homeCare
- Wear any splint exactly as your doctor or hand therapist advised: for a drooping fingertip (mallet finger), keep the tip straight all the time, and do not bend it to test it
- Keep the other fingers moving, and raise the hand when it swells
- Buddy-tape a sprained finger to its neighbour for sport if you were told to, and keep bending and straightening it
- Ice wrapped in a cloth and raising the hand for the first few days can ease swelling
- When you change a mallet splint, rest the finger flat on a table so the tip never bends, and check the skin under the splint every day
- If your splint holds only the middle joint, keep bending the fingertip as you were shown
- For a thumb sprain, wear the thumb splint as advised and avoid pinching and gripping large objects while it heals

## seePhysioIf
- The tip of a finger droops after an injury and has not been checked: see a doctor within a week
- The thumb knuckle is painful on the index-finger side, pinching is weak, it feels loose or wobbly when you pinch, or there is a firm lump on the inside of the knuckle, after a fall or the thumb was bent back: see a doctor within a few days
- The middle joint of a finger is swollen after an injury and will not straighten fully: see a doctor today
- The finger stays stiff or swollen after the splint comes off
- You would like help with exercises, splints and a return to sport or work

## clinicNotes
- Mallet finger: continuous extension splint about 6 weeks (bony) or 8 weeks (tendon), then about 2 weeks at night (hospital patient leaflets). PIP sprain or volar plate: early protected motion, buddy taping, extension-block splint if unstable; fracture-dislocation needs X-ray. Skier's thumb: Stener lesion needs surgery; partial tears, thumb spica 4 to 6 weeks then strengthening (AIM manual). Boutonnière: PIP extension splint, DIP free.
- Later (no card yet): finger ganglions (seed cyst, mucous cyst), diabetic stiff hand (cheiroarthropathy).
- Mallet (form 28, 20 Oct 2026): X-ray: a fragment over 30% of the joint or volar subluxation needs a surgical opinion; stack or thermoplastic splint, PIP free; wean with night splinting; watch for swan neck; a chronic mallet often still responds to splinting; DASH (Lamaris and Matthew 2017).
- Boutonnière (form 29): Elson test; PIP extension splint about 6 weeks continuously, DIP free, active DIP flexion; X-ray for an avulsion; established deformity: serial casting or dynamic splinting; surgical referral for an open injury, a large avulsion or a fixed deformity; pseudo-boutonnière (Elzinga and Chung 2019). Swan neck (form 30): PIP hyperextension with DIP flexion; volar plate laxity, intrinsic tightness, untreated mallet, FDS laxity, RA; figure-of-eight splint; SARAH in RA (Lamb 2015).
- Collateral sprain and PIP/MCP dislocation (form 23): stress tests in extension and at 30 degrees (laxity, end-point), grade I-III; buddy tape or splint 3-4 weeks; early AROM, oedema control, tendon gliding; intrinsic tightness after immobilisation; surgical opinion for gross laxity or an irreducible dislocation (Freiberg 2007). Volar plate (form 24): X-ray for an avulsion; dorsal blocking splint at 20-30 degrees for 3 weeks, extended about 10 degrees a week, active flexion; buddy tape to 6 weeks; surgical referral for large fragments or persistent laxity.
- Skier's thumb (form 21): X-ray before stress testing; valgus stress at 30 degrees of MCP flexion and in extension; over 30 degrees or 15 degrees more than the other side, or no end point: complete tear; a palpable mass is a Stener lesion: prompt surgical referral (HAND_INJURY I6 asks after a fall, a bent-back thumb or a jam). Grade I-II: thumb spica about 6 weeks (AIM: 4 to 6), active movement from 3 weeks, strengthening from 8 weeks, splint for sport to 3 months; protect the digital nerves (Heyman 1993; Stener 1962).
