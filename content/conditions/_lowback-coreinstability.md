## blurb
This pattern happens when the deep muscles that support your low back aren't switching on well, so the spine feels like it catches, gives way, or moves awkwardly rather than smoothly. It's often linked to repeated flare-ups of low back pain, sometimes with pain spreading into the buttock or leg.

## noticed
- A history of several episodes of low back pain over months or years, often set off by simple movements
- A feeling of catching, giving way, or needing to push up through your thighs when standing up from a bent position
- Pain that can appear with only moderate bending or twisting, not just extreme positions

## homeCare
- Practice gentle core-activation exercises that engage the deep abdominal and low back muscles
- Build trunk strength and endurance gradually rather than jumping into heavy activity
- Try to keep your low back supported in a comfortable mid-range position during daily tasks

## seePhysioIf
- Your back keeps giving way or catching with everyday movements
- You've had several repeat episodes and want a plan to reduce future flare-ups
