## blurb
A thin skin nerve runs down the thumb side of the forearm to the back of the thumb and wrist. A tight watch strap, cuff, bracelet, or a knock can press on it, giving burning, tingling, or numbness over the back of the thumb and wrist rather than a deep ache.

## noticed
- Burning, tingling, or numbness over the back of the thumb and wrist
- Worse with a tight watch, cuff, bracelet, or with turning the forearm
- Tender where the nerve runs, a little above the wrist on the thumb side

## homeCare
- Loosen or move your watch strap, cuff, or bracelet
- Avoid pressure on the thumb side of the forearm and wrist
- Ease off repeated twisting of the forearm for a while
- If the skin has become oversensitive, gently stroke it with soft, then rougher, textures for a few minutes a day

## seePhysioIf
- The burning or tingling lasts more than a few weeks after removing the pressure
- The numb patch is spreading
- You are not sure whether it is this or a tendon problem at the wrist
- Your hand or thumb becomes weak: see a doctor, as this nerve carries feeling only and does not usually weaken the hand

## physioHelps
<!-- Approved by Chandra Matla, 17 Oct 2026 (review: content/review/physioHelps-review-2026-10-17.docx) -->
- A physiotherapist can check the thin skin nerve and explain what is pressing on it.
- Simple changes, like loosening straps and easing pressure, can help the nerve settle.
- You get advice on daily tasks and forearm twisting to help the symptoms stay settled.

- Form 18 (20 Oct 2026): Tinel's at the brachioradialis/ECRL interval (the most common finding); provocation with forced pronation and ulnar deviation; ULNT2b; Finkelstein's may be positive (a nerve stretch), so tell it apart by the sensory change and no tendon tenderness; motor exam normal; cervical screen; DASH (Dang and Rodner 2009; Nee 2012; Huisstede 2014).
