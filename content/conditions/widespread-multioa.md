## blurb
Osteoarthritis is the most common reason for aching in several joints as we get older, often the knees, hips, hands and the base of the thumb, and sometimes the neck and low back. The joint surfaces and the tissues around them change and adapt over time; it is a normal part of how joints age rather than "wear and tear", and many people with these changes have little or no pain. Staying active and keeping the muscles around the joints strong is the treatment with the best evidence.

## noticed
- Aching in several joints that is worse the more you use them and eases with rest
- Brief stiffness after sitting or first thing in the morning, usually easing within half an hour
- Knobbly finger joints, or a knee or hip that creaks
- Good and bad days, often with flares after a busier spell

## homeCare
- Keep active every day: walking, cycling or a warm pool are kind to sore joints
- Strengthen the muscles around the sore joints a little at a time; it reduces pain
- Spread heavier jobs across the week rather than doing them all at once
- Warmth before activity and supportive footwear help many people

## seePhysioIf
- The aching is limiting walking, sleep, work or the things you enjoy: a physiotherapy assessment can give you a plan for exercise and load
- Morning stiffness lasting more than an hour, or joints that look swollen or feel hot: see your doctor (possible inflammatory arthritis)
- A joint that is suddenly very swollen, hot and too painful to touch, especially with a fever: see a doctor the same day
- Pain that wakes you and does not change with position, or unexplained weight loss: see your doctor within days

## clinicNotes
- Clinical diagnosis without imaging (NICE NG226 2022): 45 or over, activity-related joint pain, and no morning stiffness or stiffness of 30 minutes or less.
- Hand OA: Heberden's and Bouchard's nodes, thumb base; consider erosive OA and inflammatory arthritis when soft swelling or long morning stiffness.
- Management (NICE NG226; OARSI 2019; the site's OA foundation, content/reference/osteoarthritis.md): education, therapeutic exercise (strengthening plus general aerobic), weight management where relevant, walking aids and supports as needed; manual therapy only with exercise.
- Look-alikes: persistent widespread pain (widespread-wsp.md: non-anatomical, fatigue and sleep, light-touch tenderness; both can coexist), inflammatory arthritis and PMR (the wsInflam card), gout and pseudogout (hot joint), hypermobility in younger people.
- Sources: NICE NG226 (2022); Bannuru RR et al., OARSI 2019; the AIM Theory Manual 2023 Ch. 2.9 (via content/reference/osteoarthritis.md).
