## blurb
The tibialis posterior tendon holds up the arch from the inner ankle. When overloaded it aches behind the inner ankle bone and, over time, can let the arch flatten — early treatment matters.

## noticed
- Pain along the inner ankle/arch, behind the inner ankle bone
- A flatter arch or 'too many toes' look from behind
- Feet that tire quickly; difficulty or pain with single-leg heel raises

## homeCare
- Supportive footwear; temporary arch support can offload the tendon
- Avoid pushing through long walks that flare it
- Gentle strengthening within comfort, such as pressing the foot inwards against a band, then slow heel raises

## seePhysioIf
- A sudden pop at the inner ankle: see a doctor the same day
- The arch is flattening quickly over weeks, or you cannot rise onto your toes at all on that leg: see us or your doctor within a week, as a foot specialist may be needed
- You have diabetes and the foot is hot, red or swollen, even if it does not hurt much, or its shape is changing: see a doctor the same day and keep weight off it
- Inner-ankle pain with any new arch flattening — early care shortens recovery
- Pain with stairs, hills, or longer standing
- A single-leg heel raise is painful or impossible

## physioHelps
<!-- DRAFT 17 Oct 2026, awaiting Chandra's review -->
- A physiotherapist can check the tendon that supports your arch and how your foot is holding up.
- A graded strengthening programme with arch supports can help ease pain and support the arch.
- You get a plan to build back walking and activity, and to keep the tendon strong.

## clinicNotes
- Terminology: the 2020 consensus renamed adult-acquired flatfoot "progressive collapsing foot deformity" (PCFD), staged flexible (1) or rigid (2), classes A to E (Myerson 2020, Foot Ankle Int). Your protocol already uses "PCFD Stage I-II".
- Examination: single-leg heel raise with heel inversion, too-many-toes sign, navicular drop, FPI-6. Refer rigid or rapidly progressing deformity to orthopaedics.
- Evidence: orthoses plus eccentric and concentric exercise best (Kulig 2009, Phys Ther); Ross 2018 systematic review (BMJ Open SEM). Highly irritable cases (fewer than 3 heel raises) start with isometrics and short-foot exercises (your protocol).
