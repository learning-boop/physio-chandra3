## blurb
This is a stretch or tear of the ligament running along the inner side of your knee, usually caused by a sideways force or an awkward twist. Most heal well with the right exercise and time.

## noticed
- Pain right along the inner side of the knee after a blow or twisting injury
- Tenderness when pressing directly over the inner knee
- Pain on the inner knee when you step sideways, twist, or the knee is pushed inward
- Walking can be uncomfortable at first, even with a mild sprain

## homeCare
- Keep walking and moving the knee within comfort; ice can settle early pain and swelling
- Ease off activities that push the knee inward or twist it until it settles
- If the knee feels loose sideways, a physiotherapist can check whether a hinged brace is needed; for milder sprains a brace is optional
- Slowly build up bending, straightening and strengthening as pain allows

## seePhysioIf
- You heard a pop, or the whole knee swelled within a few hours: see a doctor the same day
- You could not take 4 steps straight after the injury, cannot bend the knee to a right angle, or you are 55 or over: an X-ray is needed first, the same day
- Your knee is stuck and will not straighten fully: see a doctor the same day
- The knee feels loose sideways, gives way or buckles: book a physiotherapy assessment within a few days
- Pain or swelling from a milder sprain has not improved within a couple of weeks

## physioHelps
<!-- Approved by Chandra Matla, 17 Oct 2026 (review: content/review/physioHelps-review-2026-10-17.docx) -->
- A physiotherapist can check your knee, test the inner ligament and explain how bad the sprain looks.
- You get brace advice if needed, and exercises to rebuild movement and strength step by step.
- A gradual plan can help you return safely to walking, work and sport.

## clinicNotes
- Knee document form 7 (Theory pp. 493-497; Lower Quadrant Clinical Manual pp. 379-381, 426-427, checked): valgus with the foot planted, or valgus plus lateral rotation (skiing); localised medial swelling; haemarthrosis uncommon in grade I-II (a whole-knee tight swelling suggests the ACL). Table 6-1: valgus stress pain at 30 degrees sens 64-92, spec 57-76; laxity at 30 degrees sens 81-100, spec 39-59. IKDC grades: I no instability; II 5-15 degrees; III over 15. Grade 1: daily activities within about 2 weeks; hinged brace from severe grade 2 (about 6 weeks; Wijdicks 2010); Lundberg and Messner: 87% normal function in strenuous activity at 10 years. Check the ACL (O'Donoghue triad; Battaglia 2009). Post-op grade III: return to play from 20 weeks (Logan 2016).
- Diagnosis (ligament CPG 2017): valgus or rotational force, typically a blow to the outer knee with the foot planted; MCL tenderness reproduces the familiar pain. Valgus stress at 30 degrees: pain sens 78%, spec 67%; laxity sens 91%, spec 49% (CPG; the AIM manual found no psychometric data, p.71). Swain test. MCL with ACL is a common combination: check Lachman and pivot shift; Ottawa knee rule.
- Grading (IKDC, AIM manual p.71): grade I no laxity; grade II 5-15 degrees; grade III over 15 degrees, with laxity in extension. Laxity at 0 degrees suggests a combined cruciate or posteromedial corner injury: orthopaedic referral. Isolated MCL swelling is fairly local; a joint effusion points to ACL, cartilage or kneecap dislocation (p.70). The deep MCL and medial meniscus are often injured together (p.69). After a kneecap dislocation, torn MPFL tenderness overlaps the femoral MCL origin (p.70).
- Management: grade I back to daily activities within about 2 weeks (p.71). Isolated grade I-III injuries are managed without surgery, with outcomes similar to surgery (systematic review of RCTs, Surgeon 2020). Bracing for severe MCL injury (F), usually a hinged brace for grade III; neuromuscular re-education with strengthening (A). The CPG gives no MCL-specific return-to-sport timeline.
- Measures (B): IKDC 2000 or KOOS, Tegner or Marx, hop tests when appropriate, effusion, ROM, quadriceps strength.
- Sources: Logerstedt DS et al., JOSPT 2017;47(11):A1-A47 (knee ligament sprain revision 2017), grades in brackets; AIM Theory Manual 2023 Ch. 2.7 pp.69-71; non-operative vs operative MCL RCT review, Surgeon 2020.
