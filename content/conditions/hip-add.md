## blurb
Pain where the inner-thigh muscles attach to the pubic bone, often building up over weeks in kicking, sprinting or change-of-direction sports, and sometimes after a sudden strain. It is the most common sports groin problem.

## noticed
- Sudden inner-thigh/groin pain during sport
- Pain squeezing the knees together or side-lunging
- Tenderness along the inner thigh
- After a sudden strain, sometimes bruising

## homeCare
- Relative rest from sprinting/kicking early on — keep walking as comfortable
- Early gentle range, then progressive adductor strengthening (for example ball squeezes): the best-supported treatment for long-lasting adductor groin pain
- Return to sport gradually via straight-line running before cutting

## seePhysioIf
- Pain limits walking beyond a few days
- You want a criteria-based return-to-sport plan — re-injury is common without one
- Groin pain keeps recurring each season: an adductor strengthening programme (such as the Copenhagen adduction exercise) cuts groin problems by about 40% in footballers
- A pop with a large bruise or a dent, or you cannot walk: see a doctor the same day
- Groin pain at rest or at night, or pain on hopping: see a doctor (possible bone stress)
- A lump in the groin, testicle pain or swelling, or burning or blood when you pass urine: see a doctor
- Under 18 with a pop while kicking: see a doctor first

## clinicNotes
- Doha agreement (Weir 2015): adductor-related groin pain = adductor tenderness and pain on resisted adduction; usually long-standing overuse (acute strains: thigh-adductor). Measures: the 5-second squeeze test (pain 0-10; reflects sporting hip and groin function) and adductor squeeze strength. Differentials: obturator nerve entrapment, hip-related groin pain, pubic bone stress, inguinal-related pain, hernia.
- Management: progressive adductor strengthening (Holmich, Lancet 1999); prevention: the Copenhagen adduction programme reduced groin problems by 41% in male footballers (Haroy, BJSM 2019). ESSKA-EHPA-ESMA 2024 consensus keeps the Doha entities; sources often coexist.
- Sources: Weir A et al., BJSM 2015 (Doha); Holmich P et al., Lancet 1999; Haroy J et al., BJSM 2019; Holmich P et al., KSSTA 2025 (ESSKA consensus).
