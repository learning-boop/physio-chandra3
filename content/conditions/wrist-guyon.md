## blurb
The ulnar nerve passes through a small tunnel at the base of the palm on the little-finger side. Long spells of pressure there, such as leaning on handlebars when cycling or pressing on a tool handle, can irritate it, giving tingling in the little and ring fingers. Unlike the same nerve pressed at the elbow, the back of the hand usually still feels normal.

## noticed
- Tingling or numbness in the little and ring fingers, on the palm side
- Worse after cycling, or pressing on tool handles or the heel of the hand
- Sometimes a weak or clumsy grip

## homeCare
- Change your hand position often when cycling; padded gloves and a higher handlebar can help
- Use padded or wider tool handles, and avoid pressing on the heel of the hand
- Take short breaks from tasks that press on that spot

## seePhysioIf
- The tingling has not eased after 2 to 3 weeks of changes
- Your grip or finger control is getting weaker
- The back of your hand is numb too: the nerve may be pressed higher up, at the elbow
