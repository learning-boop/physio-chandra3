## blurb
The ulnar nerve passes through a small tunnel at the base of the palm on the little-finger side. Long spells of pressure there, such as leaning on handlebars when cycling or pressing on a tool handle, can irritate it, giving tingling in the little and ring fingers. Unlike the same nerve pressed at the elbow, the back of the hand usually still feels normal.

## noticed
- Tingling or numbness in the little and ring fingers, on the palm side
- Worse after cycling, or pressing on tool handles or the heel of the hand
- Sometimes a weak or clumsy grip

## homeCare
- Change your hand position often when cycling; padded gloves and a higher handlebar can help
- Use padded or wider tool handles, and avoid pressing on the heel of the hand
- Take short breaks from tasks that press on that spot

## seePhysioIf
- The tingling has not eased after 2 to 3 weeks of changes
- Your grip or finger control keeps getting weaker, the muscles between your thumb and fingers look thinner, or the numbness is there all the time: see a doctor, who may arrange nerve tests
- The back of your hand is numb too: the nerve may be pressed higher up, at the elbow
- Pain in the heel of the hand after a fall, or after a club or bat hit the ground: see a doctor the same day, as a small bone there can break
- A finger goes cold, white or blue: see a doctor

## clinicNotes
- Zones: 1 mixed, 2 motor only (deep branch; e.g. hook of hamate fracture), 3 sensory only. Froment's and Wartenberg's signs; dorsal ulnar cutaneous sensation and FDP 4/5 strength are spared at Guyon's and affected at the elbow. A ganglion causes about 30-40% (StatPearls); ultrasound or MRI for a ganglion, CT for the hamate. Differentials: cubital tunnel, C8-T1, thoracic outlet, Pancoast tumour; painless wasting without sensory loss can be motor neurone disease (refer). Hypothenar hammer syndrome (ulnar artery) in people who use the palm as a hammer.
