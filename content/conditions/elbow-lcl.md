## blurb
The ligaments on the outer side of the elbow can be sprained in a fall onto an outstretched hand. They hold the forearm bones snugly against the upper arm bone as the forearm turns. It gives pain and tenderness on the outer elbow after the fall, and sometimes a clunk on the outer side when you push up from a chair. Most sprains settle with a short spell of care and a strengthening programme.

## noticed
- Pain and tenderness on the outer elbow since a fall
- Pain or a clunk on the outer side when you push up from a chair or lean on your hand
- Turning the palm up feels worse; palm down feels easier

## homeCare
- Push up from a chair or bed with your palm facing down, or use the other arm
- Avoid push-ups, planks and leaning on a straight arm for now
- Keep bending and straightening the elbow gently every day
- Ice wrapped in a cloth for 10 to 15 minutes at a time in the first few days

## seePhysioIf
- Since the fall, the elbow will not fully straighten, or the bone at the point or outer side is very sore to press: see a doctor the same day for an X-ray
- The elbow locks and will not move, or you cannot turn your palm up or down at all: see a doctor the same day
- Numbness, tingling or weakness in the hand or fingers: see a doctor the same day
- The elbow clunks or feels like it could slip out when you push up or lean on it: please book promptly, as this pattern needs a specific plan, and a surgeon's opinion if it keeps happening
- Pain is not easing after 2 weeks
- It is not better after 4 to 6 weeks: see your doctor

## physioHelps

- A physiotherapist can check the outer elbow ligaments and explain in plain words what is going on.
- Strengthening for the forearm and elbow muscles aims to help support the outer side of the joint.
- A step-by-step return to pushing and leaning on the arm, at a pace your elbow can manage.

## clinicNotes
- Form 11 (19 Oct 2026): East Riding Elbow Rule (Arundel 2014) and the elbow extension test before loading; palpate the LCL/LUCL; varus stress at 0 and 30 degrees; radial head stability; brace in pronation if lax. Also after lateral elbow surgery or repeated steroid injections for lateral elbow pain (O'Driscoll 2000; clinic note only, not patient text). Mild 3-6 weeks, more significant 2-3 months.
- Posterolateral rotatory instability (form 12, folded here; the 6 Oct cross-check N2 kept it to clinic notes): posterolateral rotatory drawer, lateral pivot shift (apprehension; awake Sn about 38%), chair stand-up and push-up signs (one and two arms), tabletop relocation test (O'Driscoll 1991); surgical opinion if clunking persists after rehabilitation (Anakwenze 2014).
- Differentials: radial head fracture (I6), radiocapitellar injury, plica, PRUJ, tennis elbow (gradual, gripping). Under 16: fracture or growth plate injury first.
