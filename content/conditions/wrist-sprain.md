## blurb
A sprain is an over-stretch of the ligaments that hold the small wrist bones together, usually from a fall onto the hand or a sudden twist. The bones stay in place, but the wrist is sore, swollen and weak while it heals. Many sprains settle within a few weeks; the important first step is making sure no bone is broken.

## noticed
- Pain that started with a fall onto the hand or a twist
- A general ache with swelling, sometimes bruising, and a weaker grip
- Sore to push up through the hand or to bend the wrist fully

## homeCare
- For the first 2 to 3 days, support the wrist in a splint and use ice wrapped in a cloth for 10 to 15 minutes at a time
- Keep the fingers, elbow and shoulder moving, and start gentle wrist movement within comfort
- Avoid pushing up through the hand and heavy lifting until it has been checked

## seePhysioIf
- Pain in the hollow at the base of the thumb, the wrist looks a different shape, it is very swollen or sore over the bone, or you cannot use the hand: see a doctor the same day for an X-ray
- It clicks, clunks or gives way when you push up or grip: see your doctor for an X-ray, as a torn ligament between the wrist bones is easy to miss
- It is painful and swollen: book in the first week, so recovery can start
- It is not clearly better after 2 to 3 weeks: see us or your doctor, as an X-ray or scan may be needed

- A physiotherapist can check the injury and whether anything needs an X-ray.
- A short time in a splint, then gentle movement and grip strengthening, can help it settle.
- You get a step-by-step return to weight-bearing, sport and work.

## clinicNotes
- Diagnosis by exclusion: snuffbox, scaphoid tubercle and axial compression: X-ray (Mallee 2014); Watson scaphoid shift, LT ballottement, DRUJ and TFCC tests to grade; grade I-III; splint, ice, early protected movement, graded strengthening; PRWHE. Carpal "fixation" (the form's look-alike) is a manual-therapy finding, not a card.
- Sources: Kitay A, Wolfe SW, J Hand Surg Am 2012; Mallee WH et al. 2014; Skirven TM et al. 2011; "PhysioChandra WristHand Conditions.docx" form 13 (v1.0, 20 Oct 2026).
