## blurb
The muscles of the pelvic floor attach to the tailbone. When they stay tight or overworked, they can cause a deep ache in the pelvis, back passage or tailbone, often with pain on opening the bowels or during sex. A pelvic health physiotherapist specialises in treating these muscles.

## noticed
- A deep ache inside the pelvis or back passage rather than on the tailbone tip
- Pain with bowel movements, or a feeling of pressure inside
- Sitting often matters less than it does with a bruised tailbone

## homeCare
- Avoid straining: fluids, fibre, and a footstool under your feet on the toilet
- Slow, relaxed breathing into the belly to let the pelvic floor soften
- Heat, and short walks through the day

## seePhysioIf
- It keeps coming back or affects bowel movements or sex
- You would like an assessment with a pelvic health physiotherapist
- You have not yet seen your doctor about bowel or gynaecological causes
