## blurb
A tendon runs in a groove on the back of the wrist, on the little-finger side. Repeated turning of the forearm, or a racquet or golf swing, can irritate it or let it flick in and out of its groove, giving pain and sometimes a visible snap when you turn your palm up and down. In most cases the tendon is not torn, and it often responds well to the right amount of load.

## noticed
- Pain on the back of the wrist, little-finger side
- A snapping or flicking feeling when you turn the palm up and down
- Worse with racquet or golf swings, or repeated twisting

## homeCare
- Ease off repeated forceful turning of the forearm for a while
- Avoid the grip or swing that makes it snap
- Keep the wrist and fingers moving gently within comfort
- Ease off rather than stop: reduce the sore activity for a week or two
- Keep the wrist straight (not bent back or sideways) when gripping and typing

## seePhysioIf
- It snapped out of place suddenly during a swing or a lift, and the wrist is now swollen and painful: see a doctor the same day, as early splinting works best
- It has not settled after 2 to 3 weeks
- The tendon keeps snapping out of place
- You want a plan to return to racquet sports, golf, or tool work
- It is not getting better after 4 to 6 weeks: see your doctor

## physioHelps
<!-- Approved by Chandra Matla, 17 Oct 2026 (review: content/review/physioHelps-review-2026-10-17.docx) -->
- A physiotherapist can check the tendon on the little-finger side and explain what is going on.
- Easing forceful twisting, and sometimes a splint, can help the tendon settle before gradual strengthening.
- You get advice on your grip or swing, and a step-by-step plan back to sport.

## clinicNotes
- ECU synergy test (resisted thumb abduction with the wrist neutral and the forearm supinated); dynamic ultrasound for subluxation. Acute subluxation under 3 weeks: above-elbow cast or Muenster splint in pronation and slight radial deviation; chronic: refer. Differentials: TFCC tear, ulnar impaction, DRUJ problems (PMC10167632).
- Form 3 (20 Oct 2026): resisted ulnar deviation and extension (ECU); FCU: tenderness proximal to the pisiform, pain on resisted flexion with ulnar deviation, differential pisotriquetral OA; graded loading from isometrics to resistance through range; splint if irritable; PRWHE (Campbell 2013; Cook and Purdam 2009).
