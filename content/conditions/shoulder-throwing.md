## blurb
In people who throw, serve, bowl or swim a lot, the arm is taken up and back again and again. Over time this can irritate the back of the rotator cuff and the rim of the socket deep inside the shoulder, and the back of the shoulder often becomes tight, so the arm turns inward less than the other side. It comes from overuse in one position, not wear and tear. Easing the throwing load while training the shoulder may help most people get back to sport.

## noticed
- A deep ache at the back of, or inside, the shoulder when the arm is up and back, as in the wind-up of a throw or serve
- Tightness at the back of the shoulder after throwing; reaching across your body or behind your back feels tight
- Your throw or serve may feel slower or less accurate; everyday lifting is often fine

## homeCare
- Cut back throwing or serving for now (about half for two weeks), and do not throw through pain
- Gently stretch the back of the shoulder (cross-body stretch) within comfort, once or twice a day
- Warm up the shoulder and upper back before every session
- Build throwing back up gradually

## seePhysioIf
- It is still out of place or looks a different shape: go to the emergency department
- Weakness or numbness spreading down the arm: see a doctor
- The shoulder is hot, red or swollen: see a doctor the same day; with a fever or feeling unwell, go to the emergency department now
- A new lump, weight loss you cannot explain, or night pain that no position eases: see a doctor
- Pain on the inside of the elbow when throwing
- Throwing or serving is painful, or your speed or accuracy has dropped
- It is not improving after 4 to 6 weeks of easing the load

## physioHelps

- A physiotherapist can check how your shoulder turns and moves, and explain in plain words what may be going on.
- Stretching the back of the shoulder and strengthening the rotator cuff and shoulder-blade muscles can help ease pain.
- You get a plan for throwing load and a graded return to throwing, with your coach where useful.

## clinicNotes
- Exam: posterior internal impingement test (abduction and external rotation, relief on relocation); IR and ER at 90° abduction with the scapula stabilised (GIRD over 18-20°, or a total arc deficit over 5°; allow for humeral retroversion); horizontal adduction (Tyler 2000); posterior glide end-feel; obligate anterior-superior translation; scapular dyskinesis and the SAT as treatment targets (Kibler 2013); ER/IR strength ratio; labral tests (crank, O'Brien's, dynamic labral shear); throwing workload. GIRD on its own is not an injury (form 18); medial elbow pain with throwing: the elbow's UCL card.
- Spectrum: GIRD and the posterosuperior peel-back SLAP lesion (Burkhart 2003); a thrower who clicks or catches: shoulder-labral.
- Sources: Drakos MC et al. 2009; Mihata T et al. 2012; Manske R et al. 2013; Borstad JD, Lewis J 2015; Burkhart SS et al., Arthroscopy 2003; "PhysioChandra Shoulder Conditions.docx" forms 8, 18 and 19 (v1.0, 19 Oct 2026).
