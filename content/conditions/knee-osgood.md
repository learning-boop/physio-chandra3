## blurb
This is irritation of the growing bone just below the kneecap (Osgood-Schlatter) or at the lower tip of the kneecap (Sinding-Larsen-Johansson), common in active children and teenagers going through a growth spurt. Both knees are often affected. Most young people improve over several months; the bump can stay but is usually painless.

## noticed
- Tenderness, and sometimes a small bony bump, just below the kneecap or at its lower tip
- Pain that's worse during or after running, jumping, kneeling, stairs or squatting
- Usually seen in children or teenagers who play a lot of sport

## homeCare
- Cut back on jumping and running for a few weeks to let it settle
- Then build back up step by step, keeping pain low (about 2 out of 10) during activity and the next morning
- Thigh and hip strengthening exercises help
- Icing the tender spot after activity is optional if it feels good

## seePhysioIf
- Sudden severe pain below the kneecap after a jump or sprint, and you cannot walk or straighten the knee: see a doctor the same day
- A limp, or pain when the hip moves: see a doctor the same day
- Pain at night or at rest, redness, or feeling unwell: see a doctor
- The pain isn't settling after a few weeks of reduced activity
- It's getting in the way of sport or everyday activity

## clinicNotes
- Osgood-Schlatter: traction apophysitis of the tibial tuberosity (tender tuberosity, often a bump); Sinding-Larsen-Johansson: the same at the inferior patellar pole. An x-ray can confirm either (AIM manual pp.29, 31). Differential: patellar tendinopathy (older), patellofemoral pain, and the hip: SUFE and Perthes can present as knee pain (in a review of 102 SUFE cases, 2 had first been called Osgood-Schlatter); tibial tubercle avulsion fracture (sudden pain after jumping, boys 12-17: same-day doctor); bone tumour (night or rest pain: kf-tumour).
- Management (Rathleff 2020, Orthop J Sports Med): 4 weeks of reduced sport, then an 11-step activity ladder advanced only with pain at or below 2/10 during and the morning after, plus knee and hip strengthening; 80% reported success at 12 weeks and 90% at 12 months, though only 69% were back in sport at 12 months. Icing and stretching have no trial support. SOGOOD RCT protocol (2024) under way.
- Sources: Rathleff MS et al., Orthop J Sports Med 2020; SOGOOD protocol 2024 (PMC11032598); SUFE diagnostic delay review (PMC2504123); Willy RW et al., JOSPT 2019 (differential); AIM Theory Manual 2023 Ch. 2.7.
