## blurb
A neck that suddenly locks, often on waking or after a small awkward movement, so the head is held to one side and turning is very sore. It is common, it is not a sign of damage, and it usually eases within a few days to a couple of weeks with gentle movement.

## noticed
- Woke up with the neck stuck, or it locked after a small awkward movement
- The head held a little to one side, with sharp pain on trying to turn one way
- Sore muscles on one side of the neck, without pain or tingling down the arm

## homeCare
- Keep the neck moving gently within comfort, little and often; avoid a collar
- Heat on the sore side can ease the muscles
- Rest the neck on a supportive pillow at night, and change position often during the day
- Most people feel much freer within a few days

## seePhysioIf
- A child or teenager with a stiff, twisted neck and a fever, a sore throat, or after a recent throat or ear operation: see a doctor the same day
- It started after a fall, an accident or a knock: see a doctor first
- A fever, a severe headache, or feeling very unwell with the stiff neck: get urgent medical care
- It is not easing after a few days, or keeps coming back: a physiotherapy assessment can help free it up and stop it recurring

## clinicNotes
- Acute wry neck: sudden painful restriction, usually on waking, often thought to be a locked facet (zygapophyseal) joint or acute disc irritation; JOSPT 2017 acute neck pain with mobility deficits (thoracic manipulation and cervical mobilisation, active ROM, advice to stay active).
- In children: exclude Grisel's syndrome (atlantoaxial rotatory subluxation after a throat or ENT infection or surgery), retropharyngeal abscess, trauma, and, rarely, a posterior fossa tumour (persistent tilt, headache, vomiting): doctor first. The site does not see children under 5.
- Safety: Canadian C-spine rule after any trauma; IFOMPT cervical framework before manual therapy; meningitis signs (fever, headache, photophobia).
- Sources: JOSPT Neck Pain CPG 2017 (Blanpied et al.); Cervical Clinic Manual 2026; IFOMPT cervical framework 2020.
