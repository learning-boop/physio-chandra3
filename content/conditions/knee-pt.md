## blurb
The tendon just below the kneecap can become sensitive when jumping, landing, or sprinting loads rise faster than the tendon adapts. Classic in jumping and court sports.

## noticed
- Pointable pain on the tendon just below the kneecap
- Worse with jumping, landing, stairs, or sprinting
- Often warms up during activity, then aches after, often more the next day

## homeCare
- Manage the spike: reduce jump and sprint volume, keep strength work
- Slow, held thigh-muscle exercises (such as a wall sit) may ease pain for some people
- Some discomfort (about 3 out of 10) during exercise is fine if it has settled by the next morning
- Keep squats and lunges fairly shallow at first, going deeper only once pain has eased
- Avoid complete rest: tendons adapt to load, not to rest

## seePhysioIf
- A sudden snap at the front of the knee, after which you cannot straighten the knee or lift the leg: see a doctor the same day
- Pain persists or returns each session
- You want a staged tendon-loading program, the core of good care
- It's affecting performance or daily stairs

## clinicNotes
- Diagnosis (Malliaras 2015): pain localised to the inferior pole of the patella, load-dependent, rising with the energy-storage demand of the tendon (jumping, landing, change of direction); first episode usually between 15 and 30 (AIM manual p.28); pain often worse 24 hours later (p.29). Decline single-leg squat at 25 degrees. Palpation is of low value (sens 37-56%, spec 47-83%). Imaging does not confirm the diagnosis: tendon changes are common without pain.
- Measure: VISA-P (minimal clinically important change about 13 points).
- Management: progressive tendon loading (Breda 2021: isometric, then isotonic, then energy-storage, then sport-specific, which beat eccentric training at 24 weeks), or heavy slow resistance; pain-monitoring model (about 3/10, settled by next morning). Isometrics (for example 5 x 45 s at 60 degrees, 70-80% MVC) may ease pain for some, though later trials did not reproduce reliable immediate relief (Clifford 2020). Increase depth only once pain has reduced substantially (p.33). Shockwave is no better than exercise; deep friction is not supported; surgery is a last resort.
- Differentials: patellofemoral pain, fat pad irritation, Sinding-Larsen-Johansson or Osgood-Schlatter in teenagers (x-ray can confirm), patellar tendon rupture (sudden snap, cannot straight-leg raise: same-day doctor).
- Sources: Malliaras P et al., JOSPT 2015;45(11):887-898; Breda SJ et al., Br J Sports Med 2021; AIM Theory Manual 2023 Ch. 2.7 pp.28-33.
