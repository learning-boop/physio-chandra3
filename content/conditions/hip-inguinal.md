## blurb
Just above the groin crease is a weaker area of the tummy wall, where the inguinal canal runs. In sports with a lot of kicking, sprinting, and twisting, this area can become painful, especially with coughing, sneezing, or sit-ups. A doctor should check for a hernia if there is a lump.

## noticed
- Pain just above the groin crease
- Worse with coughing, sneezing, sit-ups, or sprinting
- No visible lump (a lump needs a doctor to check for a hernia)
- Sometimes called a "sports hernia", but it is not a true hernia: there is no lump, and nothing has come through the tummy wall

## homeCare
- Ease off sprinting, kicking, and sit-ups for a while
- Keep active with straight-line walking or cycling if comfortable
- Build core and hip strength back gradually

## seePhysioIf
- It keeps limiting your sport after 2 to 3 weeks
- It keeps coming back each season
- You notice a lump in or just below the groin crease: see a doctor first
- A painful groin lump that will not go back in, especially with feeling sick or vomiting: go to the emergency department now
- Pain or swelling in a testicle: see a doctor; sudden severe testicle pain: go to the emergency department now
- Doha 2015 inguinal-related groin pain: pain in the inguinal canal region, tenderness there, no palpable hernia, pain on resisted abdominal testing, Valsalva, cough or sneeze. "Sports hernia" is a misnomer (British Hernia Society inguinal disruption consensus; ESSKA 2024). Femoral hernia (a lump just below the crease) is more common in women and more likely to become trapped. Ultrasound or a surgical review when a hernia is suspected; surgery is an option after failed rehabilitation.
