## blurb
The chewing muscles in the cheek and temple can become sore and tight, often with clenching or grinding, stress, or long spells of chewing and talking. The ache can spread to the temples as a headache, to the ear, or even to the teeth. It is the most common jaw problem and responds well to self-care and physiotherapy.

## noticed
- An ache in the cheek or temple, often on both sides
- Worse on waking (night clenching) or building up through the day
- Headaches at the temples, or teeth that feel sore with nothing found by the dentist

## homeCare
- Notice and stop daytime clenching: lips together, teeth apart
- Soft food for a while; stop gum chewing
- Warm packs and gentle massage of the cheek and temple muscles
- Ask your dentist about a night guard if you grind at night; time to wind down before bed can help

## seePhysioIf
- It lasts more than a few weeks or keeps coming back
- Headaches or ear symptoms come with it
- You want a plan combining jaw exercises, posture and relaxation
