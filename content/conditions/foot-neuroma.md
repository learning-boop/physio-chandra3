## blurb
A small nerve between the toes becomes irritated and thickened, giving burning, tingling or shooting pain into two toes, or a feeling of a pebble or a click under the ball of the foot, worse in tight shoes.

## noticed
- Burning or stabbing pain in the ball of the foot, often between the 3rd and 4th toes, sometimes the 2nd and 3rd
- 'Marble or pebble under the foot' feeling
- Toe tingling or numbness
- Worse in tight or high-heeled shoes; eases barefoot

## homeCare
- Switch to shoes with a wide toe box and lower heel
- A small metatarsal pad (just behind the ball of the foot) often helps
- Reduce time in the provoking footwear
- Gentle calf stretches and spreading the toes apart; avoid pushing through sharp forefoot pain

## seePhysioIf
- Numbness spreads beyond two toes or into both feet, or you have diabetes and your toes are numb: see your doctor
- The front of the foot is suddenly hot, red and very painful, or you feel unwell or feverish: see a doctor the same day
- Ball-of-foot pain or the pebble feeling persists after 2 to 3 weeks of footwear changes
- Toe tingling or numbness continues
- You want gait and footwear assessment before considering injections

## clinicNotes
- Tests (Pitcher 2024, Foot Ankle Orthop, systematic review): thumb-index web-space squeeze sensitivity 0.96, LR- 0.04 (a negative test rules out); a click reported by the patient specificity 0.96, LR+ 13.1; Mulder's sign sensitivity 29-94%, LR+ 2.19; toe-tip sensory deficit specificity 100%, sensitivity 49%; plain web-space tenderness weak. Burning alone is weak (specificity 48%).
- Imaging: ultrasound about as accurate as MRI (Bignotti 2015). Treatment: wide toe box, metatarsal pad behind the pain; Cochrane 2024 (Matthews BG et al., CD014687): ultrasound-guided corticosteroid with local anaesthetic probably reduces pain (moderate certainty; a physician decision); little evidence for other non-surgical treatments.
- Differentials: plantar plate injury (drawer test), intermetatarsal bursitis, metatarsal stress fracture, Freiberg's, peripheral neuropathy, L5/S1 nerve root.
- Your neuroma protocol: the Cochrane review's first author is Matthews BG (not Thomson); no source found for the "60-85% achieve meaningful improvement" figure.
