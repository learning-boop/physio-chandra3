## blurb
Small skin nerves come out from the spine and pass through the back muscles to the skin beside the shoulder blade. Where one is squeezed on its way through, it can cause a patch of itching, burning, tingling or numbness, often with a slightly darker patch of skin from scratching. It is harmless, though it can be very annoying, and gentle movement of the upper back and shoulder blade often helps.

## noticed
- A patch beside or just below one shoulder blade that itches, burns, tingles or feels numb
- No rash, though the skin may look a little darker from rubbing or scratching
- Sometimes worse after sitting slumped, carrying a bag on that side, or with stress
- Pressing next to the spine at that level can bring it on

## homeCare
- Keep the upper back and shoulder blade moving: gentle arching, rotation and shoulder blade squeezes through the day
- Change position often when sitting; avoid long slumped spells
- A cool cloth or a moisturiser can ease the itch; try not to scratch the skin raw
- Carry bags on both shoulders, or switch sides

## seePhysioIf
- A rash, blisters or a red strip on the skin: see your doctor, as it could be shingles
- The skin patch changes colour, bleeds, or a mole or lump appears: see your doctor
- Weakness or numbness in both legs, unsteady walking, or bladder changes: go to an emergency department
- The itch or burning is affecting sleep, or has not eased with movement after a few weeks: we can assess the upper back and the nerve

## clinicNotes
- Cutaneous branches of the dorsal rami T2-T6 entrapped as they pierce multifidus and the fascia at a right angle; often with upper thoracic stiffness or degenerative change at the same levels. Hyperpigmentation from scratching is common.
- Neurodynamics (Butler): tender spots or nodules where the nerves become cutaneous, more tender in slump long sitting and less with the neck extended (structural differentiation); more reactive laterally along the lateral branch. Upper thoracic segments: slump long sitting with cervical lateral flexion (symptoms on the convex side), PA and wedge mobilisation at the level.
- Management: thoracic mobility and exercise, local soft tissue and nerve techniques (Butler), postural load; topical treatment, nerve blocks or other medical care via the GP or dermatologist for persistent itch.
- Look-alikes: shingles and post-herpetic neuralgia (rash, dermatomal), dermatological itch (eczema, lichen), thoracic radiculopathy (upperback-nerveroot.md: band around the chest), MEN2A (familial notalgia, rare; GP), spinal cord lesions (bilateral or long-tract signs).
- Sources: Butler DS, NOI neurodynamic techniques workbook (spine, cord and meninges: notalgia paraesthetica techniques); Ellis C, Dermatol Ther 2013 (notalgia paraesthetica review).
