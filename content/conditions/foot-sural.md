## blurb
A thin nerve runs down the back of the calf, beside the heel cord and behind the outer ankle bone, to the outer edge of the foot. After an ankle sprain, with a tight boot or shoe collar, or alongside heel cord or outer ankle tendon problems, it can become irritated, giving burning, tingling or numbness along the outer heel and the outer edge of the foot. It usually settles with less pressure and gentle movement.

## noticed
- Burning, tingling or numbness along the outer heel and the outer edge of the foot
- A tender spot beside the heel cord or just behind the outer ankle bone
- Worse with boots or shoe collars that press there, or after a sprain
- Sometimes with an ache in the outer ankle that the tendons alone do not explain

## homeCare
- Avoid boots or shoe collars that press behind the outer ankle; pad or loosen them
- Keep the ankle moving gently; avoid holding strong calf stretches that bring on the tingling
- After a sprain, keep up your balance and strength exercises

## seePhysioIf
- The tingling or numbness has not eased after a few weeks
- It keeps coming back with each ankle sprain
- Pain also goes down the back of the leg from the back or buttock: the back may be the source
- Burning or numbness in both feet, like socks: see your doctor, as a general nerve condition can cause this
- A new lump along the nerve, or numbness after an ankle operation or a cut: see your doctor

## clinicNotes
- Sural nerve: lateral to the Achilles, then behind and below the lateral malleolus to the lateral border of the foot (lateral dorsal cutaneous nerve); can anastomose with the superficial fibular nerve (Shacklock). Traction in inversion sprains; compression by boots, scar, ganglia, fractures of the base of the 5th metatarsal, peroneal tendon pathology, and after Achilles or lateral ankle surgery.
- Neurodynamics: sural test DF/IN then SLR (normal response: pulling in the lateral ankle, sometimes posterolateral calf; SLR about 30-60 degrees); sensitise with hip adduction and medial rotation, contralateral lateral flexion, slump. Palpate lateral to the Achilles with counter-pressure medially; compare sides. Butler: nerve massage for swelling around the lateral Achilles.
- Management: offload the interface (footwear, scar mobility), sural sliders (DF/IN with knee extension in hip flexion; neck extension as the slider), then tensioners; address the recurrent sprain (balance, strength).
- Look-alikes: peroneal tendinopathy or subluxation (ankle-peroneal.md; snapping, pain on resisted eversion), cuboid syndrome, chronic ankle instability, S1 radiculopathy (back, sole, Achilles reflex), fifth metatarsal fracture (Ottawa rules), insertional Achilles tendinopathy.
- Sources: Butler DS, NOI neurodynamic techniques workbook (sural nerve); Shacklock NDS lower-quarter manual 2017-18 (sural neurodynamic test, palpation); Basson A et al., JOSPT 2017.
