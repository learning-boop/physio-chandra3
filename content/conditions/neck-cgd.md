## blurb
A feeling of being off-balance or unsteady that may come from the neck. Your brain uses signals from the joints and muscles of the upper neck, together with your eyes and inner ear, to know where your head is. When the neck is sore, stiff or has been injured, those signals can become less accurate, and the brain may feel "off". It is not dangerous in itself, and it often eases as the neck settles and moves better. Because dizziness has several possible causes, these are checked first, so you get the right care; if your family doctor has not yet looked at your dizziness, see them alongside physiotherapy.

## noticed
- Feeling unsteady, light-headed, "floaty" or off-balance, rather than the room spinning
- It started with neck pain or stiffness: after a whiplash injury or concussion, or gradually alongside long-standing neck pain
- Turning your head or holding it in one position (desk work, driving, looking up at a shelf) brings it on
- Episodes that last minutes to hours, often with neck stiffness and a headache at the back of the head
- It follows the neck: better when the neck is better, worse when neck pain flares

## homeCare
- Change position every 30 to 45 minutes during desk work or driving, and set screens at eye level
- Keep the neck moving gently, within comfort
- If you feel dizzy, stop, sit down, and fix your eyes on a still point
- Avoid driving, ladders or heights while you feel dizzy

## seePhysioIf
- You feel off-balance or dizzy along with neck pain or stiffness, especially after a neck injury: an assessment can help work out whether your neck is involved, and we will involve your doctor if another cause needs checking
- You would like a plan to retrain neck position sense, eye and head coordination, and balance
- The dizziness keeps coming back with desk work, screens or driving

## clinicNotes
- Diagnosis of exclusion (Reiley 2017): no single confirming test. Care route: physio-led after screening; if other causes of dizziness have not been checked, family doctor alongside.
- Screen first: Canadian C-spine rule (if trauma); IFOMPT cervical framework for vascular pathology; neuro screen (5 Ds, 3 Ns).
- Vestibular: Dix-Hallpike, supine roll, head impulse, spontaneous and gaze-evoked nystagmus, head-neck differentiation (body-on-head rotation).
- Cervical: AROM, upper-cervical PAIVMs (C0-C3), flexion-rotation test, palpation reproducing dizziness.
- Sensorimotor: joint position error (over 4.5 degrees abnormal), smooth pursuit neck torsion test, balance (Romberg, tandem, single-leg).
- Management: education; upper-cervical mobilisation and SNAGs after screening (low to very low certainty, Carrasco-Uribarren 2025; Reid 2015); position-sense and eye-head retraining, balance; neck strength and endurance; ergonomics; add vestibular rehab if an inner-ear problem coexists. No thrust manipulation without full vascular screening (IFOMPT 2023).
- Outcomes: Dizziness Handicap Inventory and Neck Disability Index.
