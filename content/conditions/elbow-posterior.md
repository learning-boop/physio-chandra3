## blurb
At the point of the elbow there is a small fluid sac (bursa) that cushions the bone. Leaning on the elbow, or a knock, can irritate the bursa so that it fills with fluid into a soft swelling. It does not affect the joint itself, and the elbow still bends and straightens. A red, hot swelling or one with a cut over it needs a doctor the same day, even without a fever, as it may be infected.

## noticed
- A soft, sometimes squashy swelling at the point of the elbow
- Tender to lean on
- The elbow still bends and straightens fully

## homeCare
- Avoid leaning on the elbow; pad desks and armrests
- Protect it from knocks while it settles
- A soft elbow pad or a gentle compression sleeve may help the swelling settle
- A cold pack wrapped in a cloth for 10 to 15 minutes, a few times a day

## seePhysioIf
- It becomes hot, red or very tender, there is a cut or graze over it, the redness is spreading, or you feel unwell: see a doctor the same day; with a fever or feeling very unwell, go to the emergency department now
- The swelling or pain has not settled after 2 to 3 weeks
- It keeps coming back
- The swelling came with no knock or leaning, or other joints are swollen too: see your doctor (gout or arthritis can cause it)
- It is not getting better after 4 to 6 weeks: see your doctor

## physioHelps

- A physiotherapist can check the swelling, make sure the joint itself is not involved, and explain it simply.
- Padding, gentle compression and avoiding pressure can help the swelling settle.
- You get a plan for getting back to leaning, work tasks and sport without stirring it up again.

## clinicNotes
- Olecranon bursitis: conservative care first (protect, pad, avoid leaning); aspiration did not raise infection risk in aseptic bursitis (Kaur 2023 systematic review). Septic bursitis: mostly S. aureus, fever in only about 4 in 10 (reported figures); aspiration and culture are physician decisions. Also consider gout or tophi and RA nodules ("sudden pain and swelling with no injury").
- Form 21 (Blackwell 2014; ACOEM 2013): fluctuant swelling over the olecranon with full range of movement (diffuse swelling with lost movement is a joint effusion); check skin integrity, warmth, erythema and systemic signs; compression, padding, ice, avoid pressure; aspiration, culture and antibiotics are physician decisions; recurrent cases: gout, RA. The form's anti-inflammatory line is not in patient text.
