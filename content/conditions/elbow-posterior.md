## blurb
At the point of the elbow there is a small fluid sac (bursa) that cushions the bone, and the triceps tendon attaches just above it. Leaning on the elbow, or a knock, can irritate the bursa so that it fills with fluid into a soft swelling; heavy pushing can overload the tendon. A red, hot swelling or one with a cut over it needs a doctor the same day, even without a fever, as it may be infected.

## noticed
- A soft, sometimes squashy swelling at the point of the elbow
- Tender to lean on
- Pain at the back of the elbow with pushing or straightening against resistance

## homeCare
- Avoid leaning on the elbow; pad desks and armrests
- Protect it from knocks while it settles
- Ease off heavy pushing, push-ups, or dips for a while

## seePhysioIf
- It becomes hot, red or very tender, there is a cut or graze over it, the redness is spreading, or you feel unwell: see a doctor the same day
- The swelling or pain has not settled after 2 to 3 weeks
- It keeps coming back
- Pushing or straightening the arm is getting weaker
- You felt a pop at the back of the elbow after a fall or a forced push, and straightening the arm is now weak: see a doctor the same day

## clinicNotes
- Olecranon bursitis: conservative care first (protect, pad, avoid leaning); aspiration did not raise infection risk in aseptic bursitis (Kaur 2023 systematic review). Septic bursitis: mostly S. aureus, fever in only about 4 in 10 (reported figures); aspiration and culture are physician decisions. Also consider gout or tophi and RA nodules ("sudden pain and swelling with no injury").
- Triceps tendinopathy: pain with resisted extension and pushing; distal triceps rupture (a pop after a fall on the outstretched hand or a forced push, weak extension, a palpable gap) needs early surgical opinion. Differentials: posteromedial impingement or valgus extension overload in throwers, snapping triceps or ulnar nerve subluxation.
