## blurb
Two small bones sit under the big toe joint, inside the tendon that bends the toe. Running, jumping, dancing, and high heels load them heavily, and they can become sore under the joint, especially pushing off. Pain that keeps getting worse, or hurts at night, needs checking for a stress fracture.

## noticed
- Pain under the big toe joint, on the ball of the foot
- Worse pushing off, rising on the toes, or in high heels
- Common in runners and dancers

## homeCare
- Ease off running, jumping, and high heels for a while
- A cushioned pad with a cut-out under the joint can offload it
- Stiffer-soled shoes reduce the bend at the toe

## seePhysioIf
- It has not settled after 2 weeks of offloading
- The pain is getting worse, one spot under the joint hurts with every step or hop, or it aches at night: see a doctor first (possible stress fracture)
- You want a plan to return to running or dance
- You have diabetes and a callus, blister or sore under the big toe joint: see your doctor or diabetes foot clinic within a day or two

## clinicNotes
- Great toe sesamoid stress fracture is a high-risk bone stress injury (AIM manual Ch. 2.8, Table 2; Warden 2014): higher non-union and surgery rates. MRI separates sesamoiditis, stress fracture and avascular necrosis (adolescents and young women). Ask about a turf-toe injury (foot injury screen I7); bipartite sesamoids are common.
