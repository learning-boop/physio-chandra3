## blurb
Just above the ankle, the two bones of the lower leg are held together by strong ligaments. A twist with the foot planted and turning outwards, common in football and rugby, can sprain them. The pain sits higher than an ordinary sprain, at the front between the two bones, and it often takes longer to recover. Some need a surgeon's opinion, so it is worth having it assessed.

## noticed
- Pain at the front of the ankle, just above the joint, between the two leg bones
- Worse pushing off, twisting, or going up on the toes
- Slower to settle than an ordinary rolled ankle

## homeCare
- Protect it early: walk only as comfort allows, with a boot or crutches if needed
- Avoid twisting and pushing off hard while it settles
- Keep the rest of the leg moving and strong

## seePhysioIf
- Have it assessed within the first week, especially if walking is hard or the pain is climbing
- It is limiting walking or sport
- You need a graded plan back to twisting sport
- You could not take 4 steps straight after the injury, or the bone is tender to press: see a doctor for an X-ray first
- The bone on the outside of your leg is tender to press just below the knee: see a doctor the same day for an X-ray
- Your calf becomes swollen, warm or tender, especially in a brace, boot or cast: see a doctor the same day; if you are also short of breath or have chest pain, call 911

## clinicNotes
- JOSPT lateral ankle sprain CPG 2021: syndesmosis injury is a key differential and copathology of acute and persisting sprains; apply the full Ottawa ankle rules first. Imaging (ACR, cited in the CPG): alignment change suggesting syndesmosis injury warrants leg and stress radiographs, MRI or CT; refer with any diastasis concern or failure to settle. (The CPG's "more than 3 weeks of sport" figure refers to severe multi-ligament sprains, not syndesmosis injuries specifically.)
- Tests (Sman 2015, BJSM 49:323; AIM manual p. 527): AITFL (syndesmosis) tenderness most sensitive (92%, LR- 0.28); squeeze test most specific (88%, LR+ 2.15); dorsiflexion-external rotation test sensitivity 71%; unable to hop sensitivity 89%; pain out of proportion specificity 79%. Use a combination, not one test. About 20% of ankle sprains (AIM manual).
- Maisonneuve: palpate the whole fibula after an external rotation injury; proximal fibula tenderness needs a full-length X-ray (injury screen I10).
- Your syndesmosis protocol's recovery percentages are marked "clinical inference": not for the card.
- Source: Martin RL et al., JOSPT 2021; Sman AD et al., BJSM 2015.
