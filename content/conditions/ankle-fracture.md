## blurb
After a broken ankle or foot, whether treated in a cast, a boot or with surgery, a guided programme of movement, strength, balance and walking helps you get back to normal. Stiffness, swelling and a limp are common at first and improve over months.

## noticed
- Stiffness and swelling, worse at the end of the day
- Weakness and less balance on that leg, and a limp
- Difficulty with stairs, slopes or uneven ground

## homeCare
- Follow the fracture clinic's advice on weight bearing and the boot, for as long as you were told
- Move the ankle and toes often, and keep the leg raised when it swells
- Build up walking a little at a time; practise balance near a support

## seePhysioIf
- Your calf becomes swollen, warm or tender, especially in a brace, boot or cast: see a doctor the same day; if you are also short of breath or have chest pain, call 911
- The wound is red, hot or leaking, or you have a fever: see a doctor the same day
- Burning pain, colour or temperature changes, or skin so sensitive that light touch hurts: see your doctor and book physiotherapy now
- You would like a structured movement, strength and balance programme
- Progress has stalled, or you are not back to the activities you want
- The cast or boot feels tighter and more painful, with numbness, tingling, or a cold, pale or blue foot: go to the emergency department now

## physioHelps
<!-- Approved by Chandra Matla, 17 Oct 2026 (review: content/review/physioHelps-review-2026-10-17.docx) -->
- Once your fracture team allows, a physiotherapist can explain what to expect as the bone heals.
- A guided programme of movement, strength, balance and walking can help ease stiffness and a limp.
- You get a step-by-step plan back to work, sport and the things you enjoy.

## clinicNotes
- Thigh, leg, ankle and foot document form 10 (signed v1.0 by Chandra, 26 Oct 2026): Ottawa Ankle Rules (Stiell 1996) (MP 483): ankle X-ray if pain in the malleolar zone plus any of – inability to bear weight both immediately and in the ED (a total of four steps); bone tenderness at the posterior edge of either malleolus; bone tenderness at the inferior tip of either malleolus. Foot X-ray if pain in the midfoot zone plus any of – inability to bear weight immediately and in the ED (four steps); bone tenderness at the base of the 5th metatarsal; bone tenderness at the navicular. Accuracy (MP 483): adults Sn 93%–100%, Sp 49%; children under 15 Sn 80%–98%, Sp 40%–53%. Incidence of ankle or foot fracture with ankle sprain ≈9%–15% (Polzer 2012). Negative rule → fracture unlikely; positive rule → X-ray indicated, does not prove a fracture. Avulsion fractures are fairly common with ankle injuries and also hurt on stability testing; fracture is hard to distinguish from ligament injury on the initial exam (Goost 2014) – use the Ottawa rules for X-ray referral (MP 490). Standard views: AP, lateral and mortise ankle + AP foot (Safran 1999; Theory p. 525). Easily missed fractures (Theory pp. 525–526; Judd 2002): lateral talar process – 2nd most common talus fracture; inversion + DF; point tenderness anterior and inferior to the tip of the lateral malleolus. Posterior talar process – lateral tubercle (extreme PF → compression, inversion → avulsion; falls, kicking; deep tenderness over the posterior talus anterior to the Achilles) and medial tubercle (rare; DF + pronation; pain posterior to the medial malleolus, anterior to the Achilles). Anterior process of calcaneus – ~15% of calcaneal fractures; inversion + PF avulsion at the bifurcate ligament origin, or forced DF compression; tender over the CC joint just distal to the ATFL insertion. Other associated injuries when recovery is slow (Theory pp. 526–527): bone bruise (up to 50% of acute inversion injuries); osteochondral lesions (up to 14%); ATFL – anterior drawer Sn 0.96, Sp 0.84 at 4–6 days; syndesmosis (~20%) – palpation tenderness most sensitive (0.92), squeeze test most specific (0.88) (Delahunt 2018); cuboid subluxation (push-off pain at the cuboid). High-risk bone stress sites overlap acute fracture sites (medial malleolus, talus lateral process, navicular, proximal 5th MT diaphysis – Theory Table 2, p. 555): if there was no single injury, see foot-bone-stress-injury. Once fracture is excluded: lateral or high ankle sprain pathway (see 9a). After fracture treatment: follow the treating team's WB, boot/cast and ROM limits; talar tilt mobilisation for capsular tightness after immobilisation (MP 549).
- Rehabilitation after ankle or foot fracture (cast, boot or ORIF): early weight bearing as permitted, ROM (weight-bearing lunge), strength, balance and gait training; measure LEFS or OMAS, lunge test, single-leg balance, hop tests for sport.
- Safety: DVT or PE (af-dvt), infection or wound problems, CRPS (A9/B9, af-crps), compartment syndrome early after injury, non-union.
- Sources: AIM Theory Manual 2023 Ch. 2.8; CRPS document (2 Oct 2026).
