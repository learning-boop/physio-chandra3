## blurb
In children aged about 8 to 14, the growth plate at the back of the heel bone is still soft, and the calf tendon pulls on it. With a growth spurt and a lot of running and jumping sport, it becomes sore at the back and underside of the heel. It settles with a sensible load, and usually settles fully once growth slows.

## noticed
- Heel pain in a child, at the back or underside of the heel
- Worse during and after running and jumping sport
- Often during a growth spurt, and sometimes a limp after sport

## homeCare
- Reduce, but do not stop, the running and jumping for a while
- Cushioned shoes or a small heel cup can ease it
- Ice after sport, and gentle calf stretches

## seePhysioIf
- It is stopping your child from playing sport
- The pain is there at rest or at night: see a doctor first
- You want a plan to manage sport through the growth spurt
- The heel is swollen, red or warm, your child has a fever, or the pain is in one heel and keeps getting worse: see a doctor first

## clinicNotes
- Peak age: girls about 8 to 12, boys about 10 to 14; often both heels. Positive medial-lateral heel squeeze; X-ray only if atypical (one side, night pain, swelling, pain at rest).
- Evidence: self-limiting; wait-and-see, heel raise and physiotherapy similar (Wiegerinck 2016, J Pediatr Orthop); heel raise or footwear (James 2016, BJSM); James 2013 systematic review.
