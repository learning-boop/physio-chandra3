## blurb
A skin nerve runs down the inside of the knee to the inner shin. It can be irritated by knee surgery, a knock, or pressure, giving a burning, tingling, or numb patch on the inner or front of the knee, or the inner shin. It is a feeling nerve only, so the leg is not weak. Numbness beside a knee scar is common, and the patch usually shrinks over the following year.

## noticed
- A burning, tingling, or numb patch on the inner or front of the knee, or the inner shin
- Often after knee surgery or a knock; after surgery, often beside and below the scar
- No weakness in the leg

## homeCare
- Avoid pressure on the inner knee, such as crossing the legs or tight braces
- Keep the knee moving normally
- Gentle desensitising, such as rubbing the area with different textures

## seePhysioIf
- The numb patch is spreading, or the leg feels weak or gives way: see a doctor soon, as it may come from the back or a larger nerve
- The burning has not eased after a few weeks
- It is limiting sleep or walking

## clinicNotes
- The saphenous nerve is purely sensory (the terminal branch of the femoral nerve). Its infrapatellar branch is commonly cut or stretched in knee surgery: after knee replacement, objective numbness is near universal and subjective numbness 37-55%, usually lateral to and below the scar (Ann R Coll Surg Engl 2013); the numb area shrinks over the first year.
- Other causes: adductor (Hunter's) canal entrapment, varicose vein surgery or vein harvest, pressure from a brace or kneeling. Weakness or spreading numbness points to the femoral nerve or an L3-L4 root: refer.
- Examination: sensory map, Tinel's sign at the medial knee or adductor canal, femoral nerve tension (prone knee bend); strength and reflexes normal in an isolated saphenous lesion.
- Sources: Ann R Coll Surg Engl 2013 (numbness after TKA); AIM Theory Manual 2023 Ch. 2.7 (differential list).
