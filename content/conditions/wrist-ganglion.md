## blurb
A smooth, harmless fluid-filled lump, most often on the back of the wrist, that can enlarge with activity and fluctuate in size. Usually more annoying than harmful. It can sometimes press on a nearby nerve and cause tingling.

## noticed
- A smooth lump you can see or feel, which may change size
- An ache when you lean on your hand with the wrist bent back (push-ups, yoga)
- Sometimes no pain at all

## homeCare
- Reduce sustained weight on the fully bent-back wrist; use fists or an angled support for floor work
- Don't try to burst it (the old book trick): that's not recommended
- Keep an eye on its size: about half go away on their own within a year or two (more in children)
- Note any change in size, firmness or the skin over the lump

## seePhysioIf
- Tingling, numbness or weakness in the fingers with the lump, or a lump that is hard, growing, painful at rest, or the skin over it changes: see a doctor
- The lump is painful with daily tasks or training
- You're unsure the lump is a typical ganglion: an assessment, sometimes with an ultrasound, gives clarity
- It limits wrist strength or mobility

## physioHelps

- Advice on wrist positions and supports for floor work and lifting can help ease discomfort.
- You get advice on when a doctor's review is worthwhile if the lump stays troublesome.

## clinicNotes
- Transillumination; carpal boss (bony, at the 2nd-3rd CMC base) as a differential; scapholunate screen (Watson test, limited value); an occult dorsal ganglion shows on ultrasound. Palm-side ganglions lie near the radial artery (no pressure techniques). Natural history: adults about 33-58% resolve (secondary sources), children 80-93%. Aspiration recurrence is high (a physician decision); refer after 6 to 12 weeks if troublesome (your ganglion protocol).
- Form 37 (20 Oct 2026): mucous cyst over DIP OA (nail grooving); seed ganglion (flexor sheath A2, pea-sized, tender on gripping, no triggering: hand-trigger); giant cell tumour of the tendon sheath (second commonest hand mass; solid, does not transilluminate); masses larger than 5 cm, deep to fascia, growing or painful: urgent imaging or referral (sarcoma pathway; wrf-lump, hnd-lump). Gude and Morelli 2008.
