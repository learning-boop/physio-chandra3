## blurb
The labrum is a rim of firm tissue that deepens the shoulder socket. It can be irritated or torn by repeated throwing or overhead sport, a fall onto an outstretched arm, or a dislocation, giving a deep pain with clicking or catching, often with the arm overhead.

## noticed
- A deep pain inside the shoulder, often at the front or top
- Clicking, catching or a clunk with the arm overhead or behind you
- Worse with throwing, overhead sport or reaching up and back; sometimes a 'dead arm' moment

## homeCare
- Ease off throwing and heavy overhead work for now, without stopping use of the arm
- Keep strengthening the shoulder and shoulder-blade muscles within comfort
- Avoid repeatedly testing the click

## seePhysioIf
- The shoulder came out of place, or your arm became weak or numb after an injury: see a doctor the same day
- Pain and catching limit throwing, sport or work
- It is not improving after a few weeks of easing off
- You want a plan for returning to throwing or overhead sport

## clinicNotes
- Mechanisms: repetitive overhead or throwing loading (posterosuperior or SLAP, peel-back), traction, a fall onto the outstretched arm, dislocation (anterior-inferior, Bankart). Often coexists with instability or rotator cuff pain.
- Tests are individually weak (AIM manual pp.306-308): crank +LR about 1.4-1.6; anterior slide +LR about 0.7; biceps load tests modest. Combine history, clusters and response to load; MR arthrography if management would change.
- Management: rehabilitation first for most (scapular and rotator cuff strength, posterior capsule mobility, kinetic chain and throwing load management); surgical opinion for persistent mechanical symptoms or instability, or failed rehabilitation in athletes.
- Sources: AIM Theory Manual 2023 Ch. 2.2 pp.306-308; Chandra's shoulder labrum tear protocol.
