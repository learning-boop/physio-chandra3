## blurb
The labrum is a rim of firm tissue that deepens the shoulder socket. It can be irritated or torn by repeated throwing or overhead sport, a fall onto an outstretched arm, or a dislocation, giving a deep pain with clicking or catching, often with the arm overhead. Many labral tears, including SLAP tears, settle with rehabilitation, and surgery is not always needed.

## noticed
- A deep pain inside the shoulder, often at the front or top
- Clicking, catching or a clunk with the arm overhead or behind you
- Worse with throwing, overhead sport or reaching up and back; sometimes a 'dead arm' moment

## homeCare
- Ease off throwing and heavy overhead work for now, without stopping use of the arm
- Keep strengthening the shoulder and shoulder-blade muscles within comfort
- Avoid repeatedly testing the click
- Lift with your elbow close to your body and your palm down
- Rehabilitation often takes 3 to 6 months; throwers may take longer

## seePhysioIf
- It is still out of place or looks a different shape: go to the emergency department
- It came out of place, or your arm became weak or numb after an injury: see a doctor the same day
- Pain and catching limit throwing, sport or work
- It is not improving after 4 to 6 weeks of easing off: see your physiotherapist or doctor
- The shoulder locks or keeps catching, or it is not improving after about 3 months of rehabilitation: ask about a specialist opinion
- You want a plan for returning to throwing or overhead sport

- A physiotherapist can check your shoulder and explain in plain words what may be going on.
- Shoulder and shoulder-blade strengthening, with advice on throwing load, can help ease pain and catching.
- You get a step-by-step return to overhead sport, and advice on when a surgeon's opinion is worthwhile.

## clinicNotes
- Mechanisms: repetitive overhead or throwing loading (posterosuperior or SLAP, peel-back), traction, a fall onto the outstretched arm, dislocation (anterior-inferior, Bankart). Often coexists with instability or rotator cuff pain.
- Tests are individually weak (AIM manual pp.306-308): crank +LR about 1.4-1.6; anterior slide +LR about 0.7; biceps load tests modest. Combine history, clusters and response to load; MR arthrography if management would change.
- Management: rehabilitation first for most (scapular and rotator cuff strength, posterior capsule mobility, kinetic chain and throwing load management); surgical opinion for persistent mechanical symptoms or instability, or failed rehabilitation in athletes.
- Clusters (form 13): passive distraction + active compression; anterior slide + crank; compression-rotation, dynamic labral shear, biceps load II, Speed's, Yergason's, the 3-pack (O'Brien's, throwing test, bicipital tunnel palpation); check GIRD; MR arthrography only if a surgical opinion is being considered. Type II SLAP: no added benefit of surgery over sham (Schrøder 2017). Posterior or posteroinferior labral lesions go with posterior slipping (jerk and Kim tests). A thrower with deep posterior pain but no click: internal impingement (shoulder-throwing).
