## blurb
Three tendons join on the inner side of the shin just below the knee, with a small fluid sac under them. They can become sore with overuse, running, or alongside knee arthritis, giving pain a few centimetres below the inner side of the knee rather than in the joint itself.

## noticed
- Tenderness on the inner shin, a few centimetres below the knee joint
- Worse going up or down stairs, getting up from a chair, or after running
- Sometimes alongside knee arthritis, or more common with extra body weight or diabetes

## homeCare
- Ease back on the activities that bring it on, such as long runs or lots of stairs, for a while
- Strengthening the thigh muscles (front and back) and hips can help
- A cold pack on the sore spot can ease pain after activity
- If side-lying is painful, a pillow between the knees can help at night

## seePhysioIf
- The knee becomes hot, red and swollen: see a doctor the same day
- The pain is not settling after a few weeks of easing off
- It is limiting walking, stairs, or sleep

## clinicNotes
- Pain and tenderness over the medial proximal tibia about 2-5 cm below the joint line, at the insertion of sartorius, gracilis and semitendinosus; swelling uncommon. More common in women, with knee OA, obesity and diabetes, and in runners. Clinical diagnosis; ultrasound if unclear.
- Differentials: medial tibiofemoral OA, medial meniscus (joint-line tenderness), MCL (along the ligament, after a valgus force), saphenous nerve, tibial stress fracture or insufficiency fracture (bone tenderness, night pain), tumour (rare).
- Management: load modification, quadriceps and hamstring strengthening, hip strengthening, treat co-existing OA; injection is a medical option.
- Sources: StatPearls, Pes Anserine Bursitis; AIM Theory Manual 2023 Ch. 2.7 p.46 (differential list).
