## blurb
A nerve runs down the front of the ankle under the straps of tissue that hold the tendons down, and across the top of the foot to the skin between the big toe and the second toe. Tight laces, ski or skate boots, a bony bump on the top of the foot, or repeated ankle sprains can press on it, giving aching or burning on the top of the foot and numbness in that web space. It usually settles once the pressure is taken off.

## noticed
- Aching or burning on the top of the foot, sometimes at night
- Tingling or numbness in the web between the big toe and the second toe
- Worse with tight laces, ski, skate or work boots, or high heels
- Sometimes after ankle sprains, or with a bony lump on the top of the foot

## homeCare
- Loosen your laces, or skip the eyelets over the sore spot (lace around it)
- Add padding to the tongue of the shoe, or change boots that press on the top of the foot
- Avoid sitting on your feet or kneeling with the toes pointed for long spells
- Keep the ankle moving normally

## seePhysioIf
- The tingling or numbness has not eased a few weeks after changing your footwear
- The toes feel weak, or the foot slaps down or the toes catch when you walk: see a doctor first
- Moving your back changes it, or it spreads up the shin: the knee or the back may be the source
- Burning or numbness in both feet, like socks: see your doctor, as a general nerve condition can cause this

## clinicNotes
- Deep fibular nerve under the inferior extensor retinaculum (anterior tarsal tunnel), or distally over a talonavicular or first tarsometatarsal osteophyte, an os intermetatarseum or EHB. Sensory: first web space; motor: EDB (often only wasting of EDB).
- Neurodynamics (Shacklock; Butler): fibular neurodynamic test PF/IN of the ankle AND toes, then SLR (the toes are often omitted; differentiate with hip flexion; sensitise with hip adduction and medial rotation, contralateral lateral flexion). Palpate between EHL and EDL tendons over the dorsum of the ankle and along to the first metatarsal; Tinel; compare sides.
- Interfaces: footwear, dorsal osteophytes (midfoot OA, foot-midfootoa.md), recurrent inversion sprains (traction), ganglia.
- Management: offload the dorsum (lacing, padding, footwear), treat interface stiffness, fibular nerve sliders (Butler: knee extension in hip flexion with PF/IN; neck extension as a slider), then tensioners; injection or decompression for persistent cases with a structural cause.
- Look-alikes: superficial fibular nerve (dorsum of the foot but NOT the first web space, anterolateral shin; leg-peroneal.md), common fibular nerve at the fibular neck (foot drop, crossing the legs), L5 radiculopathy (back, big toe extension weakness, above-knee features), midfoot arthritis, extensor tendinopathy, Morton's neuroma (between the 3rd and 4th toes, plantar).
- Sources: Butler DS, NOI neurodynamic techniques workbook (peroneal nerve); Shacklock NDS lower-quarter manual 2017-18 (nerve palpation, fibular neurodynamic test); Basson A et al., JOSPT 2017.
