## blurb
The two halves of the pelvis meet at the front, at the pubic bone. Kicking and twisting sports, or the changes of pregnancy, can make this joint sore, giving pain in the middle at the front that can spread to the inner thighs. Standing on one leg, stairs, and getting in and out of a car are often painful.

## noticed
- Pain in the middle at the front, over the pubic bone
- Worse standing on one leg, on stairs, or getting out of a car
- Common in kicking sports and in pregnancy

## homeCare
- Keep your knees together when getting in and out of a car or bed
- Take stairs one at a time, and avoid standing on one leg to dress
- Ease off kicking and twisting sport for a while

## seePhysioIf
- It is limiting walking or daily tasks
- It is not easing after 2 to 3 weeks
- You are pregnant: a physiotherapist can help with support and exercises
- Pain at rest or at night, pain on hopping, or a limp, especially if you run a lot, your periods have stopped, or you have low bone density: see a doctor (possible bone stress in the pubic bone)
- Pain at the front of the pelvis with a fever, especially after a birth or an operation: see a doctor today
- Since the birth, severe pain at the front of the pelvis so you can hardly walk, sometimes after a click: see your doctor or maternity team

## clinicNotes
- Doha 2015 pubic-related groin pain: local tenderness of the pubic symphysis and adjacent bone. MRI bone marrow oedema is common in athletes without symptoms. Differentials: pubic bone stress injury (bone stress consensus 2024; hpf-stress now names the pelvis), osteomyelitis pubis (fever; after birth or surgery), symphysis separation after birth (pg-symphysis), adductor- and inguinal-related pain. Pelvic girdle pain in pregnancy can also be felt at the back of the pelvis (sij:pgp).
- Sources: Weir A et al., BJSM 2015 (Doha); ESSKA consensus 2024; NHS pelvic girdle pain in pregnancy.
