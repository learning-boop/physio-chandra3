## blurb
Pain at the back of the knee often comes from a fluid-filled swelling there (a Baker's cyst), which usually forms when the knee joint itself is irritated, for example by arthritis or a meniscus problem. The tendons that cross the back of the knee can also be sore. If the calf becomes swollen, warm, and tender, a doctor needs to check it, because a burst cyst and a clot look the same.

## noticed
- A feeling of fullness or a soft lump at the back of the knee
- Tightness when bending or fully straightening the knee
- Often alongside other knee aches, such as arthritis

## homeCare
- Keep the knee moving gently; avoid long spells of deep bending
- Ease off the activities that make the knee swell
- A cold pack can ease the fullness after activity

## seePhysioIf
- The fullness keeps coming back
- Bending or straightening is getting harder
- The calf becomes swollen, warm, or tender: see a doctor the same day
