## blurb
The small joint where your collarbone meets your breastbone moves every time you lift your arm or shrug. A fall or a seatbelt injury can sprain it, and repeated overhead work can make it sore or stiff. Pain sits right at the inner end of the collarbone. Most sprains settle over 2 to 6 weeks.

## noticed
- Pain you can point to at the inner end of the collarbone
- Worse lifting your arm overhead, shrugging or reaching across
- Sometimes swelling, a click or a small bump there

## homeCare
- Keep the arm below shoulder height for a few days, and avoid lying on that side
- Ice wrapped in a cloth, or heat, for comfort
- Gently move your shoulder and neck within comfort every day

## seePhysioIf
- Since an injury, new trouble breathing or swallowing, a hoarse voice, or swelling in your neck or arm: call 911
- A new bump or step at the collarbone after a fall or blow: see a doctor or urgent care the same day for an X-ray
- The joint is hot, red or swollen, with a fever or feeling very unwell: get medical help now
- Pain that comes on with effort, chest tightness or breathlessness: call 911
- If this is your first pain in the chest area, see a doctor as well
- The pain persists past about 2 to 3 weeks

## physioHelps

- A physiotherapist can check the joint and explain what is causing the pain.
- Gentle hands-on techniques and shoulder-blade and shoulder exercises can help you lift your arm comfortably again.
- You get advice on overhead tasks and a plan that builds at a pace that suits the joint.

## clinicNotes
- Mechanism (fall onto the shoulder, seatbelt, overhead load); anterior prominence versus posterior depression; SC accessory glides; shrug and elevation; cervical and thoracic screen. Posterior dislocation signs (dysphagia, dyspnoea, hoarseness, venous congestion of the arm or neck) are an emergency (injury screen I8); serendipity view or CT when traumatic. Inflammatory or septic SC arthritis (SpA about 14%; IV drug use, diabetes). A mark right beside the breastbone is the chest on the body map and reaches costochondritis instead.
