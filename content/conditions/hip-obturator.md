## blurb
A nerve runs from the low back through the pelvis to the inner thigh, where it supplies the muscles that pull the legs together and a patch of skin on the inner thigh. In some active people, often footballers and runners, it can be pinched in the tissue at the top of the inner thigh, giving a deep ache in the groin that spreads down the inner thigh with exercise, sometimes with tingling or a feeling of weakness. It can also follow pelvic surgery or childbirth.

## noticed
- A deep ache at the top of the inner thigh that spreads down towards the knee, building with running, kicking or changing direction
- Tingling, burning or numbness on the inner thigh
- The inner thigh muscles feel weak or tired, sometimes with a slightly swinging walk
- Eases with rest, and returns with the next session

## homeCare
- Reduce the sport or running that brings it on, rather than stopping all activity
- Avoid strong inner thigh stretches that bring on tingling
- Keep the hip moving gently within comfort

## seePhysioIf
- It keeps returning with exercise, or the inner thigh feels weak
- Groin pain that is linked to your periods, with burning or blood when passing urine, a lump that appears when you cough, or testicle pain: see a doctor, as it can come from the organs or a hernia
- New inner thigh numbness or weakness after pelvic or hip surgery, or after childbirth: see your doctor
- Pain also goes down the front of the thigh from the back: the low back may be the source

## clinicNotes
- Obturator nerve (L2-L4): sensory to a variable medial thigh patch (often the distal medial thigh), motor to the adductors. Exercise-induced entrapment in athletes (fascia over pectineus and adductor brevis; Bradshaw 1997), after pelvic or hip surgery, obstetric injury, obturator hernia (elderly, thin women; Howship-Romberg sign), pelvic masses.
- Neurodynamics (Butler): slump knee bend in side-lying (knee flexion, hip extension) then hip ABDUCTION, differentiate with the neck; the femoral slump without abduction is the femoral nerve and the upper roots (lowback-upperroot.md). Adductor weakness, medial thigh sensation, adductor reflex.
- Management: load management, adductor and pelvic control, obturator nerve sliders (abduction with neck extension), interface work; nerve block or neurolysis for persistent cases with an electrodiagnostic confirmation.
- Look-alikes: adductor-related groin pain (hip-add.md: pain where the muscle meets the pubic bone, resisted adduction), pubic-related and inguinal-related groin pain, hip joint (FAI, OA), L3-L4 radiculopathy (lowback-upperroot.md), stress fracture of the pubic ramus or femoral neck (the site's bone-stress questions), obturator hernia.
- Sources: Butler DS, NOI neurodynamic techniques workbook (femoral nerve: obturator test); Bradshaw C et al., Am J Sports Med 1997 (obturator neuropathy in athletes); Weir A et al., Doha agreement 2015 (groin pain classification).
