## blurb
The ulnar nerve runs through a tight tunnel at the inner elbow (the 'funny bone'). Sustained bending or leaning can irritate it, causing tingling into the ring and little fingers and hand weakness.

## noticed
- Numbness or tingling in the ring and little fingers, worse at night or with the elbow bent
- Aching at the inner elbow, sometimes down the forearm
- Weak grip, clumsiness, dropping objects

## homeCare
- Avoid keeping the elbow fully bent for long: adjust phone and sleep habits
- Stop leaning on the inner elbow on desks and armrests
- A towel loosely wrapped around the elbow at night keeps it straighter
- Use a headset or speakerphone for long calls
- If you must lean on the elbow, put a soft pad under it

## seePhysioIf
- Tingling or numbness persists more than a few weeks
- Your grip keeps getting weaker, the muscle between your thumb and index finger looks thinner, or the numbness is there all the time: see a doctor, who may arrange nerve tests
- You would like help with positions and habits: many mild cases settle within a few months with these changes
- It is not clearly better after about 3 months of these changes: see your doctor, who may arrange nerve tests

## physioHelps

- A physiotherapist can explain how the nerve gets irritated and check the feeling and grip in your hand.
- Clear advice on elbow positions, leaning and sleep habits can help the nerve settle.
- A physiotherapist can track your progress and tell you when a doctor's review is worthwhile.

## clinicNotes
- Examination: McGowan grade; elbow flexion test and flexion-pressure test; Tinel's at the cubital tunnel; Froment's and Wartenberg's signs; intrinsic and first dorsal interosseous wasting; C8-T1 and thoracic outlet differentials.
- Treatment: education and avoiding sustained flexion and pressure; 89.5% improved with information alone, and splints or nerve glides added nothing (Svernlöv 2009); night extension positioning. Refer promptly with wasting or constant numbness; otherwise after about 3 months of failed care (NHS RefHelp: routine at 6 months, urgent with wasting). Simple decompression and transposition similar (Caliandro 2016, Cochrane CD006839).
- Your cubital tunnel protocols: referral timing differs between them (neurologist at 6 weeks vs surgeon at 6 to 12 weeks or 3 months); ultrasound, low-level laser, steroid iontophoresis or phonophoresis, shockwave and perineural dry needling have weak or no evidence (dry needling also risks the nerve); nerve glides are optional rather than core; "Froman's" should read "Froment's".
- Form 22 (19 Oct 2026): Tinel at the cubital tunnel (Sn 54-70%, Sp 53-99%); scratch collapse test (Cheng 2008); dorsal ulnar cutaneous sensation (spared in Guyon canal lesions); ULNT3; diabetes, old elbow fracture and arthritis as associations; education and night positioning first-line (ACOEM 2013). The form's night splint and nerve glides stay optional (Svernlöv 2009, above).
- Snapping ulnar nerve (form 5): subluxation or dislocation over the medial epicondyle, with or without a snapping medial triceps (Spinner and Goldner 1998); dynamic test through flexion past about 90 degrees (two snaps: the nerve, then the triceps); usually harmless, treat the symptoms; surgical opinion if nerve symptoms persist; distinguish from a loose body (locking) and PLRI (outer clunk on a chair push-up).
