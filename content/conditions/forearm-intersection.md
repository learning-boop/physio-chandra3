## blurb
A few finger-widths above the wrist on the thumb side, two sets of tendons that move the thumb and wrist cross over each other. A sudden increase in repeated wrist movement, such as rowing, paddling, weights, or a new job, can make them rub and swell where they cross, sometimes with a squeaking or creaking feel. It is an irritation from overuse rather than damage, and it often settles within a few weeks.

## noticed
- Pain and swelling on the top of the forearm, above the wrist on the thumb side
- A squeaking or creaking feeling there when the wrist moves
- It started after a new or bigger amount of repeated wrist work

## homeCare
- Cut back the repeated wrist movement that brought it on for a week or two
- Keep the wrist and thumb moving gently within comfort
- A cold pack over the sore spot can ease the swelling
- Build the activity back up gradually rather than all at once
- A wrist splint that holds the wrist slightly bent back, during sore tasks in the first weeks

## seePhysioIf
- It has not settled after 2 weeks of easing off
- The swelling or creaking keeps coming back when you restart
- You want a plan to return to rowing, paddling, lifting, or work
- It is not getting better after 4 to 6 weeks: see your doctor

- Easing the repeated wrist movement for a while, then gentle strengthening, can help the tendons settle.
- You get a gradual plan back to rowing, paddling, lifting or work, with tips on technique.
