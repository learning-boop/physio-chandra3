## blurb
On the outer side of the middle of the foot, the cuboid bone and its joints can become sore and stiff after an ankle sprain or from repeated load, as in dancing and running. It hurts on the outer edge of the foot when pushing off or walking, and often eases quickly with hands-on treatment, taping and strengthening.

## noticed
- Pain on the outer edge of the middle of the foot
- Worse pushing off, walking on uneven ground or rising onto the toes
- Often after an ankle sprain, or in dancers and runners

## homeCare
- Supportive shoes; avoid uneven ground for a while
- Ease off the running or jumping that brings it on
- Gentle ankle and foot movement and calf strengthening within comfort

## seePhysioIf
- After an injury, the bony knob halfway along the outer edge of the foot is sharply tender, or you cannot take 4 steps: see a doctor the same day for an X-ray
- After an injury, there is bruising on the sole in the middle of the foot: see a doctor the same day
- One spot of bone on the outer edge of the foot hurts with every step or hop, or aches at night, after more running, walking or training: see a doctor first (possible stress fracture)
- It is not settling after 1 to 2 weeks
- You want hands-on treatment and a plan back to sport

## clinicNotes
- Lateral midfoot pain over the calcaneocuboid joint or 4th and 5th TMT joints; pain with lateral column loading and push-off; often after inversion sprain or in dancers. No validated test (evidence low: case series and reviews); use history, palpation, midfoot stress and response to taping or padding. Exclude a 5th metatarsal base fracture, a Lisfranc injury (plantar ecchymosis) and stress fracture.
- Management: cuboid manipulation (whip or squeeze) with consent, taping or a cuboid pad, peroneus longus and intrinsic strengthening, footwear; the protocol expects improvement in 2 to 6 weeks in mild cases (figures not independently sourced).
- Sources: AIM Theory Manual 2023 pp. 558-560; Durall CJ 2011 (PMC3445231); cuboid syndrome protocol.
