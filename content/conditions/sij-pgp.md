## blurb
During pregnancy the load on the pelvis grows and the way the body carries it changes: weight and balance shift forward, and the tummy muscles stretch. Pain can then be felt at the back of the pelvis, over the pubic bone at the front, or both. The pelvis is strong, not loose or out of place: the pain is about how the load is being shared. It is common, it is not harmful to the baby, it responds well to physiotherapy, and most people find it has settled within three months of the birth.

## noticed
- Pain at the back of the pelvis, over the pubic bone, or both
- Standing on one leg, climbing stairs or rolling over in bed brings it on
- Lifting a straight leg while lying down can feel heavy

## homeCare
- Keep your knees together when rolling over or getting in and out of the car
- Sit down to put on trousers, socks and shoes; take stairs one step at a time
- A pillow between the knees at night, and a pelvic support belt if it helps
- Keep moving within comfort: short walks, and avoid wide or uneven stances

## seePhysioIf
- Pain limits walking, stairs or sleep
- It has not eased in the weeks after giving birth
- You want exercises and support that are safe for your stage of pregnancy
