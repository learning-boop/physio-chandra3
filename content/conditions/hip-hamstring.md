## blurb
The hamstring tendons attach to the bone you sit on, at the bottom of the buttock. With running, hill or speed work, lunges, or a lot of sitting, these tendons can become sensitive, giving a deep ache at the sit bone that is worse sitting on hard chairs and with stretching the back of the thigh. It responds well to a gradual loading programme.

## noticed
- A deep ache right at the sit bone, at the bottom of the buttock
- Worse sitting, especially on hard seats or when driving, and with running, hills, lunges or deep bending
- Stretching the back of the thigh, or bending forward with straight legs, can bring it on

## homeCare
- Use a cushion, or sit with the sore side slightly off the edge of the chair; break up long sitting
- Ease off hill running, speed work and deep lunges for a while, without stopping activity
- Avoid stretching the hamstring hard while it is sore: stretching presses the tendon onto the bone
- Gentle, held hamstring and buttock strengthening within comfort usually helps

## seePhysioIf
- A pop or sudden pain at the sit bone (for example doing the splits or slipping), with a large bruise on the back of the thigh: see a doctor the same day, as a torn tendon is best repaired early
- Tingling, numbness or weakness down the leg: see us or your doctor, as a nerve may be involved
- It is not easing after a few weeks of changing your sitting and training
- You want a staged loading plan to get back to running

## clinicNotes
- Diagnosis: deep buttock pain at the ischial tuberosity with sitting and hamstring loading; tender at the tuberosity; provocation by the bent-knee stretch, the Puranen-Orava test and the modified bent-knee stretch; ultrasound or MRI if unclear. Ischial bursitis often coexists.
- Differentials: deep gluteal syndrome (sciatic symptoms, sciatic notch tenderness), ischiofemoral impingement, lumbar radiculopathy, SIJ, an avulsion (a pop with bruising: same day, I5 in the injury screen), a stress fracture of the pelvis.
- Management: load management (reduce compression: sitting, deep hip flexion, hill and speed work), isometrics progressing to isotonic and energy-storage loading, then a return to running; avoid aggressive hamstring stretching early; shockwave or injection as add-ons for persistent cases.
