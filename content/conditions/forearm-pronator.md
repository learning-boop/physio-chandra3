## blurb
The median nerve, the same nerve involved in carpal tunnel syndrome, can be squeezed higher up, between the muscles on the palm side of the forearm just below the elbow. It gives an ache in the forearm with tingling in the thumb, index, and middle fingers that comes on with use, rather than waking you at night as carpal tunnel usually does.

## noticed
- An ache on the palm side of the forearm, just below the elbow
- Tingling in the thumb, index, and middle fingers during or after use
- Worse with repeated gripping or turning the palm down

## homeCare
- Ease off repeated forceful gripping and palm-down turning for a while
- Take short breaks and change hand position during tool or computer work
- Keep the arm moving gently rather than resting it completely
- Avoid carrying heavy loads with the palm down and the elbow straight

## seePhysioIf
- The tingling or ache has not eased after 2 to 3 weeks
- Your pinch or grip keeps getting weaker, you cannot make a round "OK" sign, or the muscles at the base of the thumb look thinner: see a doctor, who may arrange nerve tests
- It is not clearly improving after 4 to 6 weeks: see your doctor
- You are not sure whether it is this or carpal tunnel at the wrist

## physioHelps

- A physiotherapist can check where the nerve is irritated and explain it in plain words.
- Changes to gripping and forearm turning, with gentle exercises, can help the nerve settle.
- You get advice on tool and computer work to help keep the symptoms calm.

## clinicNotes
- CTS CPG 2019 lists pronator syndrome as a median nerve differential. Favour a proximal level when sensation over the thenar eminence is involved (palmar cutaneous branch leaves before the tunnel), there is no night waking eased by shaking, and the forearm aches with pronation or grip. Wrist provocation tests cannot separate the two levels and ULNT1 does not discriminate (D); use nerve conduction studies when unclear. Source: Erickson 2019.
- Form 25 (19 Oct 2026): tender over pronator teres; resisted pronation with the elbow straight (pronator teres); resisted elbow flexion in supination at 120-130 degrees (lacertus fibrosus); resisted middle-finger PIP flexion (FDS arch); Tinel over the proximal forearm; Phalen and wrist Tinel negative; ULNT1; NCS often normal; DASH, PSFS (Dang 2009; Hsu 2012).
- Anterior interosseous nerve (form 26): flat OK sign (FPL, FDP index), weak pronator quadratus, sensation intact; rule out FPL or FDP rupture (tenodesis); after a viral illness consider neuralgic amyotrophy; NCS/EMG; conservative 3-6 months, surgical opinion if no recovery by about 6 months (Dang 2009). Doctor first (frf-nerve, erf-nerve, injury screen I8); no card.
