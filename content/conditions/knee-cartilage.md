## blurb
The smooth cartilage on the ends of the knee bones can be injured in a twist, a landing or a kneecap dislocation, sometimes with a small piece of the bone beneath it, and a bone bruise is swelling in the bone just under the joint surface. In osteochondritis dissecans (OCD), seen mostly in active teenagers, a small patch of bone under the cartilage weakens. Once a doctor has checked it, rehabilitation within their plan helps many people get back to sport and activity; bone bruises usually settle within 2 to 4 months.

## noticed
- Pain and swelling inside the knee that come and go, often flaring after running, jumping or other impact
- Sometimes catching, or a sore spot along the joint line
- Easier with rest and less impact

## homeCare
- Follow your doctor's or surgeon's advice on sport and weight bearing
- Swap running and jumping for cycling or pool exercise while the knee is sore and swollen
- Check the knee the morning after activity: if it is more swollen, ease back a little

## seePhysioIf
- You have a diagnosis and would like a rehabilitation plan that works within your doctor's advice
- You have had cartilage surgery and want a staged programme that follows your surgeon's plan
- The knee suddenly locks and will not straighten, or swells up quickly: see a doctor the same day
- The knee is hot, red and swollen with a fever, or you feel unwell: go to the emergency department

## physioHelps
<!-- Approved by Chandra Matla, 26 Oct 2026 (in chat; written from the knee document forms 14 and 15) -->
- A physiotherapist can check your knee's movement, swelling and strength and explain the plan in plain words.
- Exercises for the knee and hip, then balance and control work, can help you get stronger within your doctor's limits.
- You get a gradual plan back to running, jumping and sport.

## clinicNotes
- Traumatic chondral or osteochondral lesion (form 14; Theory pp. 502-504; Logerstedt 2018 JOSPT 48(2)): acute trauma with haemarthrosis at 0-2 h (osteochondral fracture), intermittent pain and swelling, catching or locking, joint-line tenderness; ICD tear of articular cartilage classified with low certainty. ICRS grades 0-4. Defects in 60-70% of arthroscopies; over a third of ACL and meniscal tears have a cartilage injury (Flanigan 2010). Bone contusions (pivot shift, hyperextension, reduced patellar dislocation) resolve in 2-4 months (Kon 2016).
- Osteochondritis dissecans (form 15; Theory Ch. 2.8 pp. 537-538): idiopathic focal subchondral bone deterioration; knee most frequent at 12-19 y; males 3.8 times; gradual intermittent pain and oedema with sport, no trauma; acute locking with haemarthrosis and loose bodies: orthopaedic referral. X-ray first, MRI if still suspected (ESSKA survey, Kemmeren 2023; a survey, not a guideline); activity and sport restriction is the non-operative treatment of choice for a stable lesion, more successful before skeletal maturity. Wilson's test (manual pp. 438-439): limited diagnostic use, can monitor; ESSKA does not recommend it. AAOS OCD CPG (2010, rapid update 1 Dec 2023; the 2010 text checked 26 Oct 2026, the three upgraded recommendations not identified): X-rays including AP, lateral, sunrise and tunnel views are an option for knee symptoms or signs (limited); MRI an option to characterise a lesion or suspected concomitant injury (limited); unable to recommend for or against any specific non-operative treatment, activity restriction included (inconclusive); surgery offered for a salvageable unstable or displaced lesion, mature or immature (consensus); re-examine with X-ray and/or MRI if symptoms persist after treatment (consensus). So the card's "reduced sport first for a stable area" rests on the ESSKA survey and the Theory Resources, not on AAOS.
- Doctor first until diagnosed: quick swelling after an injury is the injury screen I5 (same day); a locked knee is kf-locked and I6 (same day); recurrent swelling with sport and no injury at 5 to 29 is kf-ocd (within days).
- Rehabilitation: early progressive AROM/PROM; stepwise weight bearing to full by 6-8 weeks after surgery; supervised then home programme; progressive knee and hip strength; neuromuscular training; NMES for the quadriceps (Logerstedt 2018; manual p. 428). Return to sport after cartilage surgery about 7-18 months by procedure (Campbell 2016: OCT 88-89% at 7-10 months, microfracture 75% at 8.6 months, ACI 84-86% at 16 months; Mithoefer 2009).
- Outcomes: IKDC 2000 or KOOS; effusion by the modified stroke test; joint compression and joint-line tenderness with meniscal tests to separate a meniscal tear.
