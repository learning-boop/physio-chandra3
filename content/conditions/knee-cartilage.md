## blurb
This is damage to the smooth, slippery surface that covers the ends of the bones in your knee (different from the meniscus cushion between the bones). It can happen from a sudden injury or from repeated pounding over time.

## noticed
- A specific twisting, landing, or impact injury followed by knee swelling within a couple of hours
- Episodes of catching, locking, or the knee feeling unreliable during certain movements
- A deep, aching pain that can flare up with repetitive activities like running, jumping, or squatting, sometimes with puffiness that comes and goes

## homeCare
- Cut back on high-impact activities like running, jumping, or hard pivoting until symptoms settle
- Keep gently moving the knee through its comfortable range rather than keeping it completely still
- Work on gradual, low-impact strengthening of the thigh and hip muscles to support the joint

## seePhysioIf
- Your knee swells up within a couple of hours of an injury or keeps swelling on and off
- Your knee catches, locks, or gives way, or the ache isn't easing with rest and simple activity changes
