## blurb
The biceps attaches just below the front of the elbow. Heavy lifting, pulling, or repeated turning of the forearm palm-up can overload the tendon there, giving pain in the elbow crease. A sudden pop with bruising or a bulge in the upper arm is different: that can be a tear, which needs a doctor the same day.

## noticed
- Pain deep in the front of the elbow crease
- Worse lifting with the palm up, or turning a key or screwdriver
- Tender to press in the crease
- Turning the palm up feels weaker than the other side
- After a tear: bruising on the inner side of the elbow and a bulge higher up the arm

## homeCare
- Ease off heavy palm-up lifting and pulling for a while
- Keep the elbow moving gently through its full range
- Build back lifting gradually as the pain settles
- Carry with the palm down and the elbow close to your body

## seePhysioIf
- The pain has not settled after 2 to 3 weeks
- Lifting or turning the forearm is getting weaker
- You want a graded plan to return to lifting or sport
- You felt a pop, with bruising, or the biceps now bulges higher up the arm: see a doctor the same day, as a tear is best repaired early. If it happened more than 2 weeks ago, still see a doctor within a few days

## physioHelps
<!-- Approved by Chandra Matla, 17 Oct 2026 (review: content/review/physioHelps-review-2026-10-17.docx) -->
- A physiotherapist can check the tendon, explain what is going on, and spot signs that need a doctor.
- A gradual strengthening programme for the arm aims to help the tendon handle lifting again.
- You get a step-by-step plan back to lifting, pulling and work tasks without overdoing it.

## clinicNotes
- Hook test: 100% sensitivity and specificity in the original series (O'Driscoll 2007); later series lower (reported about 78% overall, 83% complete, 30% partial tears), so a negative test does not exclude a tear. Ultrasound or MRI if a tear is suspected. Acute repair preferred; over about 4 weeks often needs reconstruction (ISAKOS 2023). Non-operative care reportedly loses about 40-50% of supination endurance (secondary source).
- Differentials: brachialis strain, bicipitoradial bursitis, lateral antebrachial cutaneous nerve entrapment, C5-6 radiculopathy.
- Form 3 (19 Oct 2026): hook test (Sn 81%, Sp 100%, Devereaux 2013), biceps squeeze test (Ruland 2005), supination/pronation test, combined tests; reverse Popeye sign; repair ideally within 2-3 weeks (I5); risk factors smoking and steroid use; look-alikes AIN syndrome (weak OK sign, no numbness), anterior capsular stiffness, proximal biceps; DASH, PSFS.
- Brachialis strain (form 6): crease or lower front-of-arm pain after a sudden effort, intact hook test, painful resisted flexion with the forearm pronated.
