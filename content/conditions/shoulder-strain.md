## blurb
A muscle around the shoulder (the deltoid on the outside, the chest muscle at the front, or the muscles at the back) can be over-stretched by a sudden hard effort or a heavier day than usual. It is sore to use and to press, but muscle has a good blood supply and usually heals well within a few weeks with gentle, gradual use.

## noticed
- Started during a sudden effort, or the morning after a heavy day
- A sore spot in the muscle itself, not deep in the joint
- Sore to use and to stretch; sometimes a little bruising

## homeCare
- For the first 2 days, ease off and use a cold pack wrapped in a cloth for 10 to 15 minutes
- Then keep it moving within comfort; avoid hard stretching early on
- Build back to heavier tasks over 2 to 3 weeks

## seePhysioIf
- You felt a pop and the muscle or arm now looks a different shape: see a doctor the same day
- The arm is weak to lift or turn outwards: see a doctor or physiotherapist within a week
- Pain that comes on with effort, chest tightness or breathlessness: call 911
- It is not improving after 2 weeks, or there is a lot of bruising
- It is not clearly improving after 4 to 6 weeks: see your doctor

## physioHelps

- A physiotherapist can check which muscle is strained and make sure the joint and tendons are not involved.
- A graded strengthening and stretching plan can help the muscle rebuild its strength.
- You get a staged plan back to sport, the gym or work.

## clinicNotes
- Grade I-III; pain on resisted contraction and on passive stretch of the muscle; tender belly or musculotendinous junction. Rule out a rotator cuff tear (lag signs), a pectoralis major rupture (pop during a bench press, bruising, loss of the anterior axillary fold) and a long head of biceps rupture (injury screen I9, same day). Deep transverse frictions as indicated.
