## blurb
The tendons that move the thumb run through a snug tunnel on the thumb side of the wrist. Repetitive gripping and lifting (classically a new baby, a kettle or a phone) can irritate that sheath. It is common, it is not arthritis, and the tendons are not torn.

## noticed
- Achy or sharp thumb-side wrist pain, sometimes up the forearm
- Worse with gripping, pinching, lifting, texting
- A tender spot or slight swelling over the bony bump on the thumb side of the wrist
- A painful catch with thumb movement

## homeCare
- Change the lift that brings it on: scoop with the palm up rather than thumb-first
- A thumb-spica splint for aggravating tasks can calm it
- Gradual return to load as pain settles
- Wear the thumb splint at night too, if it helps

## seePhysioIf
- It started after a fall onto your hand: see a doctor the same day for an X-ray first, as a broken wrist bone can look like this
- Thumb-side pain lasts more than 1 to 2 weeks
- It returns whenever you resume normal activity
- Splints or rest alone haven't fixed it: a physiotherapist can guide a gradual return to load
- It is not getting better after 4 to 6 weeks of a splint and easier lifting: see your doctor

## clinicNotes
- Tests: the thumb-in-fist-then-ulnar-deviation test (W5) is the Eichhoff test (Sn 0.89, Sp 0.14, Goubau 2014; the form labels these figures 'Finkelstein / Eichhoff'), which gives more false positives than Finkelstein's original (traction on the thumb) and the WHAT test (wrist hyperflexion and abduction of the thumb; Goubau 2014; Wu 2018). AIM manual: Finkelstein sensitivity 81%, specificity 50%, LR+ 1.62.
- Treatment: relative rest, thumb spica splint for aggravating tasks, lifting technique (new parents), graded loading (low-certainty evidence). Injection plus splint has the strongest evidence (Challoumas 2023 network meta-analysis; the splint adds little), a physician decision. Hand surgeon if not better after 6 to 12 weeks, or both sides (AIM).
- Differentials: thumb base OA (grind test), intersection syndrome, superficial radial nerve (Wartenberg's), scaphoid fracture, C6 radiculopathy.
- Form 1 (20 Oct 2026): crepitus ("wet leather"); HANDGUIDE: instructions and splint; NSAIDs and injection are the doctor's decision (not patient text); hand surgeon if conservative care fails over 6 to 12 weeks or both sides; PRWHE, DASH. FCR tendinopathy (form 3) as a differential: volar-radial pain at the scaphoid tubercle, pain on resisted flexion with radial deviation; differentials STT OA, volar ganglion, scaphoid.
