## blurb
Pain from inside the elbow joint itself tends to come with stiffness, where the elbow will no longer fully straighten or bend, or with catching or locking when a small loose piece of bone or cartilage gets in the way. It is more common after an old injury, in heavy manual work, or with age.

## noticed
- The elbow will not fully straighten or bend, compared with the other side
- Catching, locking, or a grinding feeling as it moves
- An ache around the whole joint rather than one tendon

## homeCare
- Move the elbow gently through the range you have, several times a day
- Spread heavy or repeated gripping tasks through the day
- Warmth can ease stiffness before activity

## seePhysioIf
- The elbow is losing more movement
- It locks and you have to wiggle it free
- Stiffness is limiting daily tasks like washing your hair or eating
- It locks and will not free up, or the joint becomes hot and swollen: see a doctor

## clinicNotes
- Osteoarthritis foundation (content/reference/osteoarthritis.md; "Osteoarthritis.docx" intake v1.0, signed by Chandra 2 Oct 2026; AIM Theory Manual 2023 pp. 572-581): clinical diagnosis without imaging (NICE NG226: 45 or over, activity-related pain, morning stiffness 30 minutes or less); stage early / moderate / advanced. First line at every stage: education, strength and neuromuscular exercise (2-3 times a week, 6-12 weeks), weight management where relevant; manual therapy only alongside exercise. Pain monitoring: up to 5/10 during exercise, back to usual by the next morning. Avoid "wear and tear" and "bone on bone" framing.
- Elbow: OA is uncommon (about 2%, reported) and often under 60: heavy manual work or an old injury (AIM manual p. 573 lists "elbow (with trauma)"); NICE NG226's "45 or over" fits the elbow poorly. Pain at the end of range rather than mid-range; ulnar nerve symptoms are common alongside. Catching or locking points to a loose body. The general OA principles apply to the arthritis part.
- Under 16 with catching: capitellar OCD until imaged (erf-child).
