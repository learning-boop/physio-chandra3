## blurb
Pain from inside the elbow joint itself tends to come with stiffness, where the elbow will no longer fully straighten or bend, or with catching or locking when a small loose piece of bone or cartilage gets in the way. It is more common after an old injury, in heavy manual work, or with age.
After a fall or a sprain, an elbow that still will not fully straighten weeks later should be X-rayed first; once a broken bone is ruled out, hands-on treatment and exercise may help it move again.

## noticed
- The elbow will not fully straighten or bend, compared with the other side
- Catching, locking, or a grinding feeling as it moves
- An ache around the whole joint rather than one tendon
- Since a fall or sprain, the elbow has never fully straightened
- Swelling after it catches or locks

## homeCare
- Move the elbow gently through the range you have, several times a day
- Spread heavy or repeated gripping tasks through the day
- Warmth can ease stiffness before activity
- If it locks, relax the arm and gently wiggle it free; do not force it
- Do not force the elbow straight or lift heavy loads until it has been checked

## seePhysioIf
- The elbow is losing more movement
- It locks and you have to wiggle it free
- Stiffness is limiting daily tasks like washing your hair or eating
- It locks and will not free up when you gently move it, or you cannot turn your palm up or down at all: see a doctor the same day
- The joint becomes hot and swollen: see a doctor the same day; with a fever, go to the emergency department
- Since a fall, the elbow has never fully straightened and it has not been X-rayed: see a doctor first for an X-ray
- It is not improving after 4 to 6 weeks of exercise: see your doctor

## physioHelps

- A physiotherapist can check how your elbow bends and straightens and explain what is going on.
- Regular movement and strength exercises can help ease stiffness and keep your elbow working.
- You get advice on pacing heavy gripping tasks and managing flare-ups at work and home.

## clinicNotes
- Osteoarthritis foundation (content/reference/osteoarthritis.md; "Osteoarthritis.docx" intake v1.0, signed by Chandra 2 Oct 2026; AIM Theory Manual 2023 pp. 572-581): clinical diagnosis without imaging (NICE NG226: 45 or over, activity-related pain, morning stiffness 30 minutes or less); stage early / moderate / advanced. First line at every stage: education, strength and neuromuscular exercise (2-3 times a week, 6-12 weeks), weight management where relevant; manual therapy only alongside exercise. Pain monitoring: up to 5/10 during exercise, back to usual by the next morning. Avoid "wear and tear" and "bone on bone" framing. EULAR 2023 hip and knee update (Moseng 2024): self-management education, reinforced at follow-up. Dry needling: JOSPT hip OA 2025 grade A (short term, trigger points) vs NICE NG226 (do not offer); a shared decision, adjunct only.
- Elbow: OA is uncommon (about 2%, reported) and often under 60: heavy manual work or an old injury (AIM manual p. 573 lists "elbow (with trauma)"); NICE NG226's "45 or over" fits the elbow poorly. Pain at the end of range rather than mid-range; ulnar nerve symptoms are common alongside. Catching or locking points to a loose body. The general OA principles apply to the arthritis part.
- Under 16 with catching: capitellar OCD until imaged (erf-child).
- Post-traumatic stiffness without a fracture (forms 14 and 17): X-ray first if not done (East Riding Elbow Rule, Arundel 2014; elbow extension test, I6, within 2 weeks). Abducted ulna lesion (manual-therapy positional-fault model, expert opinion, Magee 2014): blocked extension with a springy or fixed end-feel, extension/adduction quadrant most restricted, restricted medial glide of the ulna; ulnohumeral glides or distraction, a lateral-glide thrust only after a fracture is excluded. Non-capsular pattern (ligament adhesions): one direction or combined-movement loss, ligamentous end-feel, stability tests negative; accessory glides toward the restriction, combined movements, deep transverse frictions as indicated. PSFS, AROM.
- Capsular pattern (form 16; Cyriax): flexion lost more than extension, pronation equal to supination, capsular or bony end-feel, passive equal to active; functional arc 30-130 degrees and 50 degrees of rotation (Morrey 1981); crepitus; X-ray if OA is suspected; watch the ulnar nerve with OA; screen for inflammatory arthritis; hard muscle after a blow: myositis ossificans or heterotopic ossification, refer (Gramstad 2006; Nandi 2009). Medication and injection are the doctor's, not patient text.
- Loose bodies / internal derangement (form 18): true locking, effusion, crepitus, pain on end-range overpressure; throwers: posterior or posteromedial impingement, valgus extension overload test (Cain 2003). X-ray can miss fragments; MRI or CT arthrography (Dubberley 2005), via the doctor. Recurrent locking: orthopaedic opinion. No surgical outcome claims in patient text.
- Radiocapitellar injury / OCD (form 19): OCD mainly 11-16 (gymnasts, throwers, racquet sports, weightlifting), Panner's under 10; under 16 is erf-child, imaging first. Adults: cartilage bruise after a fall onto the hand or with lateral laxity; active radiocapitellar compression test, loss of 15-20 degrees of extension, effusion, lateral joint-line tenderness; X-ray (AP in 45 degrees of flexion), MRI for staging; orthopaedic referral for unstable lesions (Baker 2010; Hsu 2012). Myositis ossificans after a blow (form 7): elbow-contusion.
- PRUJ / radial head (form 15): see elbow-tennis.
