## blurb
A knock or a fall can bruise the skin, muscle and bone surface around the elbow. It is sore, may swell and bruise, and is tender to lean on. When the elbow still straightens fully and the bony points are not very sore to press, a break is unlikely. Most bruises settle over 1 to 3 weeks, and gentle movement from early on may help prevent stiffness.

## noticed
- Started straight after a knock or a fall
- Tender, swollen or bruised where it was hit
- Stiff at first, easing day by day
- Sometimes a brief electric feeling down the forearm if the funny bone was hit

## homeCare
- A cold pack wrapped in a cloth for 10 to 15 minutes, a few times a day for the first 2 to 3 days
- Keep bending and straightening the elbow gently from the first day; do not force it
- Avoid leaning on it, and do not massage a deep bruise hard
- Build back to sport and heavy tasks as the soreness settles

## seePhysioIf
- The elbow will not fully straighten, or a bony point is very sore to press: see a doctor the same day for an X-ray
- Tingling or numbness in the fingers stays after the knock: see a doctor the same day
- After 2 to 3 weeks the bruised area is getting harder or lumpier instead of softening, or the elbow is losing movement: see a physiotherapist or your doctor, as an X-ray may be needed
- It is very swollen, or not improving after a week
- It is not getting better after 4 to 6 weeks: see your doctor

- A physiotherapist can check the elbow, including whether an X-ray is needed, and explain what was bruised.
- Gentle movement, then strengthening, can help bring back full movement and strength.
- You get a plan back to sport and work tasks as it settles.

## clinicNotes
- Form 8 (19 Oct 2026): ER2 / East Riding Elbow Rule (Arundel 2014): inability to fully extend, tenderness over the olecranon, radial head or medial epicondyle, or bruising: X-ray. The site screens extension (I6, Appelboam 2008) and olecranon tenderness (I10); radial head and medial epicondyle tenderness are not asked (left with Chandra). A radial head fracture after a fall on the outstretched hand can be occult. Ulnar nerve contusion: persistent ring and little finger tingling, see elbow-cubital. Ice, compression, early range, avoid immobilisation (ACOEM 2013).
- Myositis ossificans (form 7; Walczak 2015): a firm tender intramuscular mass (brachialis most often) 2-4 weeks after a contusion, dislocation or fracture, with progressive loss of extension; warmth early. X-ray shows calcification from about 3-4 weeks, ultrasound earlier. Avoid frictions, aggressive stretching and passive forcing; gentle active range; liaise with the GP; excision only after maturation. Differentials: haematoma, sarcoma (a mass without injury or still growing: medical review), abscess. The form's "It is not cancer" is not used in patient text.
- Look-alikes: olecranon bursitis (elbow-posterior), outer ligament sprain (elbow-lcl), inner ligament (elbow-ucl), fracture (injury screen; elbow-fracture after treatment).
