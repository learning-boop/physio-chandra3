## blurb
Spondylolisthesis means one of the bones in the lower back sits a little forward of the bone below it. In younger adults this usually follows a small stress crack in a part of the bone called the pars, often from years of arching sports. From about 50 onwards it usually comes from gradual change in the discs and small joints, which lets the bone shift. Most slips are small and the spine stays stable: they are often found on scans in people with no pain at all, and a slip does not mean your back is fragile or "out of place". Many people do well with a plan that builds strength and confidence in the back and hips.

## noticed
- A deep ache or band of pain across the low back, often one spot at the base of the spine, sometimes with a sharper catch on arching
- Worse standing or walking for a long time, arching backwards, standing at a sink or counter, walking downhill, or lying on your front
- Easier sitting, bending forward, leaning on a shopping trolley or a kitchen bench, or lying with your knees bent; cycling is often comfortable when walking is not
- Tight hamstrings, and a feeling that the back "needs support"
- With a degenerative slip, legs that feel heavy, tired or tingly after a certain distance and recover once you sit

## homeCare
- Use the positions that unload it: sitting, leaning forward on a counter, or lying with your knees bent, especially during a flare
- Keep moving in ways that suit it. Cycling, the pool and shorter more frequent walks are usually easier than long periods standing
- Build strength gradually through the trunk, hips and legs: this is what gives the segment support, and it is the part that changes the pain over months
- Break up long spells of standing; shift your weight, or rest one foot on a low step
- A flare usually settles over a few weeks with sensible loading. The slip itself rarely moves further in adults

## seePhysioIf
- New difficulty passing or holding urine, loss of bowel control, or numbness around the genitals, back passage or inner thighs: go to the nearest emergency department now
- Leg weakness that is getting worse, a foot that slaps or drags, or new tripping or falls: see a doctor today
- Back pain starting after a fall, a car crash or a heavy knock, especially if you are over 65 or have thin bones: seek urgent medical care, as the bone itself needs checking
- Leg symptoms on walking that keep shortening your walking distance, or leg pain and tingling that is not settling after about 12 weeks of treatment: see your doctor to discuss whether imaging or a specialist opinion would help
- You want a plan: a physiotherapy assessment can work out which positions unload your back, and build the strength programme that suits the slip you have

## clinicNotes
Adult isthmic (younger, arching-sport history) and degenerative (over 50, women about 3:1, most often L4-L5) slips. Diagnosis needs a STANDING lateral X-ray: a slip can reduce when supine. Meyerding grade 1-2 in the great majority; grades 3-4 are rare and belong with the spine surgeon. Examination: palpable step and segmental tenderness, extension-provoked pain, hamstring tightness, hip flexor length, lordosis and sacral angle, full neuro screen L2-S1, SLR, and the claudication history that points to co-existing stenosis. Management: graded loading with trunk and hip strengthening, flexion-biased activity during flares, and education that the segment is stable. Surgery is discussed when radicular or claudication symptoms persist beyond about 12 weeks with imaging that matches (SPORT, Abdu 2018). Co-displays with the stenosis card when walking brings on heavy legs.
