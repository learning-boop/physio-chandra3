## blurb
The triceps tendon attaches to the point of the elbow and works every time you straighten the arm against resistance. A jump in dips, push-ups, bench press, paddling or pushing up from chairs can overload it. It is an overload problem rather than damage, and it usually responds to a graded loading plan.

## noticed
- An ache at the back of the elbow, sharp when you straighten it against resistance
- Tender just above the point of the elbow
- Worse with dips, push-ups, bench press, or pushing up from a chair

## homeCare
- For 2 to 3 weeks, swap dips and deep push-ups for lighter pressing over a shorter range
- Avoid leaning on the point of the elbow
- Warm up the arm before training

## seePhysioIf
- You felt a pop at the back of the elbow after a fall or a forced push, and straightening the arm is now weak: see a doctor the same day
- Pushing or straightening the arm is getting weaker
- The pain has not settled after 2 to 3 weeks
- It is not getting better after 4 to 6 weeks: see your doctor

## physioHelps
<!-- DRAFT (19 Oct 2026), new card: awaiting Chandra's review, like the 169 texts approved 17 Oct 2026 -->
- A physiotherapist can check the tendon at the back of your elbow and explain what is going on.
- A graded loading programme, starting gently and building up, can help the tendon handle pushing again.
- You get a step-by-step plan back to pressing, push-ups and sport.

## clinicNotes
- Palpation at the insertion; resisted extension in inner, mid and outer range (isometric, concentric, eccentric); end-range extension overpressure (posterior impingement). Rule out a rupture: extension against gravity, a palpable gap or a dent above the olecranon, MRI if suspected, early surgical opinion (injury screen I5, same day). Loading: isometric, then heavy slow resistance (Cook and Purdam 2009).
- Differentials: olecranon bursitis (elbow-posterior); posteromedial impingement or valgus extension overload in throwers; an olecranon stress reaction in a young thrower (bone tenderness, persistent pain: medical review; under 16, erf-child); C7 radiculopathy; triceps trigger points. PSFS, DASH (Keener 2015; Hsu 2012).
- Snapping triceps (form 5): the medial head snapping over the medial epicondyle in muscular adults or weight-trainers, often with ulnar nerve subluxation (elbow-cubital); no patient answer separates it, so no card.
