## blurb
The ball of the foot carries a lot of load with every step, especially in high heels, thin-soled shoes, or on hard floors. The joints at the base of the toes and the thick plate under them can become sore, sometimes with a swollen joint on top. Unlike a neuroma, there is no burning or tingling into the toes.

## noticed
- Aching or sharp pain under the ball of the foot
- Worse barefoot, on hard floors, or in high or thin-soled shoes
- Sometimes one toe joint swollen, or a feeling of a pebble underfoot

## homeCare
- Wear cushioned shoes with a roomy toe box; avoid high heels for a while
- A metatarsal pad just behind the sore spot can offload it
- Avoid walking barefoot on hard floors

## seePhysioIf
- A toe joint is suddenly hot, red and very painful, or you feel unwell or feverish: see a doctor the same day
- Several toe joints are swollen in both feet, or in the hands too, with morning stiffness over 30 minutes: see your doctor
- One spot of bone in the front of the foot hurts with every step or hop, or aches at night, after more running, walking or training: see a doctor first (possible stress fracture)
- A teenager with pain and swelling at the base of the 2nd toe: see us or your doctor, as an X-ray may be needed
- You have diabetes and a callus that bleeds, a blister or a sore on the foot: see your doctor or diabetes foot clinic within a day or two
- It has not settled after 2 to 3 weeks of shoe changes
- A toe is starting to drift or lift
- You want help with footwear and insoles

## clinicNotes
- Plantar plate: pain under the 2nd MTP, swelling on top, the toe drifting or lifting; vertical stress (drawer) test, often quoted as sensitivity 80.6% and specificity 99.8% (Klein 2012; secondary sources only). Taping the toe down; offloading.
- Freiberg's (osteochondrosis of the 2nd, sometimes 3rd, metatarsal head): most common in teenage girls; X-ray; offloading first.
- Differentials: Morton's neuroma, metatarsal stress fracture, MTP synovitis or early RA, gout, a long 2nd metatarsal; diabetic callus and ulcer risk.
