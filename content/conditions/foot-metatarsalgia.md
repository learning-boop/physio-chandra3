## blurb
The ball of the foot carries a lot of load with every step, especially in high heels, thin-soled shoes, or on hard floors. The joints at the base of the toes and the thick plate under them can become sore, sometimes with a swollen joint on top. Unlike a neuroma, there is no burning or tingling into the toes.

## noticed
- Aching or sharp pain under the ball of the foot
- Worse barefoot, on hard floors, or in high or thin-soled shoes
- Sometimes one toe joint swollen, or a feeling of a pebble underfoot

## homeCare
- Wear cushioned shoes with a roomy toe box; avoid high heels for a while
- A metatarsal pad just behind the sore spot can offload it
- Avoid walking barefoot on hard floors

## seePhysioIf
- It has not settled after 2 to 3 weeks of shoe changes
- A toe is starting to drift or lift
- You want help with footwear and insoles
