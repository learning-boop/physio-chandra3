## blurb
Sometimes pain that seems to come from the knee is actually being sent there from a stiff or arthritic hip, even though the knee itself is fine.

## noticed
- Knee pain that's hard to point to one exact spot
- Stiffness or reduced movement also noticed in the hip or groin
- Pain that came on gradually, without any clear knee injury

## homeCare
- Keep both the hip and knee moving with gentle daily activity
- Don't focus self-care only on the knee if the hip also feels stiff
- Gentle hip stretching and strengthening may help

## seePhysioIf
- Knee pain isn't improving with knee-focused self-care
- You notice hip or groin stiffness alongside the knee symptoms

## clinicNotes
- Hip OA pain can spread down the thigh to the knee as it progresses (Koc 2025): check the OA criteria (over 50, morning stiffness under 1 hour, IR under 24 degrees or 15 degrees less than the other side, pain on passive IR). In children and teenagers, SUFE and Perthes present at the knee (Enseki 2014). Source: Koc 2025; Enseki 2014.
