## blurb
Irritation where the long band on the outside of the thigh crosses the knee, classically in runners and cyclists, starting at a predictable distance into a session.

## noticed
- Sharp or burning pain on the outside of the knee
- Starts at a predictable point in a run or ride
- Settles with rest at first; later it can ache for days, and walking downstairs can hurt

## homeCare
- Temporarily shorten sessions to below the symptom threshold
- Check for sudden increases in mileage, downhill running, or camber; for cyclists, saddle height and cleat position
- Hip strength work within comfort

## seePhysioIf
- The outer knee swells, catches, or gives way: get it checked, as it may be the cartilage or a ligament instead
- Numbness on the outer shin, or the foot catching when you walk: see a doctor
- It recurs at the same distance despite adjustments
- You'd like a running-load and strength plan
- Pain starts appearing in daily activities too

## physioHelps
<!-- Approved by Chandra Matla, 17 Oct 2026 (review: content/review/physioHelps-review-2026-10-17.docx) -->
- A physiotherapist can check your knee and hip and explain why the outer knee is sore.
- Hip strengthening, with a short break below the point where pain starts, can help settle it.
- You get a gradual plan back to running or cycling, including tips on training, shoes and bike set-up.

## clinicNotes
- Knee document form 4 (Lower Quadrant Clinical Manual pp. 406-407, checked): onset several minutes in, often at exactly the same time, or after activity; worse downhill and on stairs (Fairclough 2007). Renne test (single-leg weight bearing at 30-40 degrees reproduces the pain; Renne 1975, 16 of 1,000 trainees) and Noble compression test (pain at about 30 degrees with pressure 1-2 cm above the lateral condyle; Noble 1980, 220 cases): no sensitivity or specificity data; diagnosis rests on history plus these tests (van der Worp 2012). Contributors: reduced ankle DF, increased hip IR and knee adduction, weak hip ER (Noehren 2014). Exclude LCL, lateral meniscus, STFJ (knee-stfj), biceps femoris, popliteus, CPN and lumbar referral. No CPG; the 2024 conservative treatment review (Sanchez-Alvarado, Front Sports Act Living 2024;6:1386456; checked 26 Oct 2026; 13 studies, 201 runners, 5 RCTs, no meta-analysis, low to moderate certainty): hip abductor strengthening is the core of care, with shockwave or manual therapy as promising add-ons; stretching did not lengthen the band (any benefit likely from the strengthening it was paired with); gait retraining only in case series; injections not studied. Matches the card's hip strength and load advice.
- Diagnosis by history (lateral knee pain at a predictable distance, worse downhill and descending stairs) plus Renne's and Noble's tests, which have no sensitivity or specificity data (AIM manual p.23); Ober's test for tightness. Imaging is largely not indicated. Typical age from the teens to the 50s (p.22).
- Differentials: lateral meniscus, LCL and posterolateral corner, superior tibiofibular joint, biceps femoris or popliteus tendons, patellofemoral pain, peroneal nerve, TFL or gluteus minimus referral, lumbar (L5) referral.
- Management: short-term load reduction below the symptom threshold, then graded return; hip abductor strengthening (consistent benefit, low-quality evidence: Sanchez-Alvarado 2024 systematic review). Stretching or foam rolling the band itself has little evidence. Surgery only after conservative care fails.
- Sources: AIM Theory Manual 2023 Ch. 2.7 pp.22-23; Sanchez-Alvarado A et al., 2024 (PMC11377285).
